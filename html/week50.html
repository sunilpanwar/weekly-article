Week 50 • December

📸 Reflecting – Look at Your Pet’s Photos from January

As the year comes to a close, take a moment to scroll through your phone gallery. Find a photo of your pet from January – then one from today. Notice the changes: maybe a few more grey hairs, a shinier coat, or a wiser expression. You’ve been there for every meal, every walk, every zoomie, every cuddle. Pet parenting is hard – you worry, you clean messes, you lose sleep. But look at that face. They trust you completely. You’re doing a great job.

📸 Common sense truth: Pets don’t measure love in toys or treats – they measure it in time spent together. This year, you gave them your presence. That’s everything.

What changed this year?

  • Did they learn a new trick or break a bad habit?
  • Did they recover from an illness or surgery – with your care?
  • Did they become calmer, more confident, or more playful?
  • Did you welcome a new pet or say goodbye to an old friend?

Every small victory matters. Write them down – they are the story of your shared year.

The health check – what to look for in photos

Comparing photos across months can also reveal health changes you might have missed:

  • Weight changes: Has your pet gained or lost weight noticeably? Compare body shape from above and side.
  • Coat condition: Is the fur duller or patchy? Could indicate thyroid issues or allergies.
  • Eyes: Any cloudiness, redness, or discharge that wasn’t there before?
  • Posture: Does your pet stand or sit differently? Could be joint pain.

If you notice changes, bring the photos to your next vet visit – they help us see what you’ve observed.

🖼️ Dr. Deepa’s suggestion: “Print one photo from each month and make a 2026 collage. Hang it where you see it daily – it’ll remind you why you do this. And bring a copy to your pet’s annual checkup; it helps us track subtle changes.”

  • You survived shedding season (Week 8) and tick season (Week 10).

That’s not nothing – that’s dedication. Your pet is healthier, happier, and safer because of you.

Real story from our clinic

A client brought in her 8‑year‑old Indie, Tara, for a senior checkup. She showed us a photo from January – Tara was noticeably heavier, with a dull coat. The current photo showed a leaner, brighter dog. The owner had followed our weight management plan and added fish oil. “I didn’t realise how much she had changed until I compared the photos,” she said. “I’m so proud of us.” Tara’s blood work was excellent. The owner now makes a photo album every year – “It keeps me accountable and reminds me why I do this.”

Looking ahead to 2027

The new year brings new opportunities to strengthen your bond. Consider:

  • Adding one new enrichment activity (nose work, a new trick).
  • Scheduling those missed preventive visits (dental, senior blood work).
  • Taking more photos – you’ll be grateful for them later.
  • Sharing your journey – post a before‑and‑after photo of your pet this year and tag us. We love celebrating with you.
