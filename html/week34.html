🚶 Street Dog Safety – Walk Calmly, Don’t Run, Don’t Stare

Walking past a stray dog can be intimidating – especially if the dog barks or approaches. But most street dogs are not aggressive; they are territorial or scared. How you react makes all the difference. At Dr. Deepa pet vet clinic, we educate pet parents and communities on safe, calm behaviour around strays – reducing bites and building coexistence.

🚶 Common sense rule: Never run from a stray dog – running triggers their chase instinct. Don’t stare directly (eye contact is a threat). Walk away calmly, sideways, with loose body language.

Why dogs chase or bark at you

  • Territorial instinct: They are protecting their area – not necessarily attacking you.
  • Fear: A scared dog may bark or lunge to make you go away.
  • Past trauma: Many strays have been abused or chased – they are defensive.
  • Chase instinct: Running triggers a natural predator‑prey response, even in friendly dogs.

How to behave when passing a stray dog – step by step

  1. Stay calm. Take a deep breath. Your fear releases pheromones dogs can smell.
  2. Stop walking if the dog is very close. Sudden movements escalate.
  3. Avoid eye contact. Look down or to the side – direct staring is a challenge.
  4. Turn your body sideways. This is less threatening than facing them directly.
  5. Walk slowly and smoothly. No running, no jerky movements.
  6. Keep your hands still. Don’t wave or reach out.
  7. If the dog approaches, stand still like a tree. Most will sniff and move on.
  8. Do not scream or swat. That can trigger an attack.
🌳 Dr. Deepa’s tip: “Teach children the ‘Be a Tree’ method: stand still, arms folded, look down, count slowly to ten. The dog will usually lose interest and leave.”

What to do if a dog charges or snaps at you

  • Do not run. You cannot outrun a dog.
  • Use an object as a barrier: An umbrella, a bag, or even a stick held out can deter without harming.
  • Speak firmly but calmly: “No” or “Go home” in a low voice.
  • Back away slowly. Do not turn your back completely.
  • If you fall, curl into a ball: Protect your face, neck, and ears with your hands.

What if you are walking your own dog?

Stray dogs may approach your leashed dog – this can lead to fights.

  • Keep your dog close on a short leash. No retractable leashes – they reduce control.
  • Put your body between the stray and your dog. You are a barrier.
  • Distract the stray: Toss a few treats away from you (if safe) to redirect.
  • Cross the street or change direction before the stray gets too close.
  • Do not pick up your small dog – this can trigger the stray to jump up.

Real case from our clinic

A 10‑year‑old boy, Rohan, was walking home from school when a street dog started barking. He panicked and ran. The dog chased and bit his calf. He needed rabies post‑exposure shots and stitches. At our clinic, we taught Rohan the “Be a Tree” technique. A month later, he met the same dog – this time he stood still, looked down, and counted to ten. The dog sniffed his shoes and walked away. Rohan told us, “I was scared, but I remembered. It worked.”

Teaching children street dog safety

  • Never approach an unfamiliar dog – even if it looks friendly.
  • Never pet a dog that is sleeping, eating, or tied up.
  • If a dog runs toward you, stand still – do not scream or run.
  • If knocked over, curl into a ball with hands over ears.
  • Always ask an adult before petting any dog.

What NOT to do

  • Do not run – ever.
  • Do not stare directly into a dog’s eyes.
  • Do not wave your arms or shout.
  • Do not try to pet a stray dog that is not familiar with you.
  • Do not separate two fighting dogs with your hands – use water, a loud noise, or a blanket.

Community coexistence – how to reduce stray dog conflicts

  • Support Animal Birth Control (ABC) programmes – sterilised dogs are less territorial and aggressive.
  • Feed strays at a fixed time and location – they become predictable.
  • Never chase or hit strays – it creates lifelong fear and aggression.
  • If a dog is consistently aggressive, contact a local animal welfare group – do not take matters into your own hands.

If you are bitten – medical steps

  1. Wash the wound thoroughly with soap and water for 15 minutes.
  2. Apply an antiseptic (Betadine).
  3. Go to a doctor immediately – you may need rabies post‑exposure prophylaxis and a tetanus shot.
  4. Report the bite to local animal control – they will monitor the dog if possible.

Action for Week 34

  1. Practice the “Be a Tree” stance at home – stand still, fold arms, look down, count to ten.
  2. Teach every family member, especially children, never to run from a dog.
  3. If you walk your dog in areas with strays, carry an umbrella or a walking stick as a gentle barrier.
  4. Report aggressive strays to a local rescue – they can assess and relocate if necessary.
