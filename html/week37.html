💙 Helping Strays – Put Out a Bowl of Clean Water

One simple act can save a life: putting out a bowl of clean water for stray animals. In India’s heat, humidity, and even winter dryness, strays struggle to find safe drinking water. At Dr. Deepa pet vet clinic, we see dehydrated, weak strays brought in by kind neighbours – many of whom started with just a bowl of water outside their home.

💙 Common sense rule: A heavy, stable bowl of fresh water placed in a shaded spot can prevent heatstroke, kidney failure, and death in stray animals. Refill twice daily. It costs nothing but time.

Why stray animals need your water bowl

  • No reliable source: Strays drink from puddles (full of germs), drains (chemicals), or go without.
  • High dehydration risk: In summer, a dog can die from heatstroke within hours without water.
  • Even in winter: Dry air and less available water (puddles dry up) still cause dehydration.
  • Lactating mothers: Need extra water to produce milk for puppies – lack of water means starving puppies.

How to set up a stray water bowl – step by step

  1. Choose a safe location: Shaded area, away from traffic, not near your own pet’s food (to avoid conflict).
  2. Use a heavy, tip‑proof bowl: Metal or ceramic bowls are best – plastic bowls tip over easily. A wide, shallow bowl is harder to spill.
  3. Fill with clean, fresh tap water. Change twice daily (morning and evening).
  4. Scrub the bowl daily – biofilm grows fast in warm weather.
  5. In winter, use lukewarm water – it won’t freeze as quickly and is gentler on cold bodies.
  6. Place a stone or brick inside – prevents small animals (birds, squirrels) from drowning.
🪣 Dr. Deepa’s tip: “If you can’t put a bowl on the ground (apartment balcony), place a shallow tray on the railing or a bowl inside your gate. Every drop counts.”

What NOT to do

  • Do not use a bowl that can tip over easily (light plastic).
  • Do not add salt, sugar, or anything to the water – strays need plain water.
  • Do not place the bowl near your own pet’s territory – it can cause fights.
  • Do not leave a bowl out if you see it’s been contaminated (dead insects, faeces) – clean it immediately.

Beyond water – other ways to help strays

  • Leave dry food nearby – but not too close to the water (wet food spoils quickly).
  • Provide shelter – an old cardboard box lined with newspapers in a dry corner can save a life in rain or cold.
  • Notify a local rescue if a stray is injured, sick, or has puppies.
  • Support Animal Birth Control (ABC) programmes – sterilised strays are healthier and less aggressive.

Real case from our clinic

A family in Mumbai put out a water bowl for the first time last summer. Within a week, three neighbourhood strays were drinking regularly. One older dog, whom they named “Brownie”, would wait by the bowl every morning. When Brownie became lethargic and stopped drinking, the family brought him to our clinic. He had a fever from an infected wound. We treated him, and he recovered. The family adopted him. Today, Brownie sleeps on their balcony. His owner says, “We only put out water. We never planned to adopt. But he chose us.”

Community impact – small acts add up

If every house on a street put out one water bowl, that street would have no dehydrated strays. Dehydration leads to weakness, which leads to accidents (hit by cars), fighting (over food), and death. Your bowl breaks that cycle.

What about feeding strays – is it safe?

Feeding strays is kind but must be done responsibly:

  • Feed at a fixed time and place – so strays don’t linger all day and cause conflicts.
  • Use dry food or cooked rice/vegetables – not leftovers with bones, spices, or onion/garlic (toxic).
  • Clean up uneaten food after 30 minutes – to avoid attracting rodents or insects.
  • Do not feed if it will disturb neighbours or create territorial fights.

Involving children – a lesson in compassion

Teaching children to refill a water bowl for strays builds empathy and responsibility. It’s a small, safe way to connect with animals without the commitment of adoption. Supervise young children and teach them not to approach unfamiliar dogs.

Action for Week 37

  1. Today, place a heavy bowl of clean water outside your home – gate, garden, balcony, or pavement.
  2. Set a daily reminder to refill it twice a day (morning and evening).
  3. If you see a stray who looks weak or sick, contact a local rescue – but start with water.
  4. Share a photo of your water bowl on social media with #WaterForStrays – inspire others.
