💉 Rabies Facts – The Once‑a‑Year Injection That Protects Everyone

Rabies is one of the oldest and deadliest diseases known to humans. It is 100% fatal once symptoms appear – but it is also 100% preventable with a simple, safe, once‑a‑year injection. At Dr. Deepa pet vet clinic, we cannot stress this enough: vaccinating your pet against rabies protects not only them, but your entire family and community.

💉 Common sense rule: Rabies vaccination is not optional – it’s the law in India. It’s also one of the most cost‑effective ways to save lives. A single bite from an infected animal can transmit rabies to your pet, and then to you.

What is rabies?

Rabies is a viral disease that attacks the central nervous system. It is transmitted through the saliva of infected animals – usually via a bite, but also through scratches or contact with mucous membranes. Once symptoms appear (aggression, drooling, paralysis, fear of water), death occurs within days. There is no cure.

Why your pet needs the rabies vaccine – even if they never go outside

  • Bats, stray cats, or rodents can enter your home – a single bite from an infected animal can infect your pet.
  • You can bring rabies home – if a stray animal bites you, you could carry the virus on your clothes or skin.
  • Legal requirement: In India, rabies vaccination is mandatory for all dogs and cats. You can be fined for non‑compliance.
  • Community protection: High vaccination rates create “herd immunity”, reducing the risk for all animals and humans.
⚖️ Dr. Deepa’s warning: “I’ve seen families devastated by rabies – a child bitten by a neighbourhood dog, a pet infected by a bat. Every single case was preventable. The vaccine costs less than a meal out. There is no excuse.”

Rabies vaccine schedule

  • Puppies and kittens: First dose at 12–16 weeks of age. A booster is given one year later.
  • Adult pets: A booster every 1–3 years depending on the vaccine type (ask us which your pet needs).
  • After a potential exposure (bite from an unknown animal): Even a vaccinated pet should receive a booster immediately and be quarantined as per local laws.

Keep your pet’s rabies certificate in a safe place – you’ll need it for boarding, travel, and legal compliance.

What to do if your pet is bitten by a stray or wild animal

  1. Wear gloves – rabies can enter through broken skin.
  2. Clean the wound thoroughly with soap and water for 15 minutes – this alone reduces risk significantly.
  3. Bring your pet to the clinic immediately – even if previously vaccinated, we will administer a booster and follow local rabies protocols.
  4. Report the bite to local animal control – they may need to monitor or test the biting animal.

Human risk – what if your pet bites someone?

If your vaccinated pet bites a person (even playfully), you must:

  • Provide the victim with your pet’s rabies vaccination certificate.
  • Confine your pet for observation (usually 10 days) as per local law.
  • Consult a doctor for the bite victim – they may need post‑exposure prophylaxis depending on the situation.

An unvaccinated pet that bites a human may be euthanised for rabies testing – a tragic outcome that is entirely preventable.

Real case from our clinic

A family brought in their 2‑year‑old Indie, Kalu, who had been bitten by a stray dog on a walk. Kalu had never been vaccinated for rabies because “he never goes out alone”. The stray dog was later found dead. Kalu was at high risk. We immediately administered a rabies booster (even though it was his first dose, it can still help) and advised a 45‑day quarantine at home. Kalu remained healthy, but the family spent weeks in fear, watching for any sign of the disease. The owner told us, “I will never skip a rabies vaccine again. The anxiety was unbearable.” Kalu is now vaccinated annually, and his owner spreads the message to every pet parent she meets.

Myths about the rabies vaccine (debunked)

  • “The vaccine causes rabies.” False. Modern rabies vaccines are inactivated (killed) – they cannot cause rabies.
  • “My pet only stays indoors – no risk.” False. Bats, rodents, or even a stray cat entering your home can bite your pet.
  • “One vaccine lasts a lifetime.” False. Immunity wanes. Boosters are required every 1–3 years.
  • “The vaccine is expensive.” False. Government and municipal clinics often offer low‑cost rabies drives. Even at private clinics, the cost is minimal compared to the consequences.

How to get your pet vaccinated

  • Visit our clinic – we stock safe, effective rabies vaccines and provide a certificate.
  • Attend a local rabies drive – many municipalities and animal welfare organisations offer free or low‑cost camps.
  • Ask us about home visits – for multiple pets or anxious animals.

After vaccination, keep your pet calm for 24 hours. Mild lethargy or a small lump at the injection site is normal. If you see signs of allergic reaction (facial swelling, hives, difficulty breathing), bring them back immediately – this is rare but serious.
