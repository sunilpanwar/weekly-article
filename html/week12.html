🐶 Finding a Puppy – What to Do When You Find a Stray

You find a tiny stray puppy – alone, shivering, maybe crying. Your first instinct is to help, and that’s wonderful. But knowing the right steps can mean the difference between life and death. At Dr. Deepa pet vet clinic, we guide many Good Samaritans through this process every year.

🐶 Common sense rule: First, warmth. Second, hydration (water only, never milk). Third, a vet visit. Never give cow’s milk – it causes deadly diarrhoea in puppies.

Step 1: Assess the situation

  • Is the mother nearby? Watch from a distance for 30 minutes. The mother may be looking for food. If she returns, leave the puppy with her – she is the best caregiver.
  • Is the puppy injured or in danger? If on a busy road, in extreme heat, or attacked by other animals, intervene immediately.
  • How old does the puppy look? Eyes closed = under 2 weeks old (needs urgent care). Eyes open and moving = 3+ weeks old.

Step 2: Provide immediate warmth

Puppies cannot regulate their body temperature until 3–4 weeks old. Hypothermia kills quickly.

  • Wrap the puppy in a soft, dry towel or cloth.
  • Place a hot water bottle (wrapped in cloth) nearby – not directly against the puppy.
  • Keep the puppy in a cardboard box lined with towels, away from drafts.
🌡️ Dr. Deepa’s tip: “A puppy that feels cold to the touch is in danger. Warm them gradually – never use a hairdryer or direct heat. Skin‑to‑skin contact (holding against your chest) works well.”

Step 3: Offer fluids – but NEVER milk

Many people offer cow’s milk, but it’s a serious mistake. Puppies lack the enzyme to digest lactose, leading to severe diarrhoea, dehydration, and death.

  • Safe option: Lukewarm water – offer a few drops from a syringe (no needle) or your finger.
  • Better option: Puppy milk replacer (available at veterinary clinics and pet stores).
  • Emergency homemade recipe (only if no replacer available): 1 cup water + 1 cup boiled, cooled rice water + 1 egg yolk (no white) – but this is temporary. Get proper replacer as soon as possible.

Step 4: Call a vet or visit the clinic

A stray puppy may have worms, fleas, or infections. Do not wait. Bring the puppy to Dr. Deepa pet vet clinic for:

  • A health assessment (temperature, hydration, signs of illness).
  • Deworming (most puppies have worms).
  • Flea/tick treatment (safe for young puppies).
  • Feeding advice (how much, how often, what formula).

What NOT to do

  • Do not bathe a young puppy – they can get hypothermia. Use a damp cloth for cleaning only if absolutely necessary.
  • Do not feed human baby formula – it lacks the right nutrients and may contain harmful ingredients.
  • Do not give honey or sugar water – this can cause bacterial infection in the gut.
  • Do not assume the puppy is abandoned – the mother may be nearby looking for food.

Real case from our clinic

Last monsoon, a family brought in a 10‑day‑old puppy they found shivering in a drain. They had given him cow’s milk and wrapped him in a wet towel. The puppy arrived with diarrhoea and a dangerously low body temperature. We warmed him gradually, started IV fluids, and fed him puppy milk replacer every two hours. After three days in the clinic, he was healthy. The family adopted him and named him “Lucky”. Today, Lucky is a healthy 2‑year‑old Indie. His owners now tell everyone: “Never give milk to a puppy.”

Long‑term care – what happens next?

If you decide to keep the puppy, you’ll need:

  • Vaccination schedule (starting at 6–8 weeks).
  • Regular deworming (every 2 weeks until 12 weeks old).
  • Proper puppy food (not adult food).
  • Socialisation and training.

If you cannot keep the puppy, contact local rescue groups, animal shelters, or ask us – we can guide you to reliable adoption networks.

Action for Week 12

  1. If you ever find a stray puppy, remember: warmth first, then water (no milk), then vet.
  2. Keep a small box, old towels, and a hot water bottle in your car or home – you never know when you might need them.
  3. Save our clinic’s location details (visit our Google page) for emergency situations.
  4. Share this information with neighbours and friends – many people still don’t know that milk is dangerous for puppies.
