🌫️ Smog & Dust – Keep Walks Short When Air Is Smoky

After Diwali, and throughout winter, many Indian cities are blanketed in thick smog and dust. The air quality index (AQI) often reaches hazardous levels – and our pets breathe it too. At Dr. Deepa pet vet clinic, we see a surge in respiratory issues every November: coughing, wheezing, red eyes, and lethargy. The simplest prevention? Keep walks short, walk early, and watch for signs of breathing distress.

🌫️ Common sense rule: If the air looks smoky or smells of pollution, keep walks under 10 minutes. Walk before sunrise when pollution is lowest. Pets get lung damage from smog just like humans – sometimes worse because they are closer to the ground.

How smog affects pets

  • Respiratory irritation: Coughing, sneezing, wheezing, and difficulty breathing.
  • Eye inflammation: Red, watery, or itchy eyes – dust and particulate matter are irritants.
  • Aggravation of existing conditions: Pets with asthma, bronchitis, or heart disease are at high risk.
  • Long‑term damage: Chronic exposure can lead to lung fibrosis and reduced lung function.

Brachycephalic breeds (Pugs, Bulldogs, Shih Tzus, Persians) are most vulnerable because their airways are already compromised.

Signs your pet is affected by smog

  • Coughing or gagging, especially after walks.
  • Rapid, shallow breathing or open‑mouth breathing (cats).
  • Lethargy or reluctance to walk – they may tire quickly.
  • Red, runny eyes or excessive blinking.
  • Nasal discharge or sneezing.
  • Wheezing or whistling sounds when breathing.

If you notice these signs, move your pet indoors immediately and bring them to us if they persist.

🫁 Dr. Deepa’s warning: “I’ve seen dogs collapse after a 20‑minute walk in hazardous AQI. Their lungs are smaller and more sensitive than ours. If you feel it’s hard to breathe, your pet is struggling twice as much.”

Safe walking guidelines during smog season

  • Check the AQI before walking. Use apps like SAFAR, AirVisual, or AQI.in. If AQI > 150 (unhealthy for sensitive groups), keep walks under 10 minutes. If > 300 (hazardous), skip outdoor walks entirely.
  • Walk early morning (before 6 AM). Pollution levels are lowest just before sunrise. Avoid evening walks – smog settles as temperatures drop.
  • Choose less polluted routes: Parks with trees (trap some particulates) are better than roadside pavements. Avoid busy roads.
  • Keep walks short – 10–15 minutes max. Two very short walks are better than one long walk.
  • After walking, wipe your pet’s paws, face, and body with a damp cloth to remove settled particulate matter.

Indoor air quality – what you can do at home

  • Use an air purifier in the room where your pet spends most time – especially in the bedroom where they sleep.
  • Keep windows closed during high‑smog hours (late evening to early morning).
  • Do not smoke indoors – cigarette smoke adds to the burden.
  • Use a damp mop on floors – dry sweeping kicks up dust. Wet cleaning traps it.

Real case from our clinic

A 5‑year‑old Pug, Golu, was brought in with a hacking cough and blue‑tinged tongue after a 20‑minute walk in heavy smog. He was struggling to breathe. We gave him oxygen, bronchodilators, and anti‑inflammatories. Golu improved over two days, but his owner was terrified. “I didn’t think a short walk could hurt him,” she said. Now she checks the AQI daily and walks Golu only before 6 AM, for 5 minutes. She also bought an air purifier for the bedroom. Golu’s cough has not returned.

Indoor exercise alternatives on high‑smog days

  • Treat hide‑and‑seek – mental stimulation tires them without lung strain.
  • Training drills – practice sit, down, stay, spin, touch.
  • Puzzle toys – commercial or DIY (muffin tin with balls).
  • Tug‑of‑war – in a hallway.
  • Flirt pole – a toy on a rope for chase games indoors.

When to seek veterinary help

  • Persistent coughing that doesn’t stop within an hour of coming indoors.
  • Blue or grey gums or tongue – sign of oxygen deprivation.
  • Extreme lethargy or collapse.
  • Open‑mouth breathing in cats (cats are obligate nasal breathers – open mouth indicates distress).

For mild symptoms, keep your pet calm, use a humidifier or steamy bathroom (run a hot shower) to soothe airways, and call us for advice.

Action for Week 41

  1. Download an AQI app today. Check it before every walk.
  2. If AQI is high, reduce walk time by half – or skip outdoors entirely.
  3. Wipe your pet’s face, paws, and body with a damp cloth after any outdoor exposure.
  4. If your pet coughs or seems tired after walks, bring them in for a respiratory check.
