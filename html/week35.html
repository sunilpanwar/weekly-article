Week 35

🩸 Tick Fever – Sudden Laziness and Not Eating? Check for Fever

After monsoon, ticks explode again – and with them, tick fever (Ehrlichiosis and Babesiosis). One of the earliest signs is subtle: your normally active dog becomes lazy, loses appetite, and just wants to sleep. At Dr. Deepa pet vet clinic, we see tick fever cases spike in September and October. The good news: if caught early, it is treatable. If ignored, it can be fatal.

🩸 Common sense rule: If your dog is suddenly lethargic, not eating, and has a fever (above 102.5°F / 39.2°C), think tick fever – even if you haven’t seen a tick. Bring them to the clinic immediately.

What is tick fever?

Tick fever is a general term for diseases transmitted by ticks. The two most common in India are:

  • Ehrlichiosis: Caused by Ehrlichia bacteria. Attacks white blood cells and platelets. Can cause bleeding disorders, nosebleeds, and swollen limbs.
  • Babesiosis: Caused by Babesia parasites. Destroys red blood cells, leading to anaemia, dark urine, and weakness.

Both are transmitted by the Brown Dog Tick (Rhipicephalus sanguineus), which is active year‑round but peaks after rains.

Signs of tick fever – what to watch for

  • Early signs (days 1–7): Fever, lethargy, loss of appetite, weight loss, enlarged lymph nodes.
  • Moderate signs (week 2–4): Pale gums (anaemia), nosebleeds, bruising on skin or gums, swollen joints, limping.
  • Severe signs (chronic): Kidney failure, eye inflammation (uveitis), neurological signs (seizures, wobbling), bleeding from multiple sites.

Some dogs progress slowly over weeks. Others crash within days. Early treatment is critical.

🌡️ Dr. Deepa’s warning: “A dog with tick fever may look fine in the morning and be collapsed by evening. If your dog is ‘off’ – not eating, sleeping more than usual – take their temperature or bring them in. Don’t wait for nosebleeds.”

How to check your dog’s temperature at home

  1. Use a digital rectal thermometer (labelled for pets).
  2. Lubricate with petroleum jelly or coconut oil.
  3. Gently insert 1–2 cm into the rectum (for small dogs) or 2–3 cm (large dogs).
  4. Wait for the beep (about 60 seconds).
  5. Normal temperature: 100.5°F – 102.5°F (38°C – 39.2°C).
  6. Above 102.5°F (39.2°C) is fever – above 104°F (40°C) is emergency.

If you’re uncomfortable doing this, bring your dog to us – we’ll check.

Diagnosis – what we do at the clinic

  • Blood test: We can run a rapid in‑house test for Ehrlichiosis and Babesiosis (results in 10 minutes).
  • Complete blood count (CBC): Looks for low platelets (Ehrlichiosis) or low red blood cells (Babesiosis).
  • Blood smear: We may see the Babesia parasites inside red blood cells under a microscope.

Early diagnosis saves lives. Do not delay.

Treatment for tick fever

  • Antibiotics (for Ehrlichiosis): Doxycycline for 4 weeks minimum. Symptoms improve within 48–72 hours, but finish the full course.
  • Antiprotozoal drugs (for Babesiosis): Imidocarb or similar – usually one or two injections.
  • Supportive care: IV fluids for dehydrated dogs, blood transfusions for severe anaemia, anti‑inflammatories for fever.

Even with treatment, some dogs become chronic carriers (especially Babesiosis) and may relapse during stress. Tick prevention remains essential.

Real case from our clinic

A 5‑year‑old Labrador, Simba, was brought in because he hadn’t eaten in two days and was sleeping all day. The owner thought he was “just tired”. His temperature was 104°F. A rapid test was positive for Ehrlichiosis. Simba’s platelet count was dangerously low – he was at risk of internal bleeding. We started doxycycline and IV fluids. Within 48 hours, Simba ate again. He completed 4 weeks of antibiotics and made a full recovery. His owner now does daily tick checks and uses a tick preventive year‑round. “I almost lost him because I thought he was just lazy,” she said.

Prevention – tick control is the only way

  • Use a vet‑approved tick preventive: Spot‑ons, oral tablets, or collars. Ask us which is right for your pet.
  • Daily tick checks: Run your hands over your dog’s body – feel for small bumps. Check ears, toes, armpits, groin.
  • Keep your yard clean: Mow grass, remove leaf litter, and treat with pet‑safe tick repellents if needed.
  • No vaccine for Babesiosis or Ehrlichiosis – prevention relies entirely on tick control.

Can cats get tick fever?

Cats are less susceptible, but they can get a form of Babesiosis. Signs include lethargy, pale gums, and fever. If you have an outdoor cat in a tick‑prone area, use a cat‑safe tick preventive (never use dog products on cats – they are toxic).

Zoonotic risk – can humans get tick fever?

Ehrlichiosis and Babesiosis are not directly contagious from pets to humans. However, the same ticks that bite your dog can bite you. Protect your pet, and you protect your family.

Action for Week 35

  1. If your dog seems lazier than usual or refuses food, take their temperature or bring them in.
  2. Check your dog’s gums – are they pale, yellow, or have tiny red spots (petechiae)? That’s an emergency.
  3. Ask us about year‑round tick prevention – it’s cheaper than treating tick fever.
  4. Mark your calendar for a tick check every evening – just 2 minutes can save a life.
