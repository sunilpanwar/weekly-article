🔥 The Paw Test – Is the Road Too Hot for Your Dog?

Summer roads turn into hot plates. Asphalt, concrete, and even metal footpath surfaces can reach temperatures high enough to burn your dog’s paw pads within seconds. At Dr. Deepa pet vet clinic, we treat burned paws every summer – almost all of which are preventable with a simple test.

🐾 Common sense rule: Press the back of your palm on the road for 5 seconds. If you can’t hold it, it will burn your dog’s paws.

The science – how hot does the road get?

On a 30°C (86°F) day, asphalt can reach 57°C (135°F). On a 35°C day, it can exceed 65°C (150°F). At these temperatures, paw pads burn in under 60 seconds. Dogs don’t wear shoes, and their paw pads are sensitive – not rubber.

  • 25°C air temperature → road ~52°C → safe for short walks only.
  • 30°C air temperature → road ~57°C → burns possible within 2 minutes.
  • 35°C+ air temperature → road ~65°C+ → burns within 30 seconds.

How to do the 5‑second paw test

  1. Choose the surface your dog will walk on (road, pavement, or concrete).
  2. Press the back of your hand or palm firmly on the surface.
  3. Hold for 5 seconds. Count slowly: “one thousand one, one thousand two…”
  4. If it’s too hot for your hand, it’s too hot for your dog’s paws.

Remember: paw pads are more sensitive than your palm. The 5‑second test is a guideline – if it feels even slightly uncomfortable, postpone the walk.

🌡️ Dr. Deepa’s tip: “The paw test works for any surface – sand at the beach, metal car ramps, even artificial grass in direct sunlight. When in doubt, test it yourself.”

Signs of burned paw pads

  • Limping or refusing to walk.
  • Licking or chewing paws excessively.
  • Pads that look red, blistered, or darker than usual.
  • Missing pieces of pad tissue.
  • Whining or acting anxious during walks.

If you see these signs, stop the walk immediately and check the paws. Minor burns can be treated at home, but severe burns need veterinary care.

What to do if your dog’s paws are burned

  • Cool the paws: Run cool (not ice‑cold) water over them for 5–10 minutes.
  • Clean gently: Use a mild antiseptic like Betadine diluted with water (1:10).
  • Prevent licking: Use an Elizabethan collar (cone) or wrap paws loosely with gauze.
  • Visit the clinic: Severe burns may need antibiotics, pain relief, or bandaging.

Safe walking times in summer

  • Best time: Before 7:00 AM – roads are cool from the night.
  • Second best: After 7:00 PM – roads have cooled down somewhat, but still test.
  • Avoid: 10:00 AM to 5:00 PM – peak heat hours.
  • Never walk: On very hot days, even early morning might be risky if the night was warm. Always test.

Alternatives to pavement walking

  • Walk on grass or mud paths – these stay much cooler.
  • Use dog booties – but get your dog used to them before summer.
  • Play indoors – mental games, fetch in a hallway, or training sessions.
  • Visit a pet‑friendly indoor space (some malls or pet stores allow dogs).

Real case from our clinic

Last May, a 4‑year‑old Labrador, Simba, was brought in with severe paw burns. His owner had taken him for a 20‑minute walk at 2 PM. The road temperature was over 60°C. Simba’s paw pads were blistered and peeling. He needed pain medication, antibiotic cream, and daily bandage changes for two weeks. His owner said, “I never thought to check the road. I just assumed it was fine because I was wearing shoes.” Now she does the paw test before every walk – and walks only early morning.

What about cooling paw sprays or waxes?

Some products claim to protect paws from heat. While they may help with minor warmth, they are not a substitute for avoiding hot surfaces. No wax or spray can protect against 60°C asphalt. Prevention is always better than treatment.

Action for Week 13

  1. Today, go outside at 2 PM (or the hottest time) and do the 5‑second paw test. You’ll be shocked how hot the road is.
  2. Adjust your walking schedule – move walks to early morning or late evening.
  3. Keep a spray bottle with cool water by the door to cool paws after walks (even if not burned).
  4. If you see any signs of burned paws, bring your dog to the clinic immediately.
