🏡 Adoption Tips – Why Adopting a Stray Is Smarter and Healthier

Every year, millions of healthy Indie puppies are born on Indian streets. They struggle for food, water, and safety – yet they are often overlooked in favour of expensive foreign breeds. At Dr. Deepa pet vet clinic, we believe adoption is not just an act of compassion – it’s a smart, practical choice for any pet parent.

🏡 Common sense truth: Adopted strays are smarter, healthier, and more resilient than many purebreds. They cost less to adopt, have fewer genetic diseases, and adapt easily to Indian homes.

Why adoption beats buying

  • Health advantages: Indie dogs have been naturally selected for thousands of years – no inbreeding, no hereditary diseases like hip dysplasia, heart problems, or breathing issues common in purebreds.
  • Lower cost: Adoption fees (if any) are a fraction of what breeders charge. You also save on initial vaccinations, deworming, and spay/neuter – many rescues include these.
  • Already temperament‑tested: Foster‑based rescues can tell you if the dog is good with children, cats, or other dogs.
  • You save a life: Adopting opens space for another stray to be rescued. Buying supports commercial breeding (often cruel).
  • Climate‑ready: Indie dogs handle Indian heat, humidity, and common diseases better than imported breeds.

Common fears about adoption – and the reality

  • “Strays are aggressive.” Reality: Any dog can be aggressive if mistreated or unsocialised. Indie dogs raised in loving homes are gentle, loyal, and affectionate.
  • “They carry diseases.” Reality: Rescues are fully vetted – vaccinated, dewormed, and treated for fleas/ticks before adoption. They are as healthy as any pet.
  • “They are not good with children.” Reality: Many Indie dogs are excellent with children – they are patient, protective, and playful. Proper introduction and supervision are key.
  • “I can’t train a stray.” Reality: Indies are highly intelligent and trainable. They excel in obedience, agility, and even therapy work.
🧠 Dr. Deepa’s observation: “In my practice, Indie dogs are the fastest learners. They are problem‑solvers, watchful, and eager to please. With positive reinforcement, they learn commands faster than many purebreds.”

Where to adopt – trusted sources

  • Local animal shelters: Most cities have municipal or private shelters with adoptable dogs.
  • Rescue groups: Organisations like Sanjay Gandhi Animal Care Centre (Delhi), CUPA (Bangalore), WVS (Mumbai), and many smaller local rescues.
  • Foster‑based networks: Dogs live in foster homes, so you get a clear idea of their personality.
  • Social media adoption drives: Many rescues post adoptable pets on Facebook, Instagram, and WhatsApp groups.

Step‑by‑step adoption guide

  1. Research: Decide on age (puppy vs adult), energy level, and size that fits your home.
  2. Contact a rescue: Fill out an application. Be honest about your lifestyle – they will match you with a suitable dog.
  3. Meet the dog: Spend time with them. If possible, do a “sleepover” or trial adoption for a few days.
  4. Home check: Most rescues will visit your home to ensure it’s safe (enclosed balcony, no hazards).
  5. Complete paperwork: Sign an adoption agreement – you commit to providing vet care, good food, and a forever home.
  6. Bring your new family member home: Prepare a bed, bowls, leash, collar, and a quiet space for adjustment.

First week at home – settling in

  • Keep the environment calm – no big parties or loud noises.
  • Introduce one room at a time. Use baby gates if needed.
  • Maintain the same food and schedule the rescue used, then transition slowly.
  • Take them to a vet within the first week for a health check (we can do this).
  • Be patient – some dogs take weeks to fully trust. Never punish fear.

Real case from our clinic

A family came to us with a 4‑month‑old Indie puppy they had adopted from a shelter. The mother was nervous – “I’ve never had a dog before. I’m scared he’ll bite.” We examined the puppy – healthy, playful, a little shy. We gave them a behaviour guide and scheduled a follow‑up. Six months later, the puppy (now named “Tara”) is a confident, well‑behaved companion. The mother told us, “She is the best decision we ever made. She wakes my son up for school, sleeps on my feet, and barks only when someone knocks. I can’t imagine buying a dog now.”

What about adult dogs?

Adult strays are often overlooked, but they make wonderful pets:

  • Already house‑trained (if fostered).
  • Calmer than puppies – no teething, less destructive.
  • Personality is known – no surprises.
  • They are grateful and bond deeply.

Consider adopting an adult Indie – you’ll save a life and skip the exhausting puppy phase.

Cost comparison – adoption vs buying

  • Adoption fee: ₹0 – ₹5,000 (often includes first vaccines, deworming, spay/neuter).
  • Buying from a breeder or pet shop: ₹15,000 – ₹1,00,000+ (no health guarantees, often supports cruelty).
  • Initial vet costs for adopted pet: ₹2,000 – ₹5,000 (remaining vaccines, checkup).
  • Initial vet costs for bought puppy: Same – but you also pay for the purchase price.

  4. Volunteer or donate to a local rescue – even if you can’t adopt, you can help.
