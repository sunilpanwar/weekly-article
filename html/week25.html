Week 25

🐾 Wet Paws – Dry Between Toes After Every Walk

Monsoon means wet walks – and wet paws. But what many pet parents don’t realise is that moisture trapped between toes creates the perfect environment for fungal and bacterial infections. At Dr. Deepa pet vet clinic, we treat “wet paw dermatitis” (interdigital pyoderma) throughout the rainy season – almost all of which could be prevented with a simple 30‑second drying routine.

🐾 Common sense rule: After every walk in rain or damp grass, dry each toe gap individually. A soft towel or a hair dryer on cool setting works best. Moisture left between toes = fungal paradise.

Why wet paws are dangerous

The spaces between a pet’s toes are dark, warm, and poorly ventilated. When they stay damp for hours, yeast (Malassezia) and bacteria multiply rapidly. What starts as mild irritation can become red, itchy, smelly, and painful – sometimes within a day.

  • Fungal infections: Yeast causes a corn‑chip smell, brown discharge, and intense itching.
  • Bacterial infections: Redness, swelling, pus, and pain (your pet may limp or lick constantly).
  • Interdigital cysts: Painful, fluid‑filled lumps between toes from chronic irritation.
  • Nail bed infections: Moisture weakens the nail attachment, leading to pain and even nail loss.

Signs your pet has a paw infection

  • Constant licking or chewing at paws.
  • Red, swollen skin between toes.
  • Brown, yellow, or black discharge.
  • Foul, yeasty, or corn‑chip smell from paws.
  • Limping or reluctance to walk.
  • Paw pads that are soft, white, or macerated (like wrinkled skin after a long bath).
👃 Dr. Deepa’s tip: “If your dog’s paws smell like corn chips or stale bread, that’s yeast. Don’t wait for licking – start drying more thoroughly and consider a vet visit.”

How to dry paws properly – step by step

  1. Keep a dedicated “paw towel” by the door. Use a soft, absorbent cloth – microfiber works well.
  2. After each walk, wipe the entire paw to remove mud and excess moisture.
  3. Lift the paw and gently separate each toe. Dry the skin between them individually – this is the most important step.
  4. For long‑haired breeds, check for mats between toes. Trim fur flush with the pad using blunt scissors (carefully).
  5. Use a hair dryer on cool setting for dogs who tolerate it – especially after swimming or heavy rain.
  6. Inspect for cuts, thorns, or ticks while you’re there – a great opportunity for a quick health check.

This entire routine takes less than 60 seconds. It’s the best investment you can make against paw infections.

What NOT to do

  • Do not let your pet air‑dry after a wet walk. Damp fur between toes stays wet for hours.
  • Avoid using alcohol or hydrogen peroxide to “dry” paws – they irritate and crack skin.
  • Do not use human antifungal creams without vet guidance – some are toxic if licked.
  • Don’t ignore early signs – a little redness today can become a painful infection tomorrow.

Real case from our clinic

A 4‑year‑old Labrador, Simba, came in with red, swollen paws and a strong yeasty smell. He was licking constantly and limping. The owner said, “I wipe his paws after walks, but I don’t dry between the toes.” We examined Simba and found a severe yeast infection between all four paws. He needed medicated antifungal soaks, oral medication, and a cone to stop licking. After two weeks of treatment, the infection cleared. The owner now uses a hairdryer on cool setting after every rainy walk – “I learned my lesson. Thirty seconds of drying saves weeks of vet visits.”

Prevention – keeping paws healthy in monsoon

  • Trim paw fur regularly. Long fur between toes traps moisture. Ask us to show you how, or we can do it during a visit.
  • Avoid walking in puddles or flooded areas – they contain bacteria and chemicals.
  • Consider paw wax or protective balms – they create a water‑repellent barrier (apply before walks).
  • Use a bootie or rain shoe for dogs prone to infections – but ensure they are dried inside after use.
  • Provide a dry, clean resting area – your pet will groom damp paws on a wet bed.

Breeds prone to paw infections

  • Labrador Retrievers (webbed toes trap moisture).
  • Golden Retrievers.
  • Cocker Spaniels (hair between toes).
  • Indies with hairy paws.
  • Any dog with allergies (allergic skin produces more moisture and yeast).

When to visit the clinic

  • Redness, swelling, or discharge that doesn’t improve with drying.
  • Your pet is limping or refuses to walk.
  • Constant licking despite your best efforts (may need a cone and medication).
  • A foul smell that doesn’t go away after cleaning.

Action for Week 25

  1. After your next walk, check your pet’s paws. Are the toe gaps wet? If yes, start drying them immediately.
  2. Keep a dedicated paw towel by the door – use it after every walk this week.
  3. If you have a long‑haired breed, trim the fur between toes (or ask us to do it).
  4. Smell your pet’s paws tonight – if you detect a corn‑chip or yeasty odour, schedule a check.
