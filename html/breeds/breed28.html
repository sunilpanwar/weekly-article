Week 28 • Cat Breed

🐱 Himalayan Cat – Persian in a Colour‑Point Coat, with Same Health Needs

The Himalayan cat is a cross between a Persian and a Siamese. They have the long, fluffy coat and flat face of a Persian, but the blue eyes and colour‑point pattern of a Siamese (darker ears, face, paws, and tail). They are calm, sweet, and love to be petted. At Dr. Deepa pet vet clinic, we see Himalayans that are gentle, quiet, and good for apartments. But they have the same health problems as Persians – eye tearing, breathing trouble, and polycystic kidney disease (PKD). They also need daily grooming. If you want a Himalayan, you must be ready for high maintenance.

🐾 Key things to remember: Himalayans need daily brushing to prevent mats. Their flat face causes eye tearing – clean eyes daily. They overheat easily – keep in air conditioning in summer. They are prone to kidney disease (PKD) – ask for screening.

What does a Himalayan cat look like?

  • Size: Medium – 3–6 kg. Stocky, heavy‑boned.
  • Coat: Long, thick, and fluffy – like a Persian. Needs daily brushing.
  • Colours: Colour‑point – lighter body with darker ears, face, paws, and tail. Seal, blue, chocolate, lilac, flame (red), or tortie points.
  • Eyes: Deep, vivid blue – round and large.
  • Build: Flat face (brachycephalic), small nose, round head, short legs.

Why Himalayans are popular in India

  • Beautiful appearance: They look like a fluffy, blue‑eyed teddy bear.
  • Calm and quiet: They are not noisy or destructive – good for apartments.
  • Affectionate: They love to sit near you and be petted.
👁️ Dr. Deepa’s warning: “Himalayans have the same flat face as Persians. Their eyes tear constantly, leaving brown stains. You must clean their eyes every day with a soft, damp cloth. Their flat face also makes them prone to breathing problems and heatstroke – never leave them in a hot room.”

Common health problems in Himalayans

  • Polycystic kidney disease (PKD): A genetic disease that causes kidney failure. Responsible breeders screen for this. Adopted Himalayans should be tested.
  • Brachycephalic airway syndrome: Flat face causes snoring, wheezing, and heat intolerance. Keep them cool.
  • Eye tearing (epiphora): Tears overflow and stain the fur. Clean daily.
  • Dental disease: Crowded teeth. Brush weekly.
  • Obesity: They love to eat – measure portions.

Grooming – high maintenance

  • Brush daily: Use a steel comb and slicker brush. Their long coat mats easily – pay attention to behind ears, armpits, and belly.
  • Bathe once a month: Use a cat‑safe shampoo. Dry thoroughly – damp fur mats faster.
  • Eye cleaning – daily: Wipe under eyes with a soft, damp cloth to remove tears and prevent staining.
  • Ear cleaning – weekly: Use a cotton ball – never Q‑tips.
  • Trim nails every 2–3 weeks. Brush teeth weekly.

Exercise and environment

  • Play: 10–15 minutes once or twice a day – feather wands, soft toys.
  • Indoor only: Never let them outside – they cannot defend themselves and may overheat.
  • Heat sensitivity: Keep them in air conditioning or cool rooms in summer.

Feeding guidelines

  • High‑quality cat food – wet food is good for kidney health.
  • For an adult Himalayan, 1/4 to 1/3 cup of dry food per day (divided into two meals) plus wet food.
  • Do not overfeed – obesity worsens breathing problems.
  • Always fresh, clean water – use a wide, shallow bowl.

Real story from our clinic

A Himalayan named “Snowy” came in with brown tear stains and crusty eyes. Her owner had never cleaned them. We showed her how to wipe daily with a damp cloth. Within two weeks, the staining faded. The owner also started brushing Snowy daily – her coat had started matting. “I didn’t know Himalayans need so much care,” she said. “Now we have a daily grooming routine.”

Should you get a Himalayan cat?

Yes, if you:

  • Have time for daily brushing and eye cleaning.
  • Can keep your home cool (air conditioning in summer).
  • Are prepared for potential PKD screening and vet costs.
  • Want a calm, quiet, beautiful indoor cat.

Maybe not, if you:

  • Cannot commit to daily grooming – their coat will mat painfully.
  • Live in a very hot, humid city without AC – they overheat easily.
  • Want a low‑maintenance cat (get an Indie cat).

Action for Week 28

  1. If you have a Himalayan, check their eyes today – any discharge or staining? Start daily cleaning.
  2. Brush their coat – feel for mats. If you find mats, gently brush them out or have a groomer shave them.
  3. Schedule a PKD screening (ultrasound or DNA test) if not already done.
  4. If you are thinking of getting a Himalayan, adopt from a rescue or ask the breeder for PKD and heart screening results.
