🐕 Pug – Cute, Wrinkly, but Struggles to Breathe in Heat

The Pug is a small, wrinkled, flat‑faced dog with a big personality. They are funny, loving, and love to be the centre of attention. At Dr. Deepa pet vet clinic, we see many Pugs because they are very popular in India. But they also have serious health problems because of their flat face (brachycephalic). They struggle to breathe, especially in summer. They overheat very easily. Their skin folds can get infected if not cleaned daily. If you want a Pug, you must be ready to keep them cool, clean their wrinkles every day, and never over‑exercise them.

🐾 Key things to remember: Pugs cannot tolerate heat – they need air conditioning in summer. Clean their face wrinkles daily with a damp cloth to prevent infections. They snore, wheeze, and may have trouble sleeping – this is normal for the breed but can worsen with obesity.

What does a Pug look like?

  • Size: Small – 6–9 kg. Height 25–30 cm.
  • Coat: Short, smooth, and shiny – low grooming, but sheds a lot.
  • Colours: Fawn (tan) or black, with a black mask on the face.
  • Build: Compact, muscular, with a wrinkled face, curled tail, and big round eyes.

Why Pugs are popular in India

  • Funny personality: They are playful, silly, and love to make you laugh.
  • Affectionate: They are lap dogs – they love to cuddle and follow you around.
  • Low exercise needs: Short walks are enough – good for apartment living.
  • Good with children: They are patient and rarely aggressive.
🌡️ Dr. Deepa’s warning: “Pugs overheat very quickly. Never walk them in the sun. Even a few minutes in a hot car can kill them. Always have air conditioning or a cool room available.”

Common health problems in Pugs

  • Brachycephalic airway syndrome: Flat face means narrow nostrils, long soft palate, and small windpipe. They snort, snore, wheeze, and may collapse in heat. Surgery can help severe cases.
  • Heatstroke: Very high risk. Signs: heavy panting, dark red tongue, weakness. Emergency! Cool them with wet towels and bring to vet.
  • Skin fold infections (pyoderma): Moisture and dirt trap in their face wrinkles. Clean daily with a damp cloth and dry thoroughly.
  • Eye problems: Protruding eyes are prone to injury (scratch from a cat or bush). Also dry eye, corneal ulcers.
  • Obesity: Pugs love to eat and become fat easily – extra weight makes breathing even harder.
  • Hip dysplasia and patellar luxation: Joint problems common in small breeds.

Grooming – easy but important

  • Brush once a week with a rubber curry brush – they shed a lot, so daily brushing is better during shedding season.
  • Bathe once a month – dry thoroughly, especially in wrinkles.
  • Face wrinkle cleaning – every day: Use a soft, damp cloth to wipe inside each wrinkle. Then dry with a dry cloth. Never leave moisture in wrinkles.
  • Clean ears weekly – their ears are small but can get dirty.
  • Trim nails every 3–4 weeks. Brush teeth weekly.

Exercise – gentle and cool

  • Walks: Only early morning (before 7 AM) or late evening (after 7 PM). Keep walks to 10–15 minutes in summer. Watch for panting – stop if they struggle.
  • Indoor play: Short games of fetch, puzzle toys, or training sessions – they don’t need intense exercise.
  • Avoid heat: Never walk when the sun is up. Use the paw test (Week 13) – if road is hot, stay inside.
  • Cooling mats or air conditioning: Essential in Indian summers.

Training and behaviour

  • Pugs are smart but stubborn. Training takes patience – use positive reinforcement (treats, praise).
  • They can be difficult to house‑train – consistency is key.
  • They are sensitive to your tone – do not shout, they will become scared.
  • They love attention and may develop separation anxiety if left alone for long hours.

Feeding guidelines

  • High‑quality small breed dog food – they need controlled calories to prevent obesity.
  • For an adult Pug, 1/2 to 3/4 cup of dry food per day (divided into two meals).
  • Do not overfeed – every extra gram makes breathing harder. Measure every meal.
  • Limit treats – use small pieces of apple or carrot.
  • Always fresh, cool water – add ice cubes in summer.

Real story from our clinic

A 3‑year‑old Pug named “Golu” was brought in after collapsing during a 10‑minute morning walk at 8 AM. The owner thought morning was cool enough. Golu’s tongue was dark red, and he was panting heavily. We cooled him with wet towels and gave oxygen. He recovered but had permanent damage to his airways. The owner now walks Golu only at 6 AM and uses air conditioning all day. “I almost lost him because I didn’t understand how sensitive they are,” he said.

Should you get a Pug?

Yes, if you:

  • Have air conditioning in summer – non‑negotiable.
  • Can commit to daily face wrinkle cleaning.
  • Live in a cool climate or are willing to walk only before sunrise.
  • Want a small, affectionate, funny companion.

Maybe not, if you:

  • Live in a very hot city without AC.
  • Cannot clean wrinkles daily – infections will happen.
  • Are not prepared for potential expensive breathing surgeries.
  • Want an active, outdoorsy dog – Pugs are not built for that.

Action for Week 13

  1. If you have a Pug, check their face wrinkles today – are they red, smelly, or oozing? Clean them.
  2. Feel their nose – are their nostrils tiny? If they struggle to breathe even at rest, discuss surgery options with us.
  3. Check your walking schedule – are you walking them after sunrise? Switch to 6 AM.
  4. If you are thinking of getting a Pug, adopt from a rescue – many need homes due to health issues.
