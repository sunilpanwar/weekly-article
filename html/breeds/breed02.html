Week 2 • Cat Breed

🐈 Indie Cat (Indian Billi) – The Hardy, Low‑Maintenance Native Cat

The Indie cat, also called the Indian Billi or Desi cat, is India’s native cat. You see them everywhere – on streets, in homes, in fields. But they are not just stray cats. They are a landrace that has evolved over thousands of years to survive in India’s heat, humidity, and disease environment. At Dr. Deepa pet vet clinic, we see that Indie cats are some of the healthiest, smartest, and most adaptable pets you can have.

🐾 Why Indie cats are special: They have strong immunity, rarely fall sick, eat almost anything, and are excellent at keeping rats and pests away. They are also very loving once they trust you.

What does an Indie cat look like?

  • Size: Small to medium – usually 3–5 kg.
  • Coat: Short, smooth, and shiny. Comes in many colours – brown, black, white, orange, grey, or mixed (calico, tortoiseshell).
  • Body: Lean and athletic – not fat or heavy.
  • Ears: Pointed, always alert.
  • Eyes: Usually green, yellow, or amber.

Why Indie cats are perfect for Indian homes

  • Heat tolerant: They handle Indian summers easily. No need for air conditioning.
  • Strong immunity: They rarely get cat flu, skin infections, or stomach problems.
  • Low grooming: A weekly brush is enough. They clean themselves very well.
  • Natural pest control: They will keep your home free of rats, mice, and even snakes.
  • Easy to feed: They do well on simple food – rice, fish, chicken, or good quality cat food.
🏡 Dr. Deepa’s advice: “If you want a cat that won’t need expensive vet visits, won’t develop breathing problems, and can handle Indian weather without fuss – adopt an Indie cat. They are the most resilient cats you will find.”

Common health problems – very few!

Indie cats are generally very healthy. But they can still have:

  • Worms: Deworm every 3 months (more often if they hunt rats).
  • Injuries from fights: If they go outside, they may get into cat fights. Keep them indoors if possible.
  • Minor skin infections: Rare, but treatable with simple medication.

With good food, clean water, yearly vet checkups, and a safe home, an Indie cat can easily live 15–20 years.

Grooming – very simple

  • Brush once a week with a soft brush – removes loose hair and reduces hairballs.
  • Bathe only when very dirty – most Indie cats never need a bath. They clean themselves.
  • Clean ears weekly with a cotton ball (no Q‑tips inside).
  • Trim nails every 3–4 weeks if they don’t wear them down naturally.

Exercise and play

Indie cats are active but not hyper. They enjoy:

  • Chasing a feather wand or laser pointer (but end with a physical toy so they “catch” something).
  • Climbing – give them a cat tree or a high shelf.
  • Exploring cardboard boxes – they love hiding and jumping out.

If you keep them indoors, 15–20 minutes of play twice a day is enough.

Training and behaviour

  • Indie cats are very smart. They can learn to use a litter box quickly.
  • They are independent – they will come to you for love, but they also enjoy alone time.
  • They are excellent hunters. If you have a rat problem, an Indie cat will solve it.
  • They bond deeply with one or two people. They are not usually “lap cats” unless raised that way from kittenhood.

Real story from our clinic

A family found a tiny grey Indie kitten under their car during the monsoon. They brought her to us. She had a cold and was very thin. We treated her, vaccinated her, and advised the family on care. They named her “Grey”. Now, two years later, Grey is healthy, playful, and the queen of the house. She sleeps on the sofa and keeps rats away from the kitchen. The family told us, “We never thought a stray kitten could become such a wonderful pet. She is better than any expensive breed.”

Should you adopt an Indie cat?

Yes, if you want:

  • A low‑maintenance, healthy cat.
  • A cat that can handle Indian weather without AC.
  • Natural pest control for your home.
  • To save a life – millions of Indie kittens are born on the streets every year.

Maybe not, if you:

  • Want a cat that stays indoors 100% and never tries to escape (Indies love exploring).
  • Want a very cuddly, always‑on‑your‑lap cat (they are more independent).
  • Have expensive birds or rodents as pets (they may hunt them).
