Week 34 • Cat Breed

🐈 Russian Blue – Shy, Elegant, but Prone to Dental Issues and Obesity

The Russian Blue is a beautiful, elegant cat with a short, plush blue‑grey coat and bright green eyes. They are known for being quiet, gentle, and a little shy with strangers. At Dr. Deepa pet vet clinic, we see Russian Blues that are very loyal to their family but may hide when guests come. They are smart and playful but not demanding. However, they have some health problems. They are prone to dental disease and can easily become overweight. They also have a risk of urinary stones. They are good for quiet homes but need regular dental care and portion control.

🐾 Key things to remember: Russian Blues are shy – give them time to warm up. They are prone to dental disease – brush teeth weekly. They love to eat – measure portions to prevent obesity. Their short coat is easy to groom – brush once a week.

What does a Russian Blue look like?

  • Size: Medium – 3–6 kg. Sleek and muscular.
  • Coat: Short, dense, plush – feels like velvet. The colour is blue‑grey with silver tips.
  • Eyes: Bright green – almond‑shaped. Kittens have yellow eyes that turn green by adulthood.
  • Build: Long, slender body, wedge‑shaped head, large pointed ears, and a long tail.

Why Russian Blues are popular in India

  • Beautiful appearance: Their blue‑grey coat and green eyes are very striking.
  • Quiet and gentle: They are not noisy or destructive – good for apartments.
  • Low grooming: Short coat needs only weekly brushing.
  • Hypoallergenic (lower allergens): They produce less Fel d 1 protein than many cats – better for some allergy sufferers.
🦷 Dr. Deepa’s warning: “Russian Blues are prone to dental disease – tartar builds up quickly, leading to red gums and tooth loss. Brush their teeth weekly and get yearly dental cleanings. They also love to eat – do not free‑feed; measure portions to prevent obesity.”

Common health problems in Russian Blues

  • Dental disease (gingivitis, periodontitis): Very common. Signs: bad breath, red gums, difficulty eating. Brush weekly and get professional cleanings.
  • Obesity: They will overeat if allowed – measure every meal.
  • Bladder stones (struvite or calcium oxalate): Can cause bloody urine, straining. Feed wet food to increase water intake.
  • Eye problems: Progressive retinal atrophy (PRA) – rare, but possible. Yearly eye checks recommended.

Grooming – easy

  • Brush once a week with a soft rubber brush – removes loose hair and spreads skin oils.
  • They shed moderately – brush more often in spring and autumn.
  • Bathe rarely – they are very clean.
  • Clean ears weekly with a cotton ball – never Q‑tips.
  • Trim nails every 2–3 weeks.
  • Brush teeth weekly – very important.

Exercise and environment

  • Play: 10–15 minutes once or twice a day – feather wands, puzzle toys, fetch.
  • Climbing: They like to be up high – provide a cat tree or shelves.
  • Quiet home: They do best in calm, predictable environments – not good for loud, chaotic homes.
  • Indoor only: Never let them outside – they are shy and may not find their way home.

Feeding guidelines

  • High‑quality cat food – wet food is good for urinary health.
  • For an adult Russian Blue, 1/4 to 1/3 cup of dry food per day (divided into two meals) plus wet food. Adjust based on activity.
  • Do not free‑feed – they will become obese.
  • Always fresh, clean water – consider a water fountain to encourage drinking.

Real story from our clinic

A 4‑year‑old Russian Blue named “Blue” came in for bad breath. His gums were red and swollen, and he had heavy tartar. He needed a professional dental cleaning. His owner had never brushed his teeth. After the cleaning, the owner started weekly brushing. “I didn’t know this breed has dental problems,” she said. “Now we brush every Sunday.”

Should you get a Russian Blue?

Yes, if you:

  • Have a quiet, calm home – they are shy and do not like chaos.
  • Can commit to weekly tooth brushing and yearly dental checks.
  • Can measure portions – they will overeat if allowed.
  • Want a gentle, elegant, low‑grooming cat.

Maybe not, if you:

  • Have a very loud, busy home with many strangers – they will hide constantly.
  • Cannot commit to dental care – they will develop serious gum disease.
  • Want a very playful, outgoing cat – Russian Blues are more reserved.

Action for Week 34

  1. If you have a Russian Blue, check their teeth – any bad breath or red gums? Schedule a dental check.
  2. Start a weekly tooth‑brushing routine – use cat‑safe toothpaste.
  3. Weigh your cat – feel their ribs. If you can’t feel them easily, reduce food by 10%.
  4. If you are thinking of getting a Russian Blue, adopt from a rescue or ask the breeder about dental and eye health in the parents.
