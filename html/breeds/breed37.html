🐕 Indian Spitz – Fluffy, Loyal, and Well‑Suited for Indian Homes

The Indian Spitz is a small to medium dog with a thick white coat, pointed ears, and a curly tail. They look like a smaller version of the German Spitz. They are one of the most popular native breeds in India – often found as family pets in apartments and houses. At Dr. Deepa pet vet clinic, we see Indian Spitz dogs that are loyal, intelligent, and very alert. They make excellent watchdogs because they bark when strangers approach. However, they shed a lot, can be barky, and need regular grooming. They are generally healthy and adapt well to Indian weather.

🐾 Key things to remember: Indian Spitz shed heavily – brush 2–3 times a week. They bark a lot – train a “quiet” command early. They are loyal and bond strongly with one family. They are generally healthy and do not need air conditioning.

What does an Indian Spitz look like?

  • Size: Small to medium – 5–12 kg. Height 30–40 cm.
  • Coat: Thick, double coat – soft undercoat and longer outer coat. Mostly white, but also cream or brown.
  • Build: Compact, fox‑like face, pointed ears, and a curled tail over the back.

Why Indian Spitz are popular in India

  • Adapted to Indian climate: They handle heat and humidity better than many foreign breeds.
  • Loyal and protective: They bond strongly with family and make good watchdogs.
  • Intelligent and trainable: They learn quickly, though they can be stubborn.
  • Low maintenance health: Fewer genetic problems than purebred foreign dogs.
🧹 Dr. Deepa’s tip: “Indian Spitz shed heavily, especially in spring and autumn. Brush them 2–3 times a week to control loose hair. A daily quick brush is even better. And train them not to bark at every noise – they can become nuisance barkers.”

Common health problems in Indian Spitz

  • Obesity: They love to eat – measure portions.
  • Dental disease: Small mouth = crowded teeth. Brush weekly.
  • Patellar luxation: Loose kneecaps – can cause limping. Keep them lean.
  • Eye problems: Progressive retinal atrophy (PRA) and cataracts – rare but possible.
  • Skin allergies: Some develop itchy skin – use vet‑approved shampoos.

Grooming – moderate to high

  • Brush 2–3 times a week: Use a slicker brush and undercoat rake. During heavy shedding seasons (spring, autumn), brush daily.
  • Bathe once a month or when dirty – use a mild dog shampoo. Dry thoroughly.
  • Clean ears weekly – their erect ears are less prone to infection but still need cleaning.
  • Trim nails every 3–4 weeks. Brush teeth weekly.

Exercise and environment

  • Walks: 20–30 minutes twice a day. They can handle Indian heat but avoid peak sun – use the paw test (Week 13).
  • Play: Fetch, puzzle toys, training sessions – they are active but not hyper.
  • Secure yard: They can dig or squeeze through small gaps – ensure fencing is secure.

Training and behaviour

  • Indian Spitz are intelligent but can be stubborn. Use positive reinforcement (treats, praise).
  • They bark a lot – train a “quiet” command from puppyhood.
  • They bond strongly with one family and may be wary of strangers – socialise early.
  • They can be possessive of food and toys – teach “drop it” and “leave it”.

Feeding guidelines

  • High‑quality dog food – they do well on Indian home food (rice, chicken, vegetables) but avoid spices, onion, garlic.
  • For an adult Indian Spitz, 1/2 to 1 cup of dry food per day (divided into two meals). Adjust based on activity.
  • Keep them lean – obesity is common.
  • Always fresh, clean water.

Real story from our clinic

A family adopted an Indian Spitz puppy named “Snowy”. He was very vocal – barking at every passerby. The family was frustrated. We suggested a “quiet” command training – rewarding him when he stopped barking. Within a month, Snowy learned to bark only once and then be quiet. The owner said, “I didn’t know you could train them not to bark. Now he is the perfect watch dog.”

Should you get an Indian Spitz?

Yes, if you:

  • Have time for regular brushing (2–3 times a week) to manage shedding.
  • Are okay with a dog that barks – or are willing to train a “quiet” command.
  • Want a loyal, intelligent, adaptable dog for an Indian home.
  • Prefer a native breed with fewer genetic problems.

Maybe not, if you:

  • Cannot handle shedding – they shed a lot.
  • Live in a noise‑sensitive apartment – they bark at strangers.
  • Want a dog that loves all strangers – Indian Spitz are one‑family dogs.

Action for Week 37

  1. If you have an Indian Spitz, brush their coat today – remove loose undercoat.
  2. Check their weight – feel their ribs. If you can’t feel them easily, reduce food.
  3. Start a “quiet” command training – reward calm behaviour.
  4. If you are thinking of getting an Indian Spitz, adopt from a rescue – many are looking for homes.
