🐕 Yorkshire Terrier – Tiny, Bold, but Prone to Dental Disease and Fragile Trachea

The Yorkshire Terrier (Yorkie) is a tiny, elegant dog with a long, silky coat. They are bold, confident, and full of personality – they often forget how small they are! At Dr. Deepa pet vet clinic, we see Yorkies that are loving companions and great watchdogs. However, they have several health problems because of their small size. They have very bad teeth – they lose them early if not brushed daily. Their windpipe is fragile (tracheal collapse) – use a harness, never a neck collar. They also have a risk of low blood sugar (hypoglycemia) as puppies. They need daily grooming – their coat mats easily.

🐾 Key things to remember: Yorkies have terrible teeth – brush daily. Use a harness, never a neck collar – protects their fragile windpipe. They can have low blood sugar as puppies – feed small meals frequently. Their long coat mats – brush daily or keep trimmed short.

What does a Yorkshire Terrier look like?

  • Size: Toy – 2–3.5 kg. Height 15–20 cm.
  • Coat: Long, silky, fine – blue and tan (dark steel‑blue from head to tail, tan on head, chest, and legs).
  • Build: Small, compact, with a small head, V‑shaped ears, and a tail that is often docked.

Why Yorkies are popular – and have special needs

  • Big personality in a small body: They are brave, curious, and very loyal.
  • Good for apartments: Their small size fits any home.
  • Low exercise needs: Short walks and indoor play are enough.
  • Very high grooming needs: Their long coat mats easily – needs daily brushing or a short trim.
🦷 Dr. Deepa’s warning: “Yorkshire Terriers have very bad teeth. By age 3, many have tartar, red gums, and loose teeth. Brush their teeth DAILY with dog‑safe toothpaste. Yearly professional cleaning is also needed. Without care, they will lose teeth and develop painful gum infections.”

Common health problems in Yorkshire Terriers

  • Dental disease (periodontitis): Very common. Brush daily and get yearly dental cleanings.
  • Tracheal collapse (collapsing windpipe): The windpipe flattens, causing a honking cough, especially when excited or pulling on a collar. Use a harness, never a neck collar.
  • Hypoglycemia (low blood sugar): Common in puppies and tiny adults. Signs: weakness, lethargy, shivering, seizures. Rub honey or glucose gel on gums and feed a meal.
  • Patellar luxation (loose kneecaps): Keep them lean.
  • Liver shunt (portosystemic shunt): A birth defect – signs: stunted growth, seizures, lethargy after eating. Requires surgery. Responsible breeders screen for this.

Grooming – high maintenance

  • Brush daily: Use a soft slicker brush and steel comb. Their silky coat mats very easily – pay attention to behind ears, armpits, and belly. Many owners keep the coat trimmed short (“puppy cut”) for easier care.
  • Bathe every 2–3 weeks: Use a good dog shampoo and conditioner. Dry thoroughly – damp fur mats faster.
  • Eye cleaning – daily: Wipe under eyes with a soft, damp cloth to prevent staining.
  • Ear cleaning – weekly: Use a cotton ball – never Q‑tips.
  • Trim nails every 2–3 weeks. Brush teeth daily – very important.

Exercise and environment

  • Walks: 15–20 minutes twice a day. Use a harness – never a neck collar.
  • Indoor play: Short play with soft toys, puzzle feeders – they don’t need intense exercise.
  • Fragile: They are tiny – can be injured by rough play or falling from heights. Supervise around children.
  • Cold sensitive: Use a sweater in winter – they have little body fat.

Training and behaviour

  • Yorkies are smart but can be stubborn. Use positive reinforcement (treats, praise).
  • House‑training can be difficult – be patient and consistent.
  • They bark a lot – train a “quiet” command early.
  • They can develop small dog syndrome (aggression, demanding behaviour) if not trained properly – set rules early.

Feeding guidelines

  • High‑quality small breed dog food – small kibble size.
  • For an adult Yorkie, 1/4 to 1/2 cup of dry food per day (divided into two meals). Adjust based on activity.
  • Puppies need 3–4 small meals daily to prevent hypoglycemia.
  • Keep them lean – obesity worsens joint and windpipe problems.
  • Always fresh, clean water.

Real story from our clinic

A Yorkshire Terrier named “Teddy” came in with a honking cough. His owner used a neck collar for walks. X‑rays showed tracheal collapse. We switched him to a harness, and the cough reduced significantly. His owner now uses a harness and says, “I had no idea neck collars are dangerous for tiny dogs.”

Should you get a Yorkshire Terrier?

Yes, if you:

  • Can commit to daily tooth brushing and regular grooming.
  • Use a harness instead of a collar – never a neck collar.
  • Are prepared for potential dental costs and tracheal issues.
  • Want a tiny, bold, affectionate companion.

Maybe not, if you:

  • Cannot brush teeth daily – they will lose teeth.
  • Have young children who may handle the dog roughly – Yorkies are fragile.
  • Want a low‑grooming, quiet dog – they need daily brushing and bark.

Action for Week 52

  1. If you have a Yorkie, check their teeth – any bad breath or red gums? Schedule a dental check.
  2. Switch to a harness if you use a neck collar – protects their windpipe.
  3. Start a daily tooth‑brushing routine – use dog‑safe toothpaste.
  4. If you are thinking of getting a Yorkie, adopt from a rescue or ask the breeder about liver shunt and knee screening.
