Week 11 • Dog Breed

🐕 Beagle – Energetic, Vocal, and Prone to Ear Infections

The Beagle is a small to medium hound dog. They are famous for their excellent nose – they were bred to hunt rabbits by following scents. Beagles are happy, curious, and full of energy. They love to explore, bark, and follow their nose. At Dr. Deepa pet vet clinic, we see Beagles that are wonderful family pets, but they also have some common problems. Their long, floppy ears get infected easily. They love to eat and can become obese. They also howl or bark loudly – which may not be good for apartment living.

🐾 Key things to remember: Beagles have floppy ears – clean them weekly to prevent infections. They are scent‑driven – always keep them on a leash or in a secure yard. They will follow their nose and get lost.

What does a Beagle look like?

  • Size: Small to medium – 9–11 kg (smaller variety) or 11–14 kg (larger variety). Height 33–41 cm.
  • Coat: Short, dense, and weather‑resistant – easy to groom.
  • Colours: Tricolour (black, tan, white) or lemon and white.
  • Build: Sturdy, muscular, with a sweet expression and long, floppy ears.

Why Beagles are popular in India

  • Friendly: They love people, children, and other dogs – rarely aggressive.
  • Playful: They stay playful even as adults – good for active families.
  • Size: Medium size fits many homes – not too big, not too small.
  • Low grooming: Short coat needs only weekly brushing.
👂 Dr. Deepa’s warning: “Beagles have floppy ears that trap moisture and wax. If you don’t clean them weekly, they will get smelly, itchy ear infections. Also, they are very vocal – they may howl when left alone. Not ideal for noise‑sensitive apartments.”

Common health problems in Beagles

  • Ear infections (otitis externa): Very common. Clean ears weekly with a vet‑approved ear cleaner. Never use Q‑tips.
  • Obesity: Beagles love food and will eat anything. Measure every meal and limit treats.
  • Epilepsy (seizures): Some Beagles have seizures. Medication can control them – bring to us if you see seizures.
  • Eye problems: Glaucoma, cherry eye, and progressive retinal atrophy (PRA). Regular eye checks are good.
  • Hypothyroidism: Low thyroid hormone causes weight gain, hair loss, and lethargy. Blood test can diagnose.

Grooming – easy

  • Brush once a week with a rubber curry brush or soft bristle brush – removes loose hair.
  • Bathe once a month or when dirty – they are active and may get muddy.
  • Ear cleaning – very important: Use a cotton ball and vet‑approved cleaner once a week. Gently wipe the outer ear – never go inside.
  • Trim nails every 3–4 weeks. Brush teeth weekly.

Exercise and mental needs

Beagles need daily exercise – they are energetic dogs bred to hunt.

  • Walks: 30–45 minutes twice a day. Always on a leash – they will follow a scent and run off.
  • Scent games: Hide treats around the house or garden – let them use their nose. This tires them mentally.
  • Secure fencing: Beagles can dig under fences or climb small fences. Your yard must be secure.
  • Barking/howling: They are vocal. If left alone for long hours, they may howl – can disturb neighbours.

Training and behaviour

  • Beagles are smart but stubborn. Training takes patience – use positive reinforcement (treats, praise).
  • They are food‑motivated – good for training, but also means they will steal food from tables or bins.
  • Start socialisation early – they are friendly but can be shy if not exposed to new things.
  • Never let them off‑leash in an unsecured area – their nose takes over and they will ignore your calls.

Feeding guidelines

  • High‑quality dog food – Beagles need good nutrition but watch calories.
  • For an adult Beagle, 1–1.5 cups of dry food per day (divided into two meals). Adjust based on activity and weight.
  • Do not leave food out – they will overeat. Measure every meal.
  • Limit treats – use small pieces of carrot or cucumber instead of biscuits.
  • Always fresh, clean water.

Real story from our clinic

A Beagle named “Charlie” came in shaking his head and scratching his ears. His ears were red, smelly, and full of brown wax – a bad yeast infection. His owner had never cleaned his ears. We cleaned them thoroughly and gave ear drops. We also showed the owner how to clean ears weekly with a cotton ball and ear cleaner. Charlie’s infection cleared up. The owner said, “I didn’t know Beagles need ear cleaning. Now we do it every Sunday.”

Should you get a Beagle?

Yes, if you:

  • Have a secure yard – they are escape artists.
  • Can handle barking/howling – not for noise‑sensitive apartments.
  • Can commit to weekly ear cleaning and daily exercise.
  • Want a friendly, playful, family dog.

Maybe not, if you:

  • Live in an apartment with thin walls – neighbours may complain about howling.
  • Cannot keep them on a leash or in a secure yard – they will run away chasing scents.
  • Are not willing to clean ears weekly – infections will keep coming back.
  • Want a quiet, low‑energy dog (Beagles are active and vocal).

Action for Week 11

  1. If you have a Beagle, check their ears today – smell them, look for redness or brown discharge. Clean if needed.
  2. Check your fencing – can your Beagle dig under or climb over? Reinforce weak spots.
  3. Start a scent game – hide treats around the house and let them search. Great mental exercise.
  4. If your Beagle is overweight, reduce food by 10% and replace treats with vegetables.
