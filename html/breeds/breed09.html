Week 9 • Dog Breed

🐕 Golden Retriever – Friendly, Loving, but Prone to Cancer and Ear Issues

The Golden Retriever is one of the most loved family dogs in the world – and in India. They are gentle, patient with children, and always happy to see you. Their golden coat and friendly smile make them hard to resist. But at Dr. Deepa pet vet clinic, we also see that Golden Retrievers have some serious health problems. They are more likely to get cancer than many other breeds. They also have floppy ears that get infected easily, and they shed a lot. With good care, regular vet checks, and a healthy lifestyle, a Golden Retriever can be a wonderful companion for 10–12 years.

🐾 Key things to remember: Golden Retrievers are prone to cancer – yearly vet checks are very important. Clean their ears weekly to prevent infections. They shed heavily – brush daily. They love to eat – measure every meal to prevent obesity.

What does a Golden Retriever look like?

  • Size: Large – males 30–34 kg, females 25–30 kg. Height 50–60 cm.
  • Coat: Dense, water‑repellent double coat. Sheds heavily year‑round, more in spring and autumn.
  • Colour: Any shade of gold – from light cream to deep golden.
  • Build: Strong, balanced, with a friendly expression and floppy ears.

Why Golden Retrievers are so popular in India

  • Temperament: They are friendly with everyone – children, strangers, other dogs. Very low aggression.
  • Trainable: They are eager to please and very smart – easy to train.
  • Patient: They tolerate clumsy handling by children – good family dogs.
  • Active but not hyper: They enjoy walks and play but also like to relax at home.
🩺 Dr. Deepa’s warning: “Golden Retrievers have a very high cancer rate – up to 60% will get cancer in their lifetime. Regular vet checkups, a good diet, and keeping them at a healthy weight may help reduce risk.”

Common health problems in Golden Retrievers

  • Cancer: Most common types – lymphoma, hemangiosarcoma, mast cell tumours. Any lump should be checked immediately.
  • Hip and elbow dysplasia: Joint problems that cause limping and arthritis. Keep them lean and give joint supplements.
  • Ear infections: Floppy ears trap moisture and wax. Clean weekly with a vet‑approved ear cleaner.
  • Skin allergies (atopic dermatitis): Itchy skin, especially paws and belly. May need special shampoos or medication.
  • Bloat (gastric torsion): Life‑threatening stomach twist. Feed two small meals daily, no exercise after eating.
  • Heart disease: Subvalvular aortic stenosis (SAS) – a heart defect. Regular heart checks recommended.

Grooming – high maintenance

  • Daily brushing: Use a slicker brush and undercoat rake. Prevents mats and controls shedding.
  • During shedding season (spring/autumn): Brush twice daily – they “blow” their coat.
  • Bathe once a month or when dirty. Use a mild dog shampoo.
  • Ear cleaning: Weekly with a cotton ball – never use Q‑tips. Floppy ears need extra care.
  • Trim nails every 3–4 weeks. Brush teeth weekly.

Exercise requirements

Golden Retrievers need at least 1 hour of exercise daily. They love to run, swim, and play fetch.

  • Walks: 30–45 minutes twice a day (early morning and evening). Avoid peak heat – use the paw test (Week 13).
  • Swimming: Excellent low‑impact exercise – most Goldens love water.
  • Fetch and tug‑of‑war: Good for burning energy.
  • Mental games: Puzzle toys, nose work, training sessions – they are smart and enjoy learning.

Training and behaviour

  • Goldens are very easy to train with positive reinforcement (treats, praise). They want to please you.
  • Start training and socialisation as puppies – they grow into calm, confident adults.
  • They are not good guard dogs – they may lick an intruder! But they will bark to alert you.
  • They can develop separation anxiety if left alone for long hours. Avoid leaving them for more than 4–5 hours.

Feeding guidelines

  • High‑quality dog food – they need good protein and healthy fats for their coat and joints.
  • For an adult Golden Retriever, 2.5–3.5 cups of dry food per day (divided into two meals). Adjust based on activity and weight.
  • Do not overfeed – obesity is common and worsens joint problems. Measure every meal.
  • Limit treats – use small pieces of carrot or apple instead of high‑calorie biscuits.
  • Always fresh, clean water.

Real story from our clinic

A 7‑year‑old Golden Retriever, Goldie, came in for a routine checkup. Her owner had not noticed any problems. During the exam, we found a small lump on her chest. A biopsy showed it was an early mast cell tumour (a type of skin cancer). We removed it surgically. Goldie recovered well and has had no recurrence. Her owner said, “If we hadn’t come for the checkup, that lump would have grown. Regular vet visits saved her life.” Goldie is now 10 years old and still healthy.

Should you get a Golden Retriever?

Yes, if you:

  • Have time for daily exercise (1 hour) and grooming (daily brushing).
  • Can afford regular vet checkups (including cancer screening).
  • Want a gentle, family‑friendly dog.
  • Have a secure yard or access to safe walking areas.

Maybe not, if you:

  • Cannot handle heavy shedding – hair will be on everything.
  • Are not prepared for potential cancer treatment costs.
  • Live in a very hot city without air conditioning (they can overheat).
  • Work long hours and cannot give them companionship.

Action for Week 9

  1. If you have a Golden Retriever, feel their body for any lumps or bumps today. Check armpits, neck, belly, and chest.
  2. Clean their ears this week – look for redness, smell, or discharge.
  3. Start a daily brushing routine – it reduces shedding and prevents mats.
  4. If your Golden is over 5 years old, schedule a senior wellness check with blood work and a thorough physical exam.
