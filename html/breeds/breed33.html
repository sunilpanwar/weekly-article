🐕 Pomeranian – Tiny, Fluffy, but Prone to Knee, Windpipe, and Teeth Problems

The Pomeranian is a tiny, fluffy dog with a big personality. They are lively, bold, and very loyal to their family. They look like a little lion, with a thick double coat and a plumed tail. At Dr. Deepa pet vet clinic, we see Pomeranians that are wonderful companions, but they have several health issues because of their small size. They are prone to loose kneecaps (luxating patella), a collapsing windpipe (tracheal collapse), and serious dental disease. They also shed heavily and need regular grooming. They are good for apartments but need careful handling.

🐾 Key things to remember: Pomeranians have fragile knees – avoid jumping off furniture (use ramps). Their windpipe can collapse – use a harness, never a neck collar. Brush their teeth daily – they lose teeth early. Brush their thick coat 2–3 times a week to prevent mats.

What does a Pomeranian look like?

  • Size: Toy – 1.5–3.5 kg. Height 15–20 cm.
  • Coat: Thick, double coat – short undercoat and long, harsh outer coat. Sheds heavily year‑round, more in spring and autumn.
  • Colours: Many colours – orange, red, black, white, cream, blue, chocolate, sable.
  • Build: Small, compact, with a fox‑like face, pointed ears, and a plumed tail that lies flat on the back.

Why Pomeranians are popular in India

  • Very small size: Perfect for apartments and small homes.
  • Big personality: They are confident, bold, and entertaining.
  • Affectionate: They bond strongly with their family.
  • Good watchdogs: They bark to alert you – good for security.
🦵 Dr. Deepa’s warning: “Pomeranians have very fragile kneecaps (luxating patella). They can pop out of place, causing pain and limping. Avoid jumping off furniture – use ramps. Their windpipe is also weak – never use a neck collar; always use a harness. And their small mouth means terrible teeth – brush daily or they will lose teeth by age 5.”

Common health problems in Pomeranians

  • Luxating patella (loose kneecaps): Very common. Signs: skipping, hopping, holding up a leg, limping. Keep them lean and avoid high jumps.
  • Tracheal collapse (collapsing windpipe): The windpipe flattens, causing a honking cough, especially when excited or pulling on a collar. Use a harness, never a neck collar.
  • Dental disease: Very common. Bad breath, red gums, loose teeth. Brush daily and get yearly dental cleanings.
  • Eye problems: Cataracts, dry eye, tear staining. Keep hair away from eyes.
  • Heart disease: Patent ductus arteriosus (PDA) and other defects. Regular heart checks recommended.
  • Obesity: They love to eat – measure portions.

Grooming – moderate to high

  • Brush 2–3 times a week: Use a slicker brush and steel comb. Their thick double coat mats easily – pay attention to behind ears, armpits, and belly. During heavy shedding (spring/autumn), brush daily.
  • Bathe every 2–4 weeks: Use a good dog shampoo. Dry thoroughly – damp fur mats faster.
  • Eye cleaning – daily: Wipe under eyes with a soft, damp cloth to prevent staining.
  • Ear cleaning – weekly: Use a cotton ball – never Q‑tips.
  • Trim nails every 2–3 weeks. Brush teeth daily.

Exercise and environment

  • Walks: 15–20 minutes twice a day. Avoid peak heat – use the paw test (Week 13).
  • No jumping: Use ramps for sofas and beds – protect their knees.
  • Use a harness: Never attach a leash to a neck collar – this can trigger tracheal collapse.
  • Indoor play: Short play with soft toys, puzzle feeders – they don’t need intense exercise.

Training and behaviour

  • Pomeranians are smart but can be stubborn. Use positive reinforcement (treats, praise).
  • They bark a lot – train a “quiet” command early.
  • House‑training can be difficult – be patient and consistent.
  • They can develop small dog syndrome (aggression, demanding behaviour) if not trained properly – set rules early.

Feeding guidelines

  • High‑quality small breed dog food – small kibble size.
  • For an adult Pomeranian, 1/4 to 1/2 cup of dry food per day (divided into two meals). Adjust based on activity.
  • Keep them lean – obesity worsens knee and windpipe problems.
  • Always fresh, clean water.

Real story from our clinic

A 3‑year‑old Pomeranian named “Teddy” came in with a honking cough. His owner used a neck collar for walks. X‑rays showed tracheal collapse. We switched him to a harness, and the cough reduced significantly. However, severe cases may need medication or surgery. His owner now uses a harness and says, “I had no idea neck collars are dangerous for small dogs.”

Should you get a Pomeranian?

Yes, if you:

  • Can use a harness instead of a collar – never a neck collar.
  • Can use ramps to prevent jumping – protect their knees.
  • Have time for daily tooth brushing and regular grooming.
  • Want a bold, lively, tiny companion.

Maybe not, if you:

  • Cannot commit to daily dental care – they will lose teeth.
  • Have young children who may handle the dog roughly – Pomeranians are fragile.
  • Live in a home with high furniture and no ramps – jumping damages knees.
  • Want a low‑grooming, quiet dog – they shed a lot and bark.

Action for Week 33

  1. If you have a Pomeranian, check their knees – any limping or hopping? Discuss with us.
  2. Switch to a harness if you use a neck collar – protects their windpipe.
  3. Start a daily tooth‑brushing routine – use dog‑safe toothpaste.
  4. If you are thinking of getting a Pomeranian, adopt from a rescue or ask the breeder about knee and heart screening.
