🐱 Exotic Shorthair – Persian Personality, Easy-Care Coat

The Exotic Shorthair is often called the “lazy man’s Persian”. They have the same flat face, round eyes, and sweet personality as a Persian, but with a short, plush coat that does not mat. At Dr. Deepa pet vet clinic, we see Exotics as wonderful, calm companions. However, they still have the same health problems as Persians – breathing issues, eye tearing, and polycystic kidney disease (PKD). They are perfect for people who love Persians but do not have time for daily brushing.

🐾 Key things to remember: Exotics have the same flat face as Persians – they can have breathing problems and eye tearing. They need daily eye cleaning. Grooming is easy – brush once a week. They are calm and perfect for apartments.

What does an Exotic Shorthair look like?

  • Size: Medium – 3.5–7 kg. Stocky and heavy-boned.
  • Coat: Short, dense, and plush – like a teddy bear. Very low matting.
  • Face: Flat (brachycephalic) – round face, small nose, large round eyes.
  • Colours: Same as Persians – white, black, blue, cream, silver, golden, many patterns.

Why Exotic Shorthairs are popular in India

  • Low grooming: No daily brushing needed – just a weekly brush.
  • Calm and quiet: They are not noisy or destructive – good for apartments.
  • Affectionate but not demanding: They like to be near you but are not as needy as some breeds.
  • Good with children and other pets: Very patient and gentle.
👁️ Dr. Deepa’s warning: “Because of their flat face, Exotics have the same eye tearing problem as Persians. You must clean their eyes daily to prevent staining and infections. Also, they can overheat easily – keep them in air conditioning during summer.”

Common health problems in Exotic Shorthairs

  • Brachycephalic airway syndrome: Flat face causes snoring, wheezing, and heat intolerance. Avoid hot weather and keep them at a healthy weight.
  • Eye tearing (epiphora): Tears overflow from the eyes, causing brown staining. Clean daily with a soft, damp cloth.
  • Polycystic kidney disease (PKD): A genetic disease that causes kidney failure. Ask the breeder for PKD screening. Adopted cats should be tested.
  • Dental disease: Crowded teeth. Brush weekly and get yearly dental checks.
  • Obesity: They love to eat and can become overweight – measure portions.

Grooming – easy

  • Brush once a week: Use a soft rubber brush or grooming glove – removes loose hair and spreads skin oils.
  • Bathe once a month or when needed: Use a mild cat shampoo. Dry thoroughly.
  • Eye cleaning – daily: Wipe under the eyes with a soft, damp cloth to remove tears and prevent staining.
  • Ear cleaning – weekly: Use a cotton ball – never Q‑tips.
  • Trim nails every 2–3 weeks. Brush teeth weekly.

Exercise and environment

Exotic Shorthairs are low‑energy cats. They are happy to lounge.

  • Play: 10–15 minutes once or twice a day – feather wands, soft toys, puzzle feeders.
  • Indoor only: Never let them outside – they cannot defend themselves and may overheat.
  • Heat sensitivity: Keep them in air conditioning or cool rooms in summer. Provide cooling mats or tiles.

Feeding guidelines

  • High‑quality cat food – they need good nutrition but watch calories.
  • For an adult Exotic Shorthair, 1/4 to 1/3 cup of dry food per day (divided into two meals) plus wet food.
  • Do not overfeed – obesity worsens breathing problems. Measure every meal.
  • Always fresh, clean water – use a wide, shallow bowl (their flat face makes drinking from deep bowls hard).

Real story from our clinic

An Exotic Shorthair named “Butter” came in with brown stains under her eyes and crusty discharge. Her owner had never cleaned her eyes. We showed her how to wipe daily with a damp cloth. Within two weeks, the staining faded. The owner also started cleaning Butter’s face wrinkles. “I didn’t know flat‑faced cats need eye cleaning every day,” she said. “Now it’s part of our morning routine.”

Should you get an Exotic Shorthair?

Yes, if you:

  • Love the Persian look but want less grooming.
  • Can clean their eyes daily and keep them in a cool home.
  • Want a calm, quiet, affectionate cat.
  • Are prepared for potential PKD screening and breathing issues.

Maybe not, if you:

  • Live in a very hot, humid city without air conditioning – they overheat easily.
  • Cannot commit to daily eye cleaning – staining and infections will happen.
  • Want an active, playful, outdoor cat – they are couch potatoes.

Action for Week 20

  1. If you have an Exotic Shorthair, check their eyes today – any discharge or staining? Start daily cleaning.
  2. Feel their nose – do they snore or wheeze at rest? Discuss breathing health with us.
  3. Schedule a PKD screening (ultrasound or DNA test) if not already done.
  4. If you are thinking of getting an Exotic, adopt from a rescue or ask the breeder for PKD and heart screening results.
