🐕 Great Dane – Gentle Giant, but Prone to Bloat and Bone Cancer

The Great Dane is a true giant – tall, powerful, but very gentle. They are called “Apollo of dogs” because of their majestic look. Despite their size, they are sweet, patient, and great with families. At Dr. Deepa pet vet clinic, we see Great Danes that are wonderful companions. But they have serious health problems because of their size. They are very prone to bloat (stomach twist) – a life‑threatening emergency. They also have high risk of bone cancer and joint problems. They do not live very long – usually 7–10 years. They need space, careful feeding, and regular vet checks.

🐾 Key things to remember: Great Danes have a very high risk of bloat – feed two small meals daily, no exercise after eating. They grow very fast as puppies – feed large breed puppy food to protect joints. They need a big home and a soft bed (hard floors hurt their joints).

What does a Great Dane look like?

  • Size: Very large – males 50–80 kg, females 45–65 kg. Height 70–85 cm (can stand on hind legs and reach 2 metres).
  • Coat: Short, smooth – easy to groom. Sheds moderately.
  • Colours: Brindle, fawn, blue, black, harlequin (white with black patches), or mantle.
  • Build: Tall, muscular, deep chest, long legs, and a long tail.

Why Great Danes are special – but challenging

  • Gentle temperament: They are friendly, patient, and good with children.
  • Protective: Their size alone is a deterrent – they will guard their family.
  • Calm indoors: Despite their size, they are not hyper – they like to lounge.
  • Low grooming: Short coat needs only weekly brushing.
🚨 Dr. Deepa’s warning: “Bloat (gastric torsion) is the number one killer of Great Danes. The stomach fills with gas and twists, cutting off blood supply. Signs: dry heaving, swollen belly, restlessness, collapse. This is a MINUTE‑BY‑MINUTE emergency – get to a vet immediately. Prevention is key: feed two or three small meals, no exercise after eating, and consider a gastropexy (stomach tacking) surgery.”

Common health problems in Great Danes

  • Bloat (gastric torsion): Life‑threatening. Prevention: feed small meals, no exercise after eating, elevated food bowls (some studies suggest), and gastropexy surgery.
  • Bone cancer (osteosarcoma): Common in giant breeds. Any limp that doesn’t go away needs X‑rays.
  • Hip and elbow dysplasia: Keep them lean and avoid over‑exercise as puppies.
  • Heart disease (dilated cardiomyopathy): Regular heart checks recommended.
  • Joint problems: Arthritis, cruciate ligament tears. Soft bedding and joint supplements help.

Grooming – easy

  • Brush once a week with a rubber curry brush – removes loose hair and spreads skin oils.
  • Bathe once a month or when dirty – use a mild dog shampoo.
  • Clean ears weekly – their floppy ears can trap moisture.
  • Trim nails every 3–4 weeks – their large nails can cause foot problems if too long.
  • Brush teeth weekly.

Exercise and environment – space is important

Great Danes need space – they are not apartment dogs. But they are not high‑energy.

  • Walks: 20–30 minutes twice a day. Avoid high-impact running on hard surfaces – protects joints.
  • Space: A house with a yard is ideal. They need room to move and lie down comfortably.
  • Soft bedding: Hard floors cause joint pain – provide orthopaedic or thick foam beds.
  • Puppy exercise: Do not over‑exercise young Danes – their bones are growing.

Feeding guidelines – very important

  • Large breed puppy food: For the first 18 months – controls growth rate to protect joints.
  • For an adult Great Dane, 4–8 cups of dry food per day (divided into TWO or THREE small meals).
  • Do not feed one large meal – this increases bloat risk.
  • Restrict water for 1 hour after meals.
  • Keep them lean – a fat Great Dane has severe joint problems.
  • Always fresh, clean water, but not right after eating.

Real story from our clinic

A 4‑year‑old Great Dane named “Apollo” was brought in with dry heaving and a swollen belly. The owner had fed him one large meal and then let him run in the yard. We rushed him to surgery – his stomach had twisted. We untwisted it and performed a gastropexy (tacking the stomach to the body wall) to prevent recurrence. Apollo survived. His owner now feeds him two small meals a day and waits an hour before exercise. “I had no idea bloat could happen so fast,” he said. “Now I am very careful.”

Should you get a Great Dane?

Yes, if you:

  • Have a large house with space for a giant dog.
  • Can afford higher costs – food, vet bills, medication are more expensive for giant breeds.
  • Are prepared for a short lifespan (7–10 years).
  • Can commit to bloat prevention (small meals, no exercise after eating).

Maybe not, if you:

  • Live in a small apartment – they need room.
  • Have a small budget – giant breeds cost more to feed and treat.
  • Are not prepared for potential emergency surgery for bloat.
  • Want a dog that lives 12+ years – choose a smaller breed.

Action for Week 23

  1. If you have a Great Dane, change to two or three small meals daily – never one large meal.
  2. Wait at least 1 hour after meals before any exercise or play.
  3. Discuss gastropexy (stomach tacking) with us – this surgery can prevent bloat.
  4. If you are thinking of getting a Great Dane, research breeders who screen for hip dysplasia and heart disease.
