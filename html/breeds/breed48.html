Week 48 • Dog Breed

🐕 Bernese Mountain Dog – Gentle Giant, but Prone to Cancer and Overheating

The Bernese Mountain Dog is a large, beautiful dog with a thick, tri‑coloured coat (black, white, and rust). They are calm, loyal, and great with families – often called “gentle giants”. At Dr. Deepa pet vet clinic, we see Bernese Mountain Dogs that are sweet and loving, but they have serious health problems. They have a very high risk of cancer, especially bone cancer and blood cancer. They also cannot tolerate Indian heat – their thick coat makes them overheat quickly. Their lifespan is short – usually only 6–8 years. They need a lot of space and are not for first‑time owners or hot climates.

🐾 Key things to remember: Bernese Mountain Dogs have a very high cancer rate – regular vet checks are essential. They cannot tolerate heat – need air conditioning in Indian summers. They shed heavily – brush daily. They need space – not for apartments.

What does a Bernese Mountain Dog look like?

  • Size: Large – 35–50 kg. Height 60–70 cm.
  • Coat: Thick, long, double coat – black with white chest and rust markings on legs and face.
  • Build: Strong, muscular, with a broad head, deep chest, and long tail.

Why Bernese Mountain Dogs are special – and challenging in India

  • Gentle temperament: They are calm, patient, and great with children and other pets.
  • Loyal: They bond deeply with their family.
  • Very high cancer risk: Up to 50% die from cancer – regular screenings are important.
  • Heat intolerant: Their thick coat is built for Swiss winters – they overheat easily in India.
🌡️ Dr. Deepa’s warning: “Bernese Mountain Dogs suffer in Indian summers. They need air conditioning 24/7. Never walk them in the sun – even early morning can be too hot. Also, their cancer risk is very high – any lump or weight loss should be checked immediately.”

Common health problems in Bernese Mountain Dogs

  • Cancer (osteosarcoma, histiocytic sarcoma, lymphoma): Very common. Signs: lumps, limping, weight loss, lethargy. Regular vet checks essential.
  • Heatstroke: Very high risk. Signs: heavy panting, dark red tongue, collapse – emergency!
  • Hip and elbow dysplasia: Keep them lean.
  • Bloat (gastric torsion): Deep chest increases risk – feed two small meals daily, no exercise after eating.
  • Progressive retinal atrophy (PRA): Eye disease – yearly eye exams recommended.

Grooming – high maintenance

  • Brush daily: Use a slicker brush and undercoat rake. Their thick coat mats easily and sheds heavily.
  • During shedding season (spring, autumn), brush twice daily – they “blow” their coat.
  • Bathe once a month or when dirty – use a mild dog shampoo. Dry thoroughly.
  • Clean ears weekly – their floppy ears trap moisture.
  • Trim nails every 3–4 weeks. Brush teeth weekly.

Exercise and environment

  • Exercise: Moderate – 30–45 minutes twice a day. Avoid peak heat – walk only before 7 AM and after 7 PM.
  • Space: They need a house with a yard – not for apartments.
  • Air conditioning: Essential in Indian summer – they cannot cool themselves.
  • Cooling mats: Provide a cooling mat or tile floor for them to lie on.

Training and behaviour

  • Bernese Mountain Dogs are intelligent but can be stubborn. Use positive reinforcement (treats, praise).
  • Early socialisation is important – they are naturally friendly but can be shy without exposure.
  • They are sensitive dogs – harsh training methods will break their spirit.
  • They can develop separation anxiety – do not leave them alone for long hours.

Feeding guidelines

  • High‑quality large breed dog food – good for joint and heart health.
  • For an adult Bernese Mountain Dog, 3–5 cups of dry food per day (divided into two meals). Adjust based on activity.
  • Keep them lean – obesity worsens joint problems and cancer risk.
  • Always fresh, cool water.

Real story from our clinic

A Bernese Mountain Dog named “Bernie” came in with a limp that wouldn’t go away. X‑rays showed bone cancer in his leg. His owner chose palliative care to keep him comfortable. Bernie lived another 6 months with pain medication. His owner said, “I knew Bernese have cancer risk, but I didn’t expect it so young. I wish I had known earlier.”

Should you get a Bernese Mountain Dog in India?

Only consider if you:

  • Live in a cool climate (hill station) or have 24/7 air conditioning.
  • Have a large, securely fenced yard.
  • Are prepared for a short lifespan (6–8 years) and potential cancer treatment costs.
  • Can commit to daily brushing and moderate exercise.

Otherwise, choose a heat‑tolerant breed like an Indie dog.

Action for Week 48

  1. If you have a Bernese Mountain Dog, schedule a cancer screening – blood work and full body check for lumps.
  2. Ensure air conditioning is available during summer – never leave them in a hot room.
  3. Brush their coat daily to manage shedding and prevent mats.
  4. If you are thinking of getting a Bernese, consider adopting a rescue or choose a breed better suited for India.
