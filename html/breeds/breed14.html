Week 14 • Cat Breed

🐈 Sphynx Cat – Hairless, Needs Skin Care and Temperature Control

The Sphynx cat is famous for being hairless. They look unique – with wrinkled skin, big ears, and lemon‑shaped eyes. But they are not maintenance‑free. Without fur, their skin produces oil that needs regular bathing. They also feel cold easily – they need warm rooms and sweaters in winter. At Dr. Deepa pet vet clinic, we see Sphynx cats that are very friendly, playful, and almost dog‑like. But they need extra care compared to furry cats.

🐾 Key things to remember: Sphynx cats need weekly baths to remove skin oil. They get cold easily – keep your home warm and use pet sweaters in winter. They also sunburn easily – keep them out of direct sunlight.

What does a Sphynx cat look like?

  • Size: Medium – 3.5–5 kg. Lean and muscular.
  • Coat: Appears hairless, but has a fine peach‑fuzz layer. Skin is wrinkled, especially on the face and shoulders.
  • Colours: Skin colour can be white, black, blue, cream, or many patterns.
  • Ears: Very large, wide at the base – need regular cleaning.
  • Build: Warm to the touch – their body temperature is slightly higher than furry cats.

Why Sphynx cats are popular in India

  • Unique look: People are drawn to their unusual appearance.
  • Friendly and social: They love attention, follow owners around, and get along with children and dogs.
  • No shedding: Great for people with allergies (though it’s the saliva, not fur, that causes most allergies).
  • Playful: They remain active and kitten‑like into adulthood.
🧼 Dr. Deepa’s warning: “Sphynx cats produce oil that would normally be absorbed by fur. Without fur, the oil builds up on their skin. You must bathe them weekly to prevent acne, greasiness, and skin infections.”

Common health problems in Sphynx cats

  • Skin problems: Oily buildup, acne, yeast infections, and dermatitis. Weekly baths are essential.
  • Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM): A heart disease common in Sphynx. Yearly heart screenings are important.
  • Temperature sensitivity: They get cold easily and can develop hypothermia. Keep indoor temperature warm. They also get sunburn – no direct sunlight.
  • Dental disease: Brush teeth weekly.
  • Patellar luxation: Loose kneecaps – can cause limping.

Grooming – moderate but very important

  • Bath once a week: Use a mild, hypoallergenic cat shampoo. Their skin is sensitive – rinse thoroughly. Dry them completely with a soft towel (they will get cold if wet).
  • Ear cleaning – weekly: Their large ears produce a lot of wax. Use a cotton ball and ear cleaner – never Q‑tips.
  • Nail trimming – every 2 weeks: Their nails can get oily and sticky.
  • Wipe skin folds: Clean between their wrinkles with a damp cloth, then dry.
  • Tooth brushing – weekly.

Temperature control – very important

  • Winter: Keep your home warm (above 20°C). Provide pet sweaters, heated beds, or soft blankets.
  • Summer: They can tolerate heat, but avoid direct sun – they burn easily. Provide shade and cool water.
  • Never leave them in a cold room or draft – they can get sick.

Exercise and play

Sphynx cats are active and playful. They need daily interaction.

  • Play sessions: 15–20 minutes, twice a day – feather wands, fetch, puzzle toys.
  • Climbing: Cat trees and shelves – they love to be up high.
  • Attention: They do not like being left alone for long hours. They are very social.

Feeding guidelines

  • High‑quality cat food – they have a high metabolism and eat more than furry cats of the same size.
  • For an adult Sphynx, 1/2 to 3/4 cup of dry food per day (divided into two meals) plus wet food. Adjust based on activity.
  • Avoid overfeeding – they can become overweight.
  • Always fresh, clean water.

Real story from our clinic

A family brought their Sphynx cat “Bare” for a checkup. He had blackheads and greasy skin. The owner was not bathing him regularly. We explained that Sphynx need weekly baths – not just wiping. We showed them how to bathe using a mild shampoo and dry thoroughly. After a month of weekly baths, Bare’s skin cleared up. The owner said, “I didn’t know hairless cats needed so much bathing. Now we have a routine every Sunday.”

Should you get a Sphynx cat?

Yes, if you:

  • Have time for weekly bathing and ear cleaning.
  • Can keep your home warm (or use sweaters) in winter.
  • Want a very friendly, social, dog‑like cat.
  • Are prepared for yearly heart screenings (HCM).

Maybe not, if you:

  • Cannot commit to weekly bathing – the cat will develop skin problems.
  • Live in a very cold or drafty home.
  • Want a low‑maintenance, independent cat (get an Indie cat).
  • Are not ready for potential expensive heart treatment.

Action for Week 14

  1. If you have a Sphynx, check their skin – any blackheads, greasiness, or red spots? Start weekly baths.
  2. Clean their ears today – Sphynx ears produce more wax than furry cats.
  3. If your home is cold, provide a heated bed or a soft sweater for your Sphynx.
  4. If you are thinking of getting a Sphynx, ask the breeder about HCM screening in the parents.
