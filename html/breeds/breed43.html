🐕 Kombai – Powerful Working Dog, Needs Strong Leadership and Secure Fencing

The Kombai (also called the Indian Bore Hound) is a powerful, muscular dog from Tamil Nadu. They were bred to hunt wild boar and guard property. They are fearless, loyal, and very protective of their family. At Dr. Deepa pet vet clinic, we see Kombais as healthy, hardy dogs that are well‑suited to Indian climates. However, they are not for everyone. They need a confident, experienced owner, a very secure yard, and early training. They can be aggressive with strangers and other dogs. They are not recommended for homes with small children or small pets.

🐾 Key things to remember: Kombais are powerful guard dogs – they need strong leadership and early socialisation. They require a very high, secure fence (they can jump and dig). They are not aggressive without reason, but they will protect their territory. They are not for first‑time owners.

What does a Kombai look like?

  • Size: Medium to large – 15–25 kg. Height 50–60 cm.
  • Coat: Short, smooth – colours: red, brown, or tan with a black mask.
  • Build: Muscular, deep chest, strong legs, and a short tail. Ears are usually cropped (traditional) or left natural.

Why Kombais are special – and challenging

  • Fearless guard dog: They will protect their family and property without hesitation.
  • Loyal to family: They bond deeply with their owners but are aloof with strangers.
  • Adapted to Indian climate: They handle heat well and have few genetic problems.
  • High prey drive: They may chase and kill small animals – not safe with cats or small dogs.
🛡️ Dr. Deepa’s warning: “Kombais are not for apartment living or for owners who cannot provide firm, consistent training. They need a very secure yard – they can jump over 6‑foot fences and dig under. Without early socialisation, they can become aggressive with strangers and other dogs.”

Common health problems in Kombais

  • Hip dysplasia: Less common than in other large breeds, but possible – keep them lean.
  • Bloat (gastric torsion): Deep chest increases risk – feed two small meals daily, no exercise after eating.
  • Skin allergies: Short coat can be sensitive – use vet‑approved shampoos.
  • Eye problems: Rare – but yearly checks are good.

Grooming – very easy

  • Brush once a week with a rubber curry brush – removes loose hair and spreads skin oils.
  • Bathe once a month or when dirty – they are clean dogs.
  • Clean ears weekly – their erect or semi‑pricked ears are less prone to infection.
  • Trim nails every 3–4 weeks.
  • Brush teeth weekly.

Exercise and environment – very important

  • Secure yard: A very high fence (minimum 6–7 feet) that extends into the ground (to prevent digging). Kombais are escape artists.
  • Exercise: 45–60 minutes of vigorous exercise daily – running, fetch, or long walks.
  • Leash walking: Always on a leash outside the yard – they can be reactive to other dogs or strangers.
  • Not for apartments: They need space and a secure outdoor area.

Training and behaviour

  • Kombais need an experienced, confident owner who can provide firm, consistent leadership.
  • Start training and socialisation as a puppy – expose them to many people, dogs, and situations.
  • Use positive reinforcement – harsh methods can make them fearful or aggressive.
  • They are natural protectors – teach them the difference between a normal visitor and a real threat.

Feeding guidelines

  • High‑quality dog food – they need good protein for their muscle.
  • For an adult Kombai, 2–3 cups of dry food per day (divided into two meals). Adjust based on activity.
  • Keep them lean – obesity affects joints and heart.
  • Always fresh, clean water.

Real story from our clinic

A Kombai named “Veeran” was brought in because he had bitten a delivery person who entered the yard. The owner had not socialised him as a puppy. We explained that Kombais need training to distinguish between friends and threats. The owner started a strict training programme with a professional behaviourist. Veeran is now managed with clear boundaries – he is kept in a separate area when guests arrive. The owner said, “I didn’t know this breed needed so much training. Now I am very careful.”

Should you get a Kombai?

Yes, if you:

  • Are an experienced dog owner – not your first dog.
  • Have a very secure, high‑fenced yard (6+ feet, dig‑proof).
  • Have time for daily exercise and consistent training.
  • Want a fearless guard dog for your property.

Maybe not, if you:

  • Are a first‑time owner – Kombais need a confident leader.
  • Live in an apartment or have a small, insecure yard.
  • Have young children or small pets – Kombais can be aggressive with strangers.
  • Are not willing to commit to lifelong training and socialisation.

Action for Week 43

  1. If you have a Kombai, check your fencing – can they jump over or dig under? Reinforce immediately.
  2. Start a daily training session – focus on recall and “leave it” commands.
  3. Socialise your Kombai (if still young) – expose them to different people in controlled settings.
  4. If you are thinking of getting a Kombai, visit a rescue or speak to an experienced breeder – understand the commitment.
