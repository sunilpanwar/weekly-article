Week 22 • Cat Breed

🐈‍⬛ Bombay Cat – Mini Panther Look, Prone to Obesity and Dental Issues

The Bombay cat looks like a small black panther. They have sleek black fur, copper or gold eyes, and a muscular body. They were bred to look like a wild cat but have a very sweet, friendly personality. At Dr. Deepa pet vet clinic, we see Bombays that are affectionate, playful, and good with families. But they also have some common problems – they love to eat and can become overweight, and they need regular dental care. They are indoor cats and do well in apartments.

🐾 Key things to remember: Bombays have a big appetite – measure every meal to prevent obesity. They are prone to dental disease – brush their teeth weekly. They are very social and do not like being left alone for long hours.

What does a Bombay cat look like?

  • Size: Medium – 3–6 kg. Muscular and heavy for their size.
  • Coat: Short, black, shiny – like patent leather. Low grooming.
  • Eyes: Round, copper or gold colour – very striking.
  • Build: Sleek, athletic, with a rounded head and medium ears.

Why Bombays are popular in India

  • Unique look: They look like a mini panther – very attractive.
  • Friendly and affectionate: They love to be around people and are good with children.
  • Intelligent and playful: They can learn tricks and enjoy interactive toys.
  • Low grooming: Short coat needs only weekly brushing.
🦷 Dr. Deepa’s warning: “Bombays are prone to dental disease – their gums can become red and sore, and they can lose teeth. Brush their teeth weekly and bring them for yearly dental checks. Also, they are food‑obsessed – do not leave food out all day.”

Common health problems in Bombays

  • Obesity: Very common. They will eat anything and act hungry always. Measure portions strictly.
  • Dental disease (gingivitis, periodontitis): Gums become red, swollen, and painful. Regular brushing and professional cleaning are important.
  • Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM): A heart disease common in many breeds. Yearly heart screening is recommended.
  • Respiratory issues: Their flat‑ish face can cause mild breathing problems – keep them cool.

Grooming – easy

  • Brush once a week with a soft rubber brush or grooming glove – removes loose hair and makes the coat shiny.
  • Bathe rarely – only when dirty. Their coat is naturally glossy.
  • Clean ears weekly with a cotton ball – never Q‑tips.
  • Trim nails every 2–3 weeks.
  • Brush teeth weekly – very important for Bombays.

Exercise and play

Bombays are active and playful. They need daily interaction to prevent boredom and obesity.

  • Play sessions: 15–20 minutes twice a day – feather wands, fetch, puzzle toys.
  • Climbing: Cat trees or shelves – they like to be up high.
  • Companionship: They do best with another cat or a dog – they hate being alone.

Feeding guidelines – very important

  • High‑quality cat food – but measure portions strictly.
  • For an adult Bombay, 1/4 to 1/3 cup of dry food per day (divided into two meals) plus wet food.
  • Do not free‑feed (leave food out). They will overeat and become obese.
  • Limit treats – use small pieces of cooked chicken or fish.
  • Always fresh, clean water.

Real story from our clinic

A Bombay cat named “Shadow” came in for a checkup. He weighed 7 kg – he should have been 4 kg. He could barely groom himself and was panting after small play sessions. His owner was free‑feeding dry food. We put Shadow on a strict diet: measured meals, wet food instead of dry, and daily play. He lost 1.5 kg in 4 months. His owner now measures every meal and says, “I didn’t realise he was so fat. Now he runs and plays again.”

Should you get a Bombay cat?

Yes, if you:

  • Can commit to strict portion control – they will overeat if allowed.
  • Can brush their teeth weekly and get yearly dental checks.
  • Want a friendly, playful, affectionate cat.
  • Are home often – they do not like being left alone.

Maybe not, if you:

  • Cannot resist giving extra food – they will become obese.
  • Work long hours and cannot give them attention – they get lonely.
  • Want a low‑maintenance, independent cat (get an Indie cat).

Action for Week 22

  1. If you have a Bombay, check their teeth today – any red gums or bad breath? Schedule a dental check.
  2. Weigh your cat – feel their ribs. If you can’t feel them easily, reduce food by 10%.
  3. Start a weekly tooth‑brushing routine – use cat‑safe toothpaste.
  4. If you are thinking of getting a Bombay, adopt from a rescue – many need homes due to health or behaviour issues.
