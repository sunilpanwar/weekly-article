🐕 Rajapalayam – Rare Indian Sighthound, Needs Space and Exercise

The Rajapalayam is a majestic, pure white sighthound from Tamil Nadu. They were historically used to hunt wild boar and guard estates. They are tall, muscular, and very loyal to their family. At Dr. Deepa pet vet clinic, we see Rajapalayams as rare but wonderful dogs – they are healthy, hardy, and well‑suited to Indian climates. However, they need a lot of space, daily exercise, and early training. They are not for apartments or first‑time owners. They also have a high prey drive – they will chase small animals.

🐾 Key things to remember: Rajapalayams need a large, secure yard – they can jump high fences. They need daily running (not just walks). They are independent and need early training. They are prone to deafness (white dogs) – test hearing.

What does a Rajapalayam look like?

  • Size: Large – 25–35 kg. Height 60–75 cm.
  • Coat: Short, smooth, pure white – easy to groom. Some have a pale cream tint.
  • Build: Tall, lean, deep chest, long legs, and a tapering tail. Nose and paw pads are usually pink.

Why Rajapalayams are special – and challenging

  • Rare breed: They are endangered – owning one helps preserve the breed.
  • Loyal and protective: They bond strongly with family and are excellent guard dogs.
  • Adapted to Indian climate: They handle heat well and have few genetic problems.
  • High prey drive: They will chase cats, squirrels, or small dogs – not good for homes with small pets.
🏃 Dr. Deepa’s warning: “Rajapalayams need to run – a leash walk is not enough. You need a large, secure yard where they can run daily. They can jump over 6‑foot fences – your fencing must be very high. They are not apartment dogs.”

Common health problems in Rajapalayams

  • Deafness (congenital): White dogs with unpigmented skin are prone to deafness – like Dalmatians. Test hearing as a puppy.
  • Bloat (gastric torsion): Deep chest increases risk – feed two small meals daily, no exercise after eating.
  • Skin allergies: White skin can be sensitive to sun – avoid midday sun exposure.
  • Eye problems: Progressive retinal atrophy (PRA) – rare but possible.

Grooming – very easy

  • Brush once a week with a rubber curry brush – removes loose hair and spreads skin oils.
  • They shed moderately – brush more in spring and autumn.
  • Bathe once a month or when dirty – use a whitening shampoo for white coats.
  • Clean ears weekly – their erect ears are less prone to infection.
  • Trim nails every 3–4 weeks – their nails wear down less if they run on grass.
  • Brush teeth weekly.

Exercise and environment – very important

  • Running space: A large, securely fenced yard (minimum 6‑foot height) is essential. They need to run free daily – not just walks.
  • Exercise: 1+ hour of running or vigorous play daily. They excel at lure coursing (chasing a fake prey).
  • Leash walking: Always on a leash outside a secure yard – they will chase anything that moves.
  • Not for apartments: They need space – small homes or apartments will frustrate them.

Training and behaviour

  • Rajapalayams are intelligent but independent – training requires patience and consistency.
  • Use positive reinforcement – they do not respond well to harsh methods.
  • Early socialisation is essential – they can be wary of strangers and aggressive with strange dogs.
  • They are not recommended for homes with cats, rabbits, or small dogs – their prey drive is very high.

Feeding guidelines

  • High‑quality large breed dog food – they need good nutrition for their energy levels.
  • For an adult Rajapalayam, 3–5 cups of dry food per day (divided into two meals). Adjust based on activity.
  • Keep them lean – obesity stresses joints and heart.
  • Always fresh, clean water – they drink a lot after running.

Real story from our clinic

A family adopted a Rajapalayam puppy named “Virat”. They lived in a small apartment and walked him on a leash twice a day. Virat became restless, chewed furniture, and barked constantly. We explained that he needed to run, not just walk. The family moved to a house with a large yard and built a 6‑foot fence. Virat now runs daily and is a calm, happy dog. His owner said, “We didn’t know sighthounds need to run. Now he is a different dog.”

Should you get a Rajapalayam?

Yes, if you:

  • Have a large, securely fenced yard (minimum 6‑foot fence).
  • Have time for daily running and exercise (1+ hour).
  • Are an experienced dog owner – not your first dog.
  • Want a rare, loyal, and healthy Indian breed.

Maybe not, if you:

  • Live in an apartment or small home – they need space.
  • Have small pets (cats, rabbits) – they will chase.
  • Cannot provide a very high, secure fence – they can jump over normal fences.
  • Want a dog that loves all strangers – Rajapalayams are protective.

Action for Week 39

  1. If you have a Rajapalayam, check your fencing – can they jump over? Reinforce to at least 6 feet.
  2. Test their hearing – clap behind them while they sleep. No response? Schedule a BAER test.
  3. Increase daily running time – a tired Rajapalayam is a well‑behaved one.
  4. If you are thinking of getting a Rajapalayam, adopt from a rescue or contact a responsible breeder who tests for deafness.
