🐕 Cocker Spaniel – Beautiful Floppy Ears, But Prone to Ear Infections

The Cocker Spaniel is a small, friendly dog with a silky coat, long floppy ears, and a sweet expression. They are happy, affectionate, and great with families. At Dr. Deepa pet vet clinic, we see many Cocker Spaniels because they are very popular in India. But they have some serious health problems. Their long, floppy ears trap moisture and wax – they get ear infections very easily. Their eyes can also have problems (cataracts, glaucoma). Their coat mats if not brushed daily. If you want a Cocker Spaniel, you must be ready for daily grooming and weekly ear cleaning.

🐾 Key things to remember: Cocker Spaniels need weekly ear cleaning to prevent painful infections. They need daily brushing – their coat mats easily. They are prone to eye problems and can become overweight – measure food.

What does a Cocker Spaniel look like?

  • Size: Small to medium – 10–15 kg. Height 35–40 cm.
  • Coat: Long, silky, feathered on ears, legs, and belly. Needs daily brushing.
  • Colours: Black, golden, red, chocolate, or parti‑colour (white with patches).
  • Build: Sturdy, compact, with a rounded head, long floppy ears, and a cheerful tail.

Why Cocker Spaniels are popular in India

  • Friendly and gentle: They are good with children and other pets.
  • Affectionate: They love to be with their family and are very loyal.
  • Active but not hyper: They enjoy walks and play but also like to relax.
  • Beautiful appearance: Their long coat and floppy ears make them very attractive.
👂 Dr. Deepa’s warning: “Cocker Spaniels have very narrow ear canals and floppy ears that trap moisture. This is a perfect recipe for ear infections. You must clean their ears WEEKLY with a vet‑approved ear cleaner. Signs of infection: head shaking, scratching, bad smell, brown discharge. If you don’t clean regularly, they will suffer from chronic, painful ear disease.”

Common health problems in Cocker Spaniels

  • Ear infections (otitis externa): Very common. Clean weekly. Signs: shaking head, scratching ears, redness, bad smell.
  • Eye problems: Cataracts (blindness), glaucoma (high pressure), cherry eye (prolapsed third eyelid). Yearly eye checks recommended.
  • Skin allergies (atopic dermatitis): Itchy skin, especially paws and belly. May need special shampoos or medication.
  • Obesity: They love to eat – measure portions strictly.
  • Heart disease (dilated cardiomyopathy): Regular heart checks recommended.
  • Bloat (gastric torsion): Less common, but possible – feed two small meals daily.

Grooming – high maintenance

  • Brush daily: Use a slicker brush and steel comb. Their coat mats easily, especially behind ears, armpits, and belly.
  • Professional grooming every 4–6 weeks: Many owners keep the coat trimmed short for easier care.
  • Bathe every 2–3 weeks: Use a good dog shampoo and conditioner. Dry thoroughly – damp fur mats faster.
  • Ear cleaning – weekly (very important): Use a cotton ball and vet‑approved ear cleaner. Gently wipe the outer ear – never go inside.
  • Trim nails every 3–4 weeks. Brush teeth weekly.

Exercise and environment

  • Walks: 20–30 minutes twice a day. Avoid peak heat – use the paw test (Week 13).
  • Play: Fetch, puzzle toys, nose work – they are smart and enjoy using their nose.
  • Secure yard: They can follow scents and wander off – always supervise or use a leash.

Training and behaviour

  • Cocker Spaniels are smart and eager to please – easy to train with positive reinforcement.
  • They are sensitive – harsh scolding can make them fearful. Use gentle methods.
  • They can develop separation anxiety – do not leave them alone for very long hours.
  • They may bark at strangers – good watchdogs.

Feeding guidelines

  • High‑quality dog food – measure portions to prevent obesity.
  • For an adult Cocker Spaniel, 1–1.5 cups of dry food per day (divided into two meals). Adjust based on activity.
  • Limit treats – use small pieces of carrot or apple.
  • Always fresh, clean water.

Real story from our clinic

A Cocker Spaniel named “Coco” came in shaking his head and scratching his ears constantly. His ears were red, smelly, and full of brown wax – a severe yeast and bacterial infection. His owner had never cleaned his ears. We cleaned them thoroughly and prescribed ear drops. We also showed the owner how to clean ears weekly. After two weeks, Coco’s ears were healthy. The owner now cleans them every Sunday. “I didn’t know floppy ears need weekly cleaning,” she said. “Now it’s part of our routine.”

Should you get a Cocker Spaniel?

Yes, if you:

  • Can commit to daily brushing and weekly ear cleaning.
  • Are prepared for potential eye problems and ear infections.
  • Have time for daily walks and play.
  • Want a friendly, affectionate, gentle family dog.

Maybe not, if you:

  • Cannot brush daily – their coat will mat painfully.
  • Cannot clean ears weekly – they will get chronic infections.
  • Live in a very hot, humid city – ear infections worsen.
  • Want a low‑grooming, independent dog (get an Indie dog).

Action for Week 27

  1. If you have a Cocker Spaniel, check their ears today – smell them, look for redness or brown discharge. Clean if needed.
  2. Brush their coat – feel for mats. If you find mats, carefully brush them out or have a groomer shave them.
  3. Schedule an eye check – Cocker Spaniels are prone to cataracts and glaucoma.
  4. If you are thinking of getting a Cocker Spaniel, adopt from a rescue or ask the breeder about eye and ear health in the parents.
