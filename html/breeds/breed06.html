Week 6 • Cat Breed

🐈 Siamese Cat – Vocal, Social, and Prone to Dental Issues

The Siamese cat is one of the oldest and most recognisable cat breeds in the world. With their striking blue eyes, sleek body, and loud voice, they are full of personality. They love to talk to you, follow you around, and be part of everything you do. At Dr. Deepa pet vet clinic, we see Siamese cats as very social – they do not like being left alone for long hours. They are also prone to dental problems, so brushing their teeth is very important.

🐾 Key things to remember: Siamese cats are very vocal – they will “talk” to you all day. They need company and can get lonely if left alone. Brush their teeth weekly to prevent dental disease.

What does a Siamese cat look like?

  • Size: Medium – 2.5–5 kg. Sleek and muscular, not heavy.
  • Coat: Short, fine, and shiny – very low grooming needs.
  • Colours: Cream body with darker “points” on ears, face, paws, and tail – seal, chocolate, blue, or lilac.
  • Eyes: Bright, deep blue – almond‑shaped.
  • Build: Elegant, long body, long tail, and large ears.

Why Siamese cats are popular in India

  • Beautiful appearance: Their blue eyes and colour‑point coat are very attractive.
  • Playful and smart: They love to play fetch, learn tricks, and solve puzzles.
  • Affectionate: They bond deeply with their owners and love to sit on laps.
  • Low grooming: Their short coat needs only a weekly brush.
🗣️ Dr. Deepa’s warning: “Siamese cats are not quiet. They will meow loudly when hungry, bored, or lonely. If you live in a small apartment with thin walls, consider if you can handle the noise.”

Common health problems in Siamese cats

  • Dental disease (periodontitis): Very common. Their teeth are prone to plaque and gingivitis. Brush weekly and get yearly dental checks.
  • Respiratory infections: They can be sensitive to cold and stress. Keep them indoors and warm.
  • Amyloidosis (kidney/liver disease): A genetic condition where protein builds up in organs. Regular blood work helps catch it early.
  • Crossed eyes (strabismus): Some Siamese have slightly crossed eyes – usually harmless, but can affect vision.
  • Asthma: Some develop breathing issues. Avoid strong perfumes, smoke, and dusty litter.

Grooming – very easy

  • Brush once a week with a soft brush or rubber glove – removes loose hair and spreads skin oils.
  • Bathe rarely – they are very clean. Only if they get into something dirty.
  • Clean ears weekly with a cotton ball (never inside the ear canal).
  • Trim nails every 2–3 weeks.
  • Brush their teeth weekly with cat‑safe toothpaste – this is very important for Siamese.

Exercise and play

Siamese cats are active and playful. They need daily interaction to stay happy.

  • Play sessions: 15–20 minutes, twice a day – feather wands, fetch, laser pointers (end with a physical toy).
  • Puzzle toys: They love solving problems for treats – great for mental stimulation.
  • Climbing: Cat trees, shelves, or window perches – they like to be up high.
  • Companionship: They do well with another cat or a dog – they hate being alone.

Feeding guidelines

  • High‑quality cat food – wet food is good for dental health and hydration.
  • Measure portions – Siamese can become overweight. For an adult, 1/4 to 1/3 cup of dry food per day (divided into two meals) plus wet food.
  • Dental treats or chewing toys help reduce plaque.
  • Always fresh, clean water.

Real story from our clinic

A family had a Siamese cat named “Blue”. He was very loving but started drooling and eating less. We checked his mouth – his teeth were covered in tartar, and his gums were red and swollen. He needed a professional dental cleaning under anaesthesia. After the cleaning, Blue felt much better. His owner now brushes his teeth every week and brings him for yearly dental checks. “I didn’t know cat teeth could get so bad,” she said. “Now brushing is part of our Sunday routine.”

Should you get a Siamese cat?

Yes, if you:

  • Are home often – they need company and get lonely.
  • Don’t mind a vocal cat who “talks” throughout the day.
  • Can commit to weekly tooth brushing and dental care.
  • Want an active, playful cat who will entertain you.

Maybe not, if you:

  • Work long hours and cannot give them attention – they may become destructive or depressed.
  • Live in a very quiet, noise‑sensitive apartment – they meow loudly.
  • Want a low‑maintenance, independent cat (get an Indie cat instead).

Action for Week 6

  1. If you have a Siamese, check their teeth today – lift their lip and look for brown tartar or red gums.
  2. Start a weekly tooth‑brushing routine – use cat‑safe toothpaste.
  3. Spend extra playtime with your Siamese – they need interaction.
  4. If you are thinking of getting a Siamese, consider adopting two so they keep each other company.
