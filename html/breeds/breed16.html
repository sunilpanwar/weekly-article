🐱 British Shorthair – Calm, Independent, but Prone to Obesity and HCM

The British Shorthair is a calm, quiet, and independent cat. They have a round face, big round eyes, and a plush, dense coat. They look like a teddy bear. They are not as active or demanding as some other breeds. At Dr. Deepa pet vet clinic, we see British Shorthairs that are gentle and easy to live with. But they also have some health problems – they easily become overweight, and they can get a heart disease called HCM. They are not lap cats – they prefer to sit next to you, not on you.

🐾 Key things to remember: British Shorthairs love to eat – measure every meal to prevent obesity. They need yearly heart screening for HCM. They are independent – they don’t need constant attention, but they enjoy being near you.

What does a British Shorthair look like?

  • Size: Medium to large – 4–8 kg. Males are bigger than females.
  • Coat: Short, dense, and plush – like a velvet carpet. Low grooming needs.
  • Colours: Blue‑grey is the most famous, also black, white, cream, and many patterns.
  • Build: Round head, chubby cheeks, round eyes (copper or gold), stocky body, short legs.

Why British Shorthairs are popular in India

  • Calm temperament: They are quiet, not destructive, and good for apartments.
  • Independent: They don’t demand attention – good for working owners.
  • Good with children and other pets: They are patient and not aggressive.
  • Low grooming: Their short coat needs only weekly brushing.
🍽️ Dr. Deepa’s warning: “British Shorthairs are very food‑motivated. They will act hungry even after a full meal. Do not give in – obesity is their biggest health risk. A fat British Shorthair can develop diabetes, joint problems, and heart disease.”

Common health problems in British Shorthairs

  • Obesity: Very common. Measure food carefully and limit treats. An overweight cat has a shorter life.
  • Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM): A heart disease that thickens the heart muscle. Yearly heart screening (ultrasound) is recommended.
  • Dental disease: Brush teeth weekly.
  • Polycystic kidney disease (PKD): Less common than in Persians, but possible. Ask breeder for screening.

Grooming – easy

  • Brush once a week with a rubber curry brush or soft bristle brush – removes loose hair and distributes skin oils.
  • They shed more in spring and autumn – brush twice a week during shedding season.
  • Bathe rarely – only when dirty. Their coat is naturally water‑repellent.
  • Clean ears weekly with a cotton ball – never Q‑tips.
  • Trim nails every 3–4 weeks. Brush teeth weekly.

Exercise and play

British Shorthairs are not very active. They are happy to lounge. But they still need some play to prevent obesity.

  • Play sessions: 10–15 minutes, once or twice a day – feather wands, puzzle toys, treat balls.
  • Climbing: They can climb, but they are not extreme jumpers. A cat tree or a low shelf is enough.
  • Encourage movement: Place food puzzles around the house so they have to walk to eat.

Feeding guidelines – very important

  • High‑quality cat food – but measure portions strictly.
  • For an adult British Shorthair, 1/4 to 1/3 cup of dry food per day (divided into two meals) plus a small amount of wet food.
  • Do not free‑feed (leave food out all day). They will overeat.
  • Limit treats – use small pieces of cooked chicken or fish, not high‑calorie cat treats.
  • Always fresh, clean water.

Real story from our clinic

A 5‑year‑old British Shorthair named “Blue” came in for a checkup. He weighed 9 kg – he should have been 5 kg. He was so fat that he could not groom himself. He had arthritis and was pre‑diabetic. His owner was free‑feeding dry food. We put Blue on a strict diet: measured meals, no treats, and we added wet food for satiety. He lost 2 kg in 6 months. His owner now measures every meal and says, “I almost killed him with kindness. Now I know food is love, but too much is harm.”

Should you get a British Shorthair?

Yes, if you:

  • Want a calm, quiet, independent cat – good for apartments.
  • Can commit to strict portion control – they will overeat if allowed.
  • Are prepared for yearly heart screenings (HCM).
  • Want a low‑grooming, low‑energy cat.

Maybe not, if you:

  • Want a very playful, active cat (they are couch potatoes).
  • Cannot resist giving extra food – they will become obese.
  • Want a cat that will sit on your lap (they prefer sitting beside you).

Action for Week 16

  1. If you have a British Shorthair, weigh them today. Feel their ribs – can you feel them easily? If not, reduce food.
  2. Start measuring every meal with a standard cup – no guessing.
  3. Schedule a heart screening (echocardiogram) if your cat is over 2 years old.
  4. If you are thinking of getting a British Shorthair, adopt from a rescue or ask the breeder about HCM and PKD screening.
