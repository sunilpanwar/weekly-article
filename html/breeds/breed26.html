Week 26 • Cat Breed

🐈 Abyssinian Cat – Active, Curious, Prone to Kidney and Dental Issues

The Abyssinian cat is often called the “Aby”. They look like a small cougar – with a ticked coat, large ears, and an athletic body. They are very active, curious, and love to climb and explore. At Dr. Deepa pet vet clinic, we see Abyssinians that are full of energy and very intelligent. But they also have some serious health problems. They are prone to a kidney disease called renal amyloidosis. They also have high risk of dental issues and can suffer from a genetic eye disease. They are not for people who want a calm, lazy cat.

🐾 Key things to remember: Abyssinians are very active – they need climbing space and daily play. They are prone to kidney disease – yearly blood work is important. They also have dental problems – brush their teeth weekly.

What does an Abyssinian cat look like?

  • Size: Medium – 3–5 kg. Lean and muscular.
  • Coat: Short, ticked (each hair has bands of colour) – looks like a wild rabbit. Very low grooming.
  • Colours: Ruddy (warm brown), sorrel (cinnamon), blue, fawn.
  • Build: Sleek, long body, large ears, almond‑shaped eyes (gold or green), and a wedge‑shaped head.

Why Abyssinians are popular in India

  • Active and playful: They are always on the move – good for active families.
  • Intelligent and curious: They can learn tricks, open doors, and get into everything.
  • Low grooming: Short coat needs only weekly brushing.
  • Affectionate but not needy: They like to be near you but do not demand constant lap time.
🩺 Dr. Deepa’s warning: “Abyssinians have a high risk of renal amyloidosis – a disease where abnormal protein builds up in the kidneys, leading to kidney failure. Signs: increased thirst, increased urination, weight loss. There is no cure, but early detection can slow progression. Yearly blood and urine tests are essential.”

Common health problems in Abyssinians

  • Renal amyloidosis (kidney disease): Very common. Causes kidney failure. Yearly blood work (BUN, creatinine) and urine tests are important.
  • Dental disease (gingivitis, periodontitis): Common in Abyssinians. Brush teeth weekly and get yearly dental cleanings.
  • Progressive retinal atrophy (PRA): An eye disease that causes blindness. Responsible breeders test for this.
  • Patellar luxation: Loose kneecaps – can cause limping.
  • Pyruvate kinase deficiency: A genetic anaemia – rare but serious. DNA test available.

Grooming – very easy

  • Brush once a week with a soft rubber brush – removes loose hair and spreads skin oils.
  • Bathe rarely – they are very clean.
  • Clean ears weekly with a cotton ball – never Q‑tips.
  • Trim nails every 2–3 weeks.
  • Brush teeth weekly – very important.

Exercise and environment – very important

Abyssinians are high‑energy cats. They need a stimulating environment.

  • Play sessions: 15–20 minutes, two or three times a day – feather wands, fetch, puzzle toys.
  • Climbing: Tall cat trees, wall shelves, or a cat wheel – they love to climb and jump.
  • Secure home: They are escape artists – ensure windows and doors are secure.
  • Companionship: They do well with another active cat – they can get bored alone.

Feeding guidelines

  • High‑quality cat food – kidney health is a priority. Consider wet food to increase water intake.
  • For an adult Abyssinian, 1/4 to 1/3 cup of dry food per day (divided into two meals) plus wet food.
  • Keep them lean – obesity worsens kidney strain.
  • Always fresh, clean water – consider a cat water fountain to encourage drinking.

Real story from our clinic

A 7‑year‑old Abyssinian named “Rusty” came in for a checkup. His owner noticed he was drinking more water and losing weight. Blood tests showed early kidney disease (renal amyloidosis). We changed his diet to a kidney‑support formula and added a phosphate binder. Rusty lived another 3 years with good quality of life. His owner now brings his other Abyssinian for yearly kidney screening. “I had no idea this breed is prone to kidney problems,” she said. “Early detection gave us more time with Rusty.”

Should you get an Abyssinian cat?

Yes, if you:

  • Have time for daily play and an active lifestyle.
  • Can provide climbing space – tall cat trees or shelves.
  • Can commit to yearly kidney screening (blood and urine tests).
  • Want a smart, curious, energetic cat.

Maybe not, if you:

  • Want a calm, low‑energy, lap cat – Abyssinians are very active.
  • Work long hours and cannot give them attention – they get bored and destructive.
  • Cannot afford regular vet checks for kidney disease.
  • Have a small apartment with no climbing space.

Action for Week 26

  1. If you have an Abyssinian, schedule a kidney screening (blood work and urine test) – even if they seem healthy.
  2. Check their teeth – any redness or bad breath? Start weekly brushing.
  3. Provide climbing space – a tall cat tree or shelves – to burn their energy.
  4. If you are thinking of getting an Abyssinian, adopt from a rescue or ask the breeder for PRA and pyruvate kinase test results.
