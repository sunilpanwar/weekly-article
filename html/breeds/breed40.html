🐱 Oriental Shorthair – Sleek, Vocal, Prone to Dental and Respiratory Issues

The Oriental Shorthair is a close relative of the Siamese – they have the same sleek body, large ears, and talkative personality, but come in over 300 colours and patterns. They are very social, intelligent, and demand attention. At Dr. Deepa pet vet clinic, we see Oriental Shorthairs that are loving but can be overwhelming for first‑time owners. They are prone to dental disease, respiratory infections, and separation anxiety. They need a lot of interaction and do best with another cat. They are not for people who work long hours.

🐾 Key things to remember: Oriental Shorthairs are very vocal – they will “talk” all day. They need company – do not leave them alone for long hours. Brush their teeth weekly – they are prone to dental disease. They can get respiratory infections – keep them warm and stress‑free.

What does an Oriental Shorthair look like?

  • Size: Small to medium – 3–5 kg. Sleek and muscular.
  • Coat: Short, fine, shiny – very low grooming. Comes in many colours – white, black, blue, red, cream, lilac, chocolate, tabby, tortie, etc.
  • Build: Long, slender body, large pointed ears, wedge‑shaped head, and almond‑shaped green eyes.

Why Oriental Shorthairs are popular – and demanding

  • Very affectionate: They bond deeply with their family and want to be involved in everything.
  • Intelligent and curious: They can learn tricks, open cabinets, and get into trouble.
  • Vocal: They meow, chirp, and yowl – they will tell you when they are hungry, bored, or lonely.
  • Low grooming: Short coat needs only weekly brushing.
🗣️ Dr. Deepa’s warning: “Oriental Shorthairs are not quiet cats. If you live in an apartment with thin walls, your neighbours may hear them. They also hate being alone – separation anxiety can lead to destructive behaviour. Consider getting two cats if you work long hours.”

Common health problems in Oriental Shorthairs

  • Dental disease (gingivitis, periodontitis): Very common. Brush teeth weekly and get yearly dental cleanings.
  • Upper respiratory infections: They are prone to cat flu (herpesvirus, calicivirus). Keep them warm, reduce stress, and vaccinate.
  • Separation anxiety: They become distressed when left alone – may urinate outside the box, over‑groom, or yowl.
  • Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM): A heart disease – yearly screening recommended.
  • Amyloidosis (kidney disease): Rare but possible – blood work can detect.

Grooming – very easy

  • Brush once a week with a soft rubber brush – removes loose hair and spreads skin oils.
  • Bathe rarely – they are clean.
  • Clean ears weekly – their large ears can trap wax.
  • Trim nails every 2–3 weeks.
  • Brush teeth weekly – very important.

Exercise and environment

  • Play: 15–20 minutes twice a day – feather wands, fetch, puzzle toys, climbing.
  • Companionship: They do best with another cat – preferably another Oriental or Siamese.
  • Indoor only: Never let them outside – they are too trusting and may get lost or injured.
  • Enrichment: Cat trees, window perches, rotating toys – they get bored easily.

Feeding guidelines

  • High‑quality cat food – good for dental and heart health.
  • For an adult Oriental Shorthair, 1/4 to 1/3 cup of dry food per day (divided into two meals) plus wet food. Adjust based on activity.
  • Keep them lean – obesity worsens heart strain.
  • Always fresh, clean water.

Real story from our clinic

An Oriental Shorthair named “Ziggy” came in with bad breath and drooling. His gums were bright red and his teeth had heavy tartar – advanced gingivitis. He needed a professional dental cleaning. His owner started weekly brushing. “I didn’t know this breed has such bad teeth,” she said. “Now we brush every Sunday.”

Should you get an Oriental Shorthair?

Yes, if you:

  • Are home often – they need attention and can get lonely.
  • Don’t mind a vocal cat who talks all day.
  • Can commit to weekly tooth brushing and dental care.
  • Can provide a companion (another cat) if you work long hours.

Maybe not, if you:

  • Work long hours and cannot give them attention – they will get anxious and destructive.
  • Live in a noise‑sensitive apartment – they meow loudly.
  • Want a low‑maintenance, independent cat (get an Indie cat).

Action for Week 40

  1. If you have an Oriental Shorthair, check their teeth – any bad breath or red gums? Schedule a dental check.
  2. Start a weekly tooth‑brushing routine – use cat‑safe toothpaste.
  3. If you are away long hours, consider adopting a second cat for company.
  4. If you are thinking of getting an Oriental Shorthair, adopt from a rescue or ask the breeder about dental and heart health in the parents.
