Week 29 • Dog Breed

🐕 Lhasa Apso – Long Coat, Independent, Needs Daily Grooming and Eye Care

The Lhasa Apso is a small, ancient breed from Tibet. They were bred to be indoor watchdogs in palaces and monasteries. They have a long, beautiful coat that reaches the floor. They are independent, confident, and loyal to their family. At Dr. Deepa pet vet clinic, we see Lhasa Apsos that are loving but can be stubborn with strangers. They need daily grooming – their coat mats very easily. They also have eye problems and kidney issues. They are good for apartments but need a committed owner.

🐾 Key things to remember: Lhasa Apsos need daily brushing – their long coat mats quickly. They are prone to eye problems (dry eye, cataracts) and kidney disease (renal dysplasia). Keep their face hair trimmed to prevent eye irritation.

What does a Lhasa Apso look like?

  • Size: Small – 5–7 kg. Height 25–28 cm.
  • Coat: Long, thick, double coat – falls straight to the floor. Needs daily brushing.
  • Colours: Gold, cream, black, white, or mixed.
  • Build: Sturdy, longer body than tall, with a feathered tail that curls over the back.

Why Lhasa Apsos are popular in India

  • Small size: Good for apartments and small homes.
  • Loyal and protective: They bond closely with family and make good watchdogs.
  • Long lifespan: They often live 12–15 years.
  • Independent: They are not needy like some small breeds.
🧴 Dr. Deepa’s warning: “Lhasa Apsos have a high risk of kidney problems (renal dysplasia). Yearly blood work is important to catch it early. Their long coat mats painfully if not brushed daily. Also, their eyes are prone to dry eye and corneal ulcers – keep hair away from eyes.”

Common health problems in Lhasa Apsos

  • Renal dysplasia (kidney disease): A genetic condition where kidneys don’t develop properly. Signs: increased thirst, increased urination, weight loss. Yearly blood and urine tests are important.
  • Eye problems: Dry eye (keratoconjunctivitis sicca), cataracts, progressive retinal atrophy (PRA), and corneal ulcers. Keep facial hair trimmed.
  • Dental disease: Small mouth = crowded teeth. Brush weekly.
  • Patellar luxation: Loose kneecaps – can cause limping.
  • Skin allergies: May develop itchy skin – use vet‑approved shampoos.

Grooming – very high maintenance

  • Brush daily: Use a slicker brush and steel comb. Their coat mats easily – pay attention to behind ears, armpits, and belly. Many owners keep the coat trimmed short (“puppy cut”) for easier care.
  • Bathe every 2–3 weeks: Use a good dog shampoo and conditioner. Dry thoroughly – damp fur mats faster.
  • Eye care – daily: Check for redness or discharge. Wipe gently with a soft, damp cloth. Trim hair around eyes to prevent irritation.
  • Ear cleaning – weekly: Use a cotton ball – never Q‑tips.
  • Trim nails every 3–4 weeks. Brush teeth weekly.

Exercise and environment

  • Walks: 15–20 minutes twice a day. Avoid peak heat – use the paw test (Week 13).
  • Indoor play: They enjoy short play sessions with soft toys or puzzle feeders.
  • Secure yard: They can be escape artists – ensure fencing is secure.

Training and behaviour

  • Lhasa Apsos are intelligent but independent. Training takes patience – use positive reinforcement (treats, praise).
  • They can be stubborn and may ignore commands if they don’t see the benefit.
  • Socialise early – they can be wary of strangers and other dogs.
  • They are good watchdogs – they bark to alert you.

Feeding guidelines

  • High‑quality small breed dog food – good for kidney health.
  • For an adult Lhasa Apso, 1/2 to 3/4 cup of dry food per day (divided into two meals). Adjust based on activity.
  • Keep them lean – obesity worsens joint and kidney problems.
  • Always fresh, clean water.

Real story from our clinic

A 6‑year‑old Lhasa Apso named “Leo” came in for increased thirst and weight loss. Blood tests showed kidney disease (renal dysplasia). His owner had no idea. We started a kidney‑support diet and added supplements. Leo lived another 4 years with good quality of life. His owner now brings his other Lhasa for yearly kidney checks. “I didn’t know this breed has kidney problems,” he said. “Early detection gave us more time.”

Should you get a Lhasa Apso?

Yes, if you:

  • Have time for daily brushing or budget for regular professional grooming.
  • Can trim hair around eyes and clean eyes daily.
  • Are prepared for yearly kidney screening (blood work).
  • Want a loyal, independent, small watchdog.

Maybe not, if you:

  • Cannot commit to daily brushing – their coat will mat painfully.
  • Want a very obedient, eager‑to‑please dog – Lhasa Apsos are stubborn.
  • Have small children who may handle the dog roughly – Lhasas prefer gentle handling.

Action for Week 29

  1. If you have a Lhasa Apso, schedule a kidney screening (blood and urine test) – even if they seem healthy.
  2. Check their eyes – any redness or discharge? Trim hair around eyes.
  3. Brush their coat today – feel for mats. If you find mats, carefully brush them out or have a groomer shave them.
  4. If you are thinking of getting a Lhasa Apso, adopt from a rescue or ask the breeder about kidney and eye health in the parents.
