🐈 Turkish Angora – Elegant White Cat, Prone to Deafness and Heart Disease

The Turkish Angora is an elegant, long‑haired cat, often pure white with striking blue or odd‑coloured eyes. They are graceful, playful, and very affectionate. At Dr. Deepa pet vet clinic, we see Turkish Angoras that are intelligent and love to be involved in everything. However, they have some serious health concerns. White cats with blue eyes have a high risk of congenital deafness. They are also prone to a heart disease called hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM). Their silky coat needs regular grooming, and they shed heavily in warm weather. They are best kept indoors.

🐾 Key things to remember: White Turkish Angoras with blue eyes are often deaf – test their hearing as a kitten. They are prone to HCM – yearly heart screening is recommended. Their long, silky coat needs brushing 2–3 times a week. They are active and need climbing space.

What does a Turkish Angora look like?

  • Size: Small to medium – 3–5 kg. Sleek and fine‑boned.
  • Coat: Long, silky, single coat (no undercoat). White is the most famous colour, but other colours exist.
  • Eyes: Almond‑shaped – blue, green, amber, or odd‑eyed (one blue, one amber/green).
  • Build: Slender, long body, long tail, large pointed ears, and a wedge‑shaped head.

Why Turkish Angoras are special – and challenging

  • Striking appearance: Their pure white coat and blue or odd eyes are beautiful.
  • Playful and intelligent: They love to climb, fetch, and open cabinets – very active.
  • Affectionate but not demanding: They enjoy attention but also like to explore.
  • Low matting coat: Their single coat mats less than double‑coated longhairs, but still needs regular brushing.
👂 Dr. Deepa’s warning: “Up to 80% of white Turkish Angoras with two blue eyes are deaf. Cats with one blue eye may be deaf on the blue‑eye side. Test hearing early – deaf cats need special care (keep indoors, approach carefully, use visual cues). Also, they are prone to HCM – yearly heart checks are important.”

Common health problems in Turkish Angoras

  • Deafness (congenital): Common in white cats with blue eyes. Signs: not responding to sounds, sleeping through loud noises, easily startled. Deaf cats can live normal lives indoors.
  • Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM): A heart disease that thickens the heart muscle. Yearly heart screening (echocardiogram) recommended.
  • Dental disease: Brush teeth weekly.
  • Obesity: They love to eat – measure portions.
  • Ataxia (cerebellar hypoplasia): Rare – wobbliness from a small cerebellum. Responsible breeders screen.

Grooming – moderate

  • Brush 2–3 times a week: Use a steel comb and soft slicker brush. Their single coat mats less than Persians, but still needs regular care.
  • During shedding season (spring/autumn): Brush daily – they shed a lot for a longhair.
  • Bathe rarely: Only when dirty – their coat is naturally water‑repellent.
  • Clean ears weekly: Their large ears can trap dirt – use a cotton ball.
  • Trim nails every 2–3 weeks. Brush teeth weekly.

Exercise and environment

  • Play: 15–20 minutes twice a day – feather wands, fetch, puzzle toys, climbing.
  • Climbing: They love heights – provide tall cat trees or shelves.
  • Indoor only: Never let them outside unsupervised – deaf cats are especially vulnerable.
  • Deaf cat care: Approach from the front, use vibration or hand signals, keep them indoors, and never let them roam.

Feeding guidelines

  • High‑quality cat food – good for heart health.
  • For an adult Turkish Angora, 1/4 to 1/3 cup of dry food per day (divided into two meals) plus wet food. Adjust based on activity.
  • Keep them lean – obesity worsens heart strain.
  • Always fresh, clean water.

Real story from our clinic

A family adopted a beautiful white Turkish Angora kitten with blue eyes. They noticed she didn’t respond to her name or clapping. A BAER hearing test confirmed she was deaf. The family was worried, but we taught them how to care for a deaf cat – approach from the front, use hand signals for commands, and keep her strictly indoors. Now, “Snow” is a happy, healthy cat who responds to light flashes and foot vibrations. Her owner says, “She’s just like any other cat, just quieter.”

Should you get a Turkish Angora?

Yes, if you:

  • Are prepared for possible deafness (white cats with blue eyes) and can adapt care.
  • Can commit to yearly heart screening (HCM).
  • Have time for regular brushing and play.
  • Want an active, intelligent, elegant cat.

Maybe not, if you:

  • Are not prepared for a deaf cat – they need special handling.
  • Live in a very hot, humid city without AC – they overheat more easily than shorthairs.
  • Want a low‑grooming, couch‑potato cat – Angoras are active.

Action for Week 32

  1. If you have a white Turkish Angora, test their hearing – clap behind them while they sleep. No response? Schedule a BAER test.
  2. Schedule a heart screening (echocardiogram) – even if they seem healthy.
  3. Brush their coat today – remove loose hair to keep them cool.
  4. If you are thinking of getting a Turkish Angora, adopt from a rescue or ask the breeder about hearing and HCM screening.
