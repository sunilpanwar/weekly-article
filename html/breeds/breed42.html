🐕 Chippiparai – Enduring Indian Sighthound, Loyal to One Owner

The Chippiparai is a rare Indian sighthound from Tamil Nadu, known for its incredible speed, stamina, and loyalty. They were traditionally used for hunting wild boar and deer. They are sleek, muscular, and extremely devoted – often bonding with just one person. At Dr. Deepa pet vet clinic, we see Chippiparai as healthy, heat‑tolerant dogs that are well‑suited to Indian climates. However, they need a lot of space to run, are not good with small pets, and need early socialisation. They are not for first‑time owners or apartment living.

🐾 Key things to remember: Chippiparai bond very strongly with one person – they may be aloof with others. They need a large, secure yard and daily running. They have a high prey drive – not safe with cats or small dogs. They are generally healthy but can have eye problems.

What does a Chippiparai look like?

  • Size: Medium to large – 15–22 kg. Height 55–65 cm.
  • Coat: Short, smooth – colours: fawn, cream, brown, black, or brindle.
  • Build: Lean, deep chest, long legs, curved tail, and a long head with semi‑pricked ears.

Why Chippiparai are special – and challenging

  • Extremely loyal: They bond with one person – they are not “family dogs” in the usual sense.
  • Great stamina: They can run for hours – perfect for active owners.
  • Adapted to Indian climate: They handle heat well and have few genetic problems.
  • High prey drive: They will chase small animals – not safe with cats, rabbits, or small dogs.
❤️ Dr. Deepa’s warning: “Chippiparai often choose one person and are indifferent to others. They need an experienced owner who can provide firm, gentle leadership. They are not recommended for homes with young children who may not respect their space.”

Common health problems in Chippiparai

  • Eye problems: Progressive retinal atrophy (PRA) and other inherited eye issues – yearly eye exams recommended.
  • Bloat (gastric torsion): Deep chest increases risk – feed two small meals daily, no exercise after eating.
  • Hip dysplasia: Less common, but possible – keep them lean.
  • Skin allergies: Short coat offers less protection – use vet‑approved shampoos.

Grooming – very easy

  • Brush once a week with a rubber curry brush – removes loose hair and spreads skin oils.
  • Bathe once a month or when dirty – they are clean dogs.
  • Clean ears weekly – their semi‑pricked ears are less prone to infection.
  • Trim nails every 3–4 weeks – they wear down less if they run on soft ground.
  • Brush teeth weekly.

Exercise and environment – very important

  • Running space: A large, securely fenced yard (minimum 6‑foot height) is essential. They need to run free daily – not just walks.
  • Exercise: 1+ hour of running or vigorous play daily. They excel at lure coursing.
  • Leash walking: Always on a leash outside a secure yard – they will chase anything that moves.
  • Not for apartments: They need space – small homes or apartments will frustrate them.

Training and behaviour

  • Chippiparai are intelligent but independent – training requires patience and consistency.
  • Use positive reinforcement – they do not respond well to harsh methods.
  • Early socialisation is essential – they can be wary of strangers and aggressive with strange dogs.
  • They are not recommended for homes with cats, rabbits, or small dogs – their prey drive is very high.

Feeding guidelines

  • High‑quality large breed dog food – they need good nutrition for their energy levels.
  • For an adult Chippiparai, 2–3 cups of dry food per day (divided into two meals). Adjust based on activity.
  • Keep them lean – obesity stresses joints and heart.
  • Always fresh, clean water – they drink a lot after running.

Real story from our clinic

A Chippiparai named “Vel” was brought in for limping after a run. X‑rays showed no fracture, but he had a muscle strain from over‑exertion. His owner had been running him for 2 hours on hard pavement. We advised switching to soft ground (grass or mud) and limiting high‑impact running to 45 minutes. Vel recovered quickly. His owner now varies his exercise and says, “I didn’t know sighthounds can strain muscles too. Now we run on softer surfaces.”

Should you get a Chippiparai?

Yes, if you:

  • Have a large, securely fenced yard (minimum 6‑foot fence).
  • Have time for daily running and exercise (1+ hour).
  • Are an experienced dog owner – not your first dog.
  • Want a loyal, one‑person dog and do not need a pet that loves everyone.

Maybe not, if you:

  • Live in an apartment or small home – they need space.
  • Have small pets (cats, rabbits) – they will chase.
  • Cannot provide a very high, secure fence – they can jump over normal fences.
  • Have young children who may not respect the dog’s space – Chippiparai can be aloof.

Action for Week 42

  1. If you have a Chippiparai, schedule a yearly eye exam to check for PRA.
  2. Check your fencing – can they jump over? Reinforce to at least 6 feet.
  3. Increase daily running time on soft ground – a tired Chippiparai is a happy one.
  4. If you are thinking of getting a Chippiparai, adopt from a rescue or contact a responsible breeder who tests for eye issues.
