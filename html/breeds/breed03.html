🐕 Labrador Retriever – Friendly, Active, but Needs Portion Control and Joint Care

The Labrador Retriever is one of the most popular dog breeds in India. They are friendly, full of energy, and great with families. But they also have some common health problems – especially obesity and joint issues. At Dr. Deepa pet vet clinic, we see many Labradors who are overweight because their owners do not measure food. With proper diet, exercise, and regular vet checks, a Labrador can live a long, happy life.

🐾 Key things to remember: Labradors love to eat – they will never say no to food. You must measure every meal. They also need daily exercise and joint support as they get older.

What does a Labrador look like?

  • Size: Large – males 30–36 kg, females 25–32 kg. Height about 55–60 cm.
  • Coat: Short, dense, and water‑repellent. Comes in black, yellow, or chocolate.
  • Body: Strong and athletic. They love to run, swim, and play fetch.
  • Face: Friendly eyes, medium‑length muzzle, floppy ears.

Why Labradors are popular in India

  • Friendly nature: They are great with children, other dogs, and even strangers.
  • Trainable: They are smart and eager to please – easy to train with treats and praise.
  • Active: They love walks, swimming, and games. Good for active families.
  • Adaptable: They can live in apartments if given enough exercise, but a house with a garden is better.
🍽️ Dr. Deepa’s warning: “Labradors are eating machines. They will act hungry even after a full meal. Do not give in – obesity is their biggest health risk. Measure food with a cup and limit treats.”

Common health problems in Labradors

  • Obesity: Very common. Extra weight damages joints and shortens life. Follow portion guidelines strictly.
  • Hip and elbow dysplasia: Joint problems that cause limping and pain. Keep your Labrador lean and give joint supplements as advised.
  • Ear infections: Floppy ears trap moisture. Clean ears weekly with a cotton ball (no Q‑tips).
  • Bloat (gastric torsion): Life‑threatening stomach twist. Feed two small meals a day, not one large meal. No exercise immediately after eating.
  • Skin allergies: Some Labradors get itchy skin. Use vet‑approved shampoos and avoid over‑bathing.

Grooming needs

  • Brush once a week with a rubber curry brush or deshedding tool – they shed heavily twice a year.
  • Bathe once a month or when dirty. Use a mild pet shampoo.
  • Clean ears weekly with a cotton ball (never inside the ear canal).
  • Trim nails every 3–4 weeks.

Exercise requirements

Labradors need at least 1 hour of exercise every day. Without it, they become bored and destructive (chewing, digging, barking).

  • Walks: 30–45 minutes twice a day (early morning and evening). Avoid walking in peak heat – use the paw test (Week 13).
  • Swimming: Labradors love water. A safe pond or pool is great exercise without joint stress.
  • Fetch and tug‑of‑war: Good for burning energy indoors or in a garden.
  • Mental games: Hide treats, use puzzle toys – a tired mind is as important as a tired body.

Feeding guidelines

  • Measure every meal with a standard cup. For an adult Labrador, 2–3 cups of good quality kibble per day (divided into two meals) is typical – adjust based on activity.
  • Avoid table scraps and high‑calorie treats. Use small pieces of carrot or green beans as low‑calorie treats.
  • Do not leave food out all day – Labradors will overeat.
  • Fresh, clean water must always be available.

Real story from our clinic

A 5‑year‑old Labrador, Simba, came to us weighing 42 kg – he should have been 30 kg. He could barely climb stairs and panted constantly. His owner was pouring kibble until the bowl “looked full”. We put Simba on a strict diet: 200 gm of food per day (divided into two meals), replaced treats with green beans, and added a 15‑minute slow walk. In 6 months, Simba dropped to 32 kg. He started running and jumping again. His owner now measures every meal and says, “I almost killed him with kindness.”

Should you get a Labrador?

Yes, if you:

  • Have time for daily exercise (1+ hours).
  • Can commit to measuring food and preventing obesity.
  • Want a friendly, family‑loving dog.
  • Can afford regular vet checkups and joint supplements as they age.

Maybe not, if you:

  • Live in a very hot city without air conditioning (they can overheat).
  • Work long hours and cannot give them attention.
  • Are not willing to control their diet – obesity will shorten their life.

Action for Week 3

  1. If you have a Labrador, weigh them today. Feel their ribs – can you feel them easily? If not, reduce food by 10%.
  2. Start measuring every meal with a standard cup – no guessing.
  3. Clean their ears this week – check for redness or bad smell.
  4. Schedule a joint health check if your Labrador is over 5 years old.
