🐕 Maltese – Small, White, Fluffy, but Prone to Dental and Eye Problems

The Maltese is a tiny, white, long‑haired dog with a gentle, playful personality. They are one of the oldest toy breeds, bred to be companions. They are affectionate, lively, and love to be with their family. At Dr. Deepa pet vet clinic, we see Maltese dogs that are wonderful lap dogs. But they have several health problems. Their small mouth leads to serious dental disease – teeth can fall out if not brushed. Their eyes produce tears that stain the white fur. They have fragile bones (can break a leg from jumping off a sofa). They also have a risk of a liver problem called liver shunt. They need daily grooming and regular vet checks.

🐾 Key things to remember: Maltese have very bad teeth – brush daily and get yearly dental cleanings. Their eyes tear constantly – clean under eyes daily to prevent staining. They are fragile – use ramps, do not let them jump off furniture. Their white coat needs daily brushing to prevent mats.

What does a Maltese look like?

  • Size: Toy – 2–4 kg. Height 20–25 cm.
  • Coat: Long, silky, pure white – falls straight to the floor. No undercoat.
  • Build: Small, compact, with a slightly rounded head, floppy ears, and a tail that curves over the back.

Why Maltese are popular in India

  • Very small size: Perfect for apartments and small homes.
  • Affectionate and gentle: They love to cuddle and are great lap dogs.
  • Good for first‑time owners: They are easy to handle and not aggressive.
  • Long lifespan: Often live 12–15 years.
🦷 Dr. Deepa’s warning: “Maltese have terrible dental problems. By age 3, many have tartar, red gums, and loose teeth. You must brush their teeth DAILY with dog‑safe toothpaste. Yearly professional cleaning is also needed. Without care, they will lose teeth and develop painful gum infections.”

Common health problems in Maltese

  • Dental disease (periodontitis): Very common. Signs: bad breath, red gums, loose teeth, difficulty eating. Brush daily and get yearly dental cleanings.
  • Eye problems (tear staining): Tears overflow and stain the white fur brown. Clean under eyes daily with a soft, damp cloth. Also prone to dry eye and cataracts.
  • Patellar luxation (loose kneecaps): Can cause limping or skipping. Keep them lean.
  • Liver shunt (portosystemic shunt): A birth defect where blood bypasses the liver. Signs: stunted growth, seizures, lethargy after eating. Requires surgery. Responsible breeders screen for this.
  • Fragile bones: They can break a leg from jumping off a sofa or bed. Use ramps, do not let them jump.

Grooming – high maintenance

  • Brush daily: Use a soft slicker brush and steel comb. Their silky coat mats easily – pay attention to behind ears, armpits, and belly. Many owners keep the coat trimmed short (“puppy cut”) for easier care.
  • Bathe every 2–3 weeks: Use a whitening shampoo for white coats. Dry thoroughly.
  • Eye cleaning – daily: Wipe under eyes with a soft, damp cloth to prevent brown staining.
  • Ear cleaning – weekly: Use a cotton ball – never Q‑tips.
  • Trim nails every 2–3 weeks.
  • Brush teeth daily – very important.

Exercise and environment

  • Walks: 15–20 minutes twice a day. Avoid peak heat – use the paw test (Week 13).
  • Indoor play: They are low‑energy – short play with soft toys is enough.
  • Fragile – no jumping: Use ramps for sofas and beds. Do not let them jump off furniture.
  • Secure home: They are tiny and can escape through small gaps – ensure doors and windows are secure.

Training and behaviour

  • Maltese are smart and eager to please – easy to train with positive reinforcement.
  • House‑training can be difficult – be patient and consistent.
  • They can develop separation anxiety – do not leave them alone for very long hours.
  • They bark at strangers – good watchdogs.

Feeding guidelines

  • High‑quality small breed dog food – small kibble size.
  • For an adult Maltese, 1/4 to 1/2 cup of dry food per day (divided into two meals). Adjust based on activity.
  • Keep them lean – obesity worsens joint and liver problems.
  • Always fresh, clean water.

Real story from our clinic

A 4‑year‑old Maltese named “Snowy” came in with bad breath and difficulty eating. His gums were red and swollen, and several teeth were loose. He needed a professional dental cleaning and three extractions. His owner had never brushed his teeth. After the procedure, Snowy felt much better. The owner now brushes Snowy’s teeth every night. “I didn’t know small dogs have such bad teeth,” she said. “Now it’s part of our bedtime routine.”

Should you get a Maltese?

Yes, if you:

  • Have time for daily brushing and dental care.
  • Can clean under eyes daily to prevent staining.
  • Are prepared for yearly dental cleanings and potential health costs.
  • Want a tiny, affectionate, gentle companion.

Maybe not, if you:

  • Cannot commit to daily tooth brushing – dental disease will be severe.
  • Have young children who may handle the dog roughly – Maltese are fragile.
  • Have a home with high furniture and no ramps – they can break bones jumping.
  • Want a low‑grooming, independent dog (get an Indie dog).

Action for Week 31

  1. If you have a Maltese, check their teeth today – any bad breath, red gums, or loose teeth? Schedule a dental check.
  2. Start a daily tooth‑brushing routine – use dog‑safe toothpaste.
  3. Check their eyes – any brown staining? Start daily wiping with a damp cloth.
  4. If you are thinking of getting a Maltese, ask the breeder about liver shunt screening in the parents.
