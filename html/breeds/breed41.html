🐕 Mudhol Hound (Caravan Hound) – Indian Sighthound, Prone to Eye Issues and Needs Space

The Mudhol Hound, also called the Caravan Hound, is an Indian sighthound from Karnataka. They are tall, lean, and built for speed – used for hunting and guarding. They are loyal to their family but can be aloof with strangers. At Dr. Deepa pet vet clinic, we see Mudhol Hounds that are healthy, hardy, and well‑suited to Indian climates. However, they need a lot of space to run, a high prey drive (they will chase small animals), and are prone to certain eye disorders. They are not for apartments or first‑time owners.

🐾 Key things to remember: Mudhol Hounds need a large, secure yard – they can jump high. They need daily running, not just walks. They are prone to progressive retinal atrophy (PRA) – yearly eye checks are important. They have a high prey drive – not safe with cats or small pets.

What does a Mudhol Hound look like?

  • Size: Large – 20–30 kg. Height 65–75 cm.
  • Coat: Short, smooth – colours: fawn, brindle, cream, black, or brown. Some have white markings.
  • Build: Lean, deep chest, long legs, curved tail, and a long, narrow head with semi‑pricked ears.

Why Mudhol Hounds are special – and challenging

  • Native Indian breed: They are well‑adapted to heat and have few genetic problems.
  • Loyal and protective: They bond strongly with family and are excellent guard dogs.
  • Very fast: They need to run – a leash walk is not enough.
  • High prey drive: They will chase anything that moves – cats, squirrels, rabbits, small dogs.
👁️ Dr. Deepa’s warning: “Mudhol Hounds are prone to progressive retinal atrophy (PRA) – an eye disease that causes blindness. There is no cure, but it is not painful. Yearly eye exams can detect it early. Also, never let them off‑leash outside a secure yard – they will chase and may not return.”

Common health problems in Mudhol Hounds

  • Progressive retinal atrophy (PRA): Inherited eye disease leading to blindness. Yearly eye exams recommended.
  • Bloat (gastric torsion): Deep chest increases risk – feed two small meals daily, no exercise after eating.
  • Hip dysplasia: Less common than in other large breeds, but possible – keep them lean.
  • Skin allergies: Short coat means less protection – use vet‑approved shampoos.

Grooming – very easy

  • Brush once a week with a rubber curry brush – removes loose hair and spreads skin oils.
  • Bathe once a month or when dirty – they are clean dogs.
  • Clean ears weekly – their semi‑pricked ears are less prone to infection.
  • Trim nails every 3–4 weeks – they wear down less if they run on soft ground.
  • Brush teeth weekly.

Exercise and environment – very important

  • Running space: A large, securely fenced yard (minimum 6‑foot height) is essential. They need to run free daily – not just walks.
  • Exercise: 1+ hour of running or vigorous play daily. They excel at lure coursing (chasing a fake prey).
  • Leash walking: Always on a leash outside a secure yard – they will chase anything that moves.
  • Not for apartments: They need space – small homes or apartments will frustrate them.

Training and behaviour

  • Mudhol Hounds are intelligent but independent – training requires patience and consistency.
  • Use positive reinforcement – they do not respond well to harsh methods.
  • Early socialisation is essential – they can be wary of strangers and aggressive with strange dogs.
  • They are not recommended for homes with cats, rabbits, or small dogs – their prey drive is very high.

Feeding guidelines

  • High‑quality large breed dog food – they need good nutrition for their energy levels.
  • For an adult Mudhol Hound, 3–5 cups of dry food per day (divided into two meals). Adjust based on activity.
  • Keep them lean – obesity stresses joints and heart.
  • Always fresh, clean water – they drink a lot after running.

Real story from our clinic

A Mudhol Hound named “Karna” was brought in for bumping into furniture and acting confused. An eye exam showed advanced progressive retinal atrophy – he was going blind. His owner was devastated, but we explained that blind dogs can live happily if the home is kept the same. The owner rearranged nothing and used scent markers (different rugs) to help Karna navigate. Karna adapted well and still enjoys running in the secure yard. His owner said, “I wish I had known about eye testing earlier.”

Should you get a Mudhol Hound?

Yes, if you:

  • Have a large, securely fenced yard (minimum 6‑foot fence).
  • Have time for daily running and exercise (1+ hour).
  • Are an experienced dog owner – not your first dog.
  • Want a rare, loyal, and healthy Indian breed.

Maybe not, if you:

  • Live in an apartment or small home – they need space.
  • Have small pets (cats, rabbits) – they will chase.
  • Cannot provide a very high, secure fence – they can jump over normal fences.
  • Want a dog that loves all strangers – Mudhol Hounds are protective.

Action for Week 41

  1. If you have a Mudhol Hound, schedule a yearly eye exam (OFA or CERF) to check for PRA.
  2. Check your fencing – can they jump over? Reinforce to at least 6 feet.
  3. Increase daily running time – a tired Mudhol Hound is a well‑behaved one.
  4. If you are thinking of getting a Mudhol Hound, adopt from a rescue or contact a responsible breeder who tests for PRA.
