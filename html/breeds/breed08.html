Week 8 • Cat Breed

🐆 Bengal Cat – High Energy, Loves Water, Needs Mental Stimulation

The Bengal cat looks like a tiny leopard. Their spotted or marbled coat is stunning. They are also very active, very smart, and very curious. At Dr. Deepa pet vet clinic, we see Bengals that are full of energy – they love to climb, play fetch, and even play with water. They are not lap cats. They need a lot of attention and space. If you are looking for a quiet, lazy cat, a Bengal is not for you.

🐾 Key things to remember: Bengals are very active – they need 1–2 hours of play daily. They love water – don’t be surprised if they dip their paws in your glass. They can jump very high, so you need a tall cat tree or shelves.

What does a Bengal cat look like?

  • Size: Medium‑large – 4–8 kg. Males are bigger than females.
  • Coat: Short, dense, and very soft – feels like silk. Low grooming needs.
  • Patterns: Spotted or marbled – like a wild leopard. Colours: brown, silver, snow (white).
  • Build: Muscular, long body, strong legs. They look like a small wild cat.
  • Eyes: Large, round, often gold or green.

Why Bengals are special – and challenging

  • Very active: They need at least 1 hour of active play every day. Without it, they will climb curtains, open cupboards, and knock things over.
  • Love water: Unlike most cats, Bengals often love playing with water – dripping taps, water bowls, even showers.
  • Highly intelligent: They can learn to open doors, fetch toys, and solve puzzles. You need to keep their mind busy.
  • Social: They bond with their family but may not like strangers. They do well with other active pets.
💧 Dr. Deepa’s tip: “If you have a Bengal, put a heavy water bowl that cannot tip over. They will splash. Also, keep toilet lids closed – they may play in the toilet water.”

Common health problems in Bengals

  • Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM): A heart disease common in Bengals. Regular heart checkups and ultrasound screening are important.
  • Progressive retinal atrophy (PRA): An eye disease that can cause blindness. Responsible breeders test for this.
  • Patellar luxation: Loose kneecaps – can cause limping. Keep them at a healthy weight.
  • Dental disease: Brush their teeth weekly.
  • Pica (eating non‑food items): Some Bengals eat fabric, plastic, or string – dangerous. Keep small objects away.

Grooming – very easy

  • Brush once a week with a soft brush or rubber grooming glove – removes loose hair and distributes oils.
  • Bathe rarely – Bengals are very clean. Only if they get into something messy.
  • Clean ears weekly with a cotton ball (never inside the ear canal).
  • Trim nails every 2–3 weeks – their nails are sharp and they love to climb.
  • Brush teeth weekly – Bengals can get dental tartar.

Exercise and mental stimulation – very important

A bored Bengal will destroy your home. You must provide:

  • Active play: Feather wands, fetch, laser pointers (end with a physical toy). 15–20 minutes, 3–4 times a day.
  • Climbing space: Tall cat trees (at least 6 feet), wall shelves, or a cat wheel.
  • Puzzle toys: Hide treats inside – they love solving problems.
  • Water play: Some Bengals love a shallow pan of water with floating toys.
  • Leash training: Bengals can be walked on a harness – great for extra exercise.

Feeding guidelines

  • High‑protein, grain‑free cat food – Bengals need good nutrition for their energy.
  • For an adult Bengal, 1/2 to 3/4 cup of dry food per day (divided into two meals) plus wet food.
  • Avoid overfeeding – Bengals can become overweight if not exercised enough.
  • Always fresh water – but expect splashing.

Real story from our clinic

A family adopted a Bengal cat named “Leo”. They loved his energy but could not keep up. Leo climbed their curtains, opened kitchen cabinets, and even learned to turn on the tap. They brought him to us for advice. We recommended a tall cat tree, puzzle feeders, and daily play sessions with a feather wand. They also started walking Leo on a harness in their garden. Within two weeks, the destructive behaviour stopped. The owner said, “We didn’t know Bengals needed so much. Now we play with him for an hour every evening, and he is a different cat.”

Should you get a Bengal cat?

Yes, if you:

  • Have time for 1–2 hours of active play daily.
  • Have a large, cat‑proofed home (no delicate items on shelves).
  • Can provide climbing space – tall cat trees or shelves.
  • Enjoy an active, playful, and very smart cat.

Maybe not, if you:

  • Work long hours and cannot give attention – they will get destructive.
  • Live in a small apartment without climbing space.
  • Want a calm, quiet, lap cat (get a Persian or Indie cat instead).

Action for Week 8

  1. If you have a Bengal, assess their activity – are they getting enough play? Increase playtime this week.
  2. Check your home for things they can knock over – secure fragile items.
  3. Buy a puzzle feeder or make one from a cardboard box – mental stimulation is key.
  4. If you are thinking of getting a Bengal, visit a rescue – there are many Bengal mixes needing homes.
