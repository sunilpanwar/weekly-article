🐕 Dalmatian – Spotted Beauty, Prone to Urinary Stones and Deafness

The Dalmatian is famous for its unique black or brown spots on a white coat. They are energetic, intelligent, and love to run. At Dr. Deepa pet vet clinic, we see Dalmatians that are loyal and good with active families. However, they have some serious health problems. They are prone to urinary stones (bladder stones) because of how their bodies process protein. They also have a high rate of deafness – up to 30% of Dalmatians are deaf in one or both ears. They need a special low‑purine diet and daily exercise. They are not for first‑time owners or sedentary families.

🐾 Key things to remember: Dalmatians need a low‑purine diet to prevent bladder stones – avoid organ meats, red meat, and some fish. Test their hearing as a puppy – deaf Dalmatians need special care. They need lots of exercise – a bored Dalmatian can be destructive.

What does a Dalmatian look like?

  • Size: Medium to large – 20–27 kg. Height 50–60 cm.
  • Coat: Short, dense, glossy – white with black or brown spots. Low grooming.
  • Build: Lean, muscular, deep chest, long legs, and a long tail.

Why Dalmatians are popular – and challenging

  • Striking appearance: Their spotted coat makes them stand out.
  • Energetic: They were bred to run alongside horse‑drawn carriages – they need lots of exercise.
  • Intelligent and loyal: They bond strongly with their family.
  • Low grooming: Short coat needs only weekly brushing.
🩺 Dr. Deepa’s warning: “Dalmatians have a unique urinary system – they produce high levels of uric acid, which can form bladder stones. Feed a low‑purine diet (special Dalmatian food or a vet‑approved homemade diet). Also, up to 30% are deaf – test hearing early. A deaf Dalmatian can still be a great pet but needs hand signals and a secure yard.”

Common health problems in Dalmatians

  • Bladder stones (urolithiasis): Very common. Signs: blood in urine, straining to pee, frequent small amounts. Emergency if unable to pee. Feed low‑purine diet and provide plenty of water.
  • Deafness (congenital): Common in white‑coated dogs. BAER test can diagnose. Deaf dogs need visual cues, secure fencing, and never off‑leash.
  • Hip dysplasia: Keep them lean.
  • Skin allergies: Some Dalmatians have sensitive skin – avoid harsh shampoos.

Grooming – easy

  • Brush once a week with a rubber curry brush – removes loose hair. They shed heavily year‑round.
  • Bathe once a month or when dirty – use a mild dog shampoo.
  • Clean ears weekly – their erect ears are less prone to infection.
  • Trim nails every 3–4 weeks. Brush teeth weekly.

Exercise – very important

  • Exercise needs: High – at least 1 hour of vigorous exercise daily. They love running, fetch, and long walks.
  • Secure yard: They need a securely fenced yard – they can jump and dig.
  • Mental stimulation: Puzzle toys, training sessions – they are smart and get bored easily.

Training and behaviour

  • Dalmatians are intelligent but can be stubborn. Use positive reinforcement (treats, praise).
  • They need early socialisation – they can be wary of strangers.
  • They have a high prey drive – may chase small animals.
  • They can be destructive if bored or under‑exercised.

Feeding guidelines – very important

  • Low‑purine diet: Avoid organ meats (liver, kidney), red meat, sardines, anchovies, and some beans. Feed a commercial low‑purine diet or a vet‑approved homemade diet.
  • For an adult Dalmatian, 2–3 cups of low‑purine dry food per day (divided into two meals). Adjust based on activity.
  • Encourage water drinking: Add water to dry food, use a water fountain – dilute urine prevents stones.
  • Always fresh, clean water.

Real story from our clinic

A Dalmatian named “Spot” came in with bloody urine and straining. X‑rays showed a large bladder stone. He needed surgery to remove it. His owner had been feeding him a normal diet with red meat. After the surgery, we put him on a low‑purine diet and encouraged water intake. Spot has not had another stone. His owner says, “I had no idea Dalmatians need a special diet. Now I am very careful.”

Should you get a Dalmatian?

Yes, if you:

  • Have time for daily vigorous exercise (1+ hour).
  • Can commit to a low‑purine diet and encourage water drinking.
  • Are prepared for potential deafness and can adapt training.
  • Have a securely fenced yard.

Maybe not, if you:

  • Live in an apartment without a yard – they need space to run.
  • Are a first‑time owner – they need experienced handling.
  • Cannot afford a special diet or potential bladder stone surgery.
  • Have a sedentary lifestyle – they will become destructive.

Action for Week 45

  1. If you have a Dalmatian, have their hearing tested (BAER test) – even as an adult.
  2. Switch to a low‑purine diet – ask us for recommendations.
  3. Encourage water intake – add water to food, use a fountain.
  4. If you are thinking of getting a Dalmatian, adopt from a rescue or ask the breeder about BAER testing and stone history in the parents.
