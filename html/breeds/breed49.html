Week 49 • Dog Breed

🐕 Newfoundland – Giant, Water‑Loving, but Prone to Heatstroke and Heart Disease

The Newfoundland is a giant, powerful dog with a thick, water‑repellent coat and webbed feet. They are famously good swimmers – they have saved many people from drowning. At Dr. Deepa pet vet clinic, we see Newfoundlands that are gentle, patient, and wonderful with families. However, they have serious health problems. Their thick coat makes them very prone to heatstroke in Indian summers. They also have a high risk of heart disease (subvalvular aortic stenosis) and joint problems. They drool a lot and shed heavily. They need a lot of space and are not for hot climates.

🐾 Key things to remember: Newfoundlands overheat easily – they need air conditioning in Indian summers. They are prone to heart disease – yearly heart checks are essential. They drool constantly – be prepared for slime. They need space – not for apartments.

What does a Newfoundland look like?

  • Size: Giant – 50–70 kg. Height 65–75 cm.
  • Coat: Thick, long, double coat – colours: black, brown, grey, or black and white (Landseer).
  • Build: Massive, muscular, with a broad head, deep chest, webbed feet, and a thick tail.

Why Newfoundlands are special – and challenging in India

  • Gentle temperament: They are calm, patient, and great with children – called “nanny dogs”.
  • Natural swimmers: They love water and are excellent rescue dogs.
  • Very heat sensitive: Their thick coat is built for cold water – they overheat quickly in Indian weather.
  • High drool and shedding: They drool constantly (especially after drinking) and shed heavily.
❤️ Dr. Deepa’s warning: “Newfoundlands have a high risk of subvalvular aortic stenosis (SAS) – a heart defect that can cause sudden death. Yearly heart screenings (echocardiogram) are essential. Also, they cannot tolerate Indian heat – they need air conditioning 24/7.”

Common health problems in Newfoundlands

  • Subvalvular aortic stenosis (SAS): Heart disease – can cause fainting or sudden death. Yearly heart checks.
  • Heatstroke: Very high risk. Signs: heavy panting, dark red tongue, collapse – emergency!
  • Hip and elbow dysplasia: Keep them lean.
  • Cystinuria (bladder stones): Genetic condition – feed a special diet and provide plenty of water.
  • Bloat (gastric torsion): Deep chest increases risk – feed two small meals daily, no exercise after eating.

Grooming – high maintenance

  • Brush 2–3 times a week: Use a slicker brush and undercoat rake. During shedding season (spring, autumn), brush daily.
  • Bathe once a month or when dirty – use a mild dog shampoo. Dry thoroughly – damp fur mats.
  • Drool management: Keep a towel handy – wipe their mouth and furniture regularly.
  • Clean ears weekly – their floppy ears trap moisture.
  • Trim nails every 3–4 weeks. Brush teeth weekly.

Exercise and environment

  • Exercise: Moderate – 30–45 minutes twice a day. Avoid peak heat – walk only before 7 AM and after 7 PM.
  • Swimming: Excellent low‑impact exercise – they love it. Ensure safe water access.
  • Space: They need a house with a large yard – not for apartments.
  • Air conditioning: Essential in Indian summer – they cannot cool themselves.

Training and behaviour

  • Newfoundlands are intelligent and eager to please – easy to train with positive reinforcement.
  • Early socialisation is important – they are naturally friendly but can be shy without exposure.
  • They are sensitive dogs – harsh training will break their spirit.
  • They can develop separation anxiety – do not leave them alone for long hours.

Feeding guidelines

  • High‑quality large breed dog food – good for joint and heart health.
  • For an adult Newfoundland, 4–6 cups of dry food per day (divided into two meals). Adjust based on activity.
  • Keep them lean – obesity worsens joint and heart problems.
  • Always fresh, cool water – encourage drinking to prevent bladder stones.

Real story from our clinic

A Newfoundland named “Bear” was brought in after collapsing during a morning walk at 8 AM. His temperature was 106°F – severe heatstroke. We cooled him with wet towels and IV fluids. He survived but had permanent kidney damage. His owner now walks him only at 6 AM and keeps him in air conditioning all day. “I had no idea they overheat so easily,” he said. “Now we are very careful.”

Should you get a Newfoundland in India?

Only consider if you:

  • Live in a cool climate (hill station) or have 24/7 air conditioning.
  • Have a large, securely fenced yard and access to safe water for swimming.
  • Are prepared for high grooming, heavy shedding, and constant drool.
  • Can afford yearly heart screenings and potential health costs.

Otherwise, choose a breed better suited for India – like an Indie dog.

Action for Week 49

  1. If you have a Newfoundland, schedule a heart screening (echocardiogram) – even if they seem healthy.
  2. Ensure air conditioning is available during summer – never leave them in a hot room.
  3. Provide a large water bowl and encourage drinking – helps prevent bladder stones.
  4. If you are thinking of getting a Newfoundland, consider adopting a rescue or choose a breed better suited for India.
