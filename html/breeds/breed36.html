Week 36 • Cat Breed

🐆 Savannah Cat – Hybrid Wild Look, Very Active, High Escape Risk

The Savannah cat is a hybrid breed – a cross between a domestic cat and the wild African Serval. They look like a small wild cat – tall, long legs, large ears, and a spotted coat. They are very active, intelligent, and curious. At Dr. Deepa pet vet clinic, we see Savannah cats that are extremely energetic and need a lot of space. They are not for first‑time cat owners. They have special needs – they love to climb and can jump very high. They are escape artists – you need a secure, tall enclosure. Their diet is also different – they need high‑protein food. Some places have legal restrictions on owning hybrid cats – check your local laws.

🐾 Key things to remember: Savannahs are very active – they need daily play, climbing space, and mental stimulation. They can jump up to 2 metres high – secure your home. They are escape artists – never let them outside unsupervised. They need a high‑protein, grain‑free diet.

What does a Savannah cat look like?

  • Size: Medium to large – 4–9 kg (F1 and F2 can be larger). Height can reach 40–45 cm.
  • Coat: Short, with bold spots – similar to a Serval. Colours: brown spotted, silver spotted, black.
  • Build: Tall, long legs, large ears, long neck, and a wedge‑shaped head.

Why Savannah cats are challenging

  • Very high energy: They need hours of play and climbing – not for lazy owners.
  • Escape artists: They can open doors, jump high fences, and squeeze through small gaps. You need a secure, tall enclosure.
  • Legal restrictions: Some states and cities restrict or ban ownership of hybrid cats (especially early generations F1–F3). Check your local laws.
  • Cost: They are expensive to buy and maintain – high food costs, secure housing, and vet care.
🚪 Dr. Deepa’s warning: “Savannahs are not indoor‑only cats in the usual sense – they need a large, secure outdoor enclosure (catio) or a very tall, escape‑proof fence. They can jump over 2 metres. If they escape, they may not return and can be killed by dogs or cars.”

Common health problems in Savannah cats

  • Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM): A heart disease common in many breeds – yearly heart screening recommended.
  • Dental disease: Brush teeth weekly.
  • Obesity: They are active but can overeat – measure portions.
  • Intestinal issues: Some Savannahs have sensitive stomachs – high‑protein, grain‑free diet helps.

Grooming – easy

  • Brush once a week with a soft rubber brush – removes loose hair.
  • Bathe rarely – they are clean.
  • Clean ears weekly – their large ears can trap dirt.
  • Trim nails every 2–3 weeks. Brush teeth weekly.

Exercise and environment – very important

  • Play: At least 1 hour of active play daily – feather wands, fetch, chase games, puzzle toys.
  • Climbing: Very tall cat trees (over 2 metres), wall shelves, or a cat wheel.
  • Secure outdoor area: A fully enclosed catio (outdoor cage) is ideal – never let them roam free.
  • Indoor enrichment: Rotate toys, hide treats, teach tricks – they are very smart and get bored easily.

Feeding guidelines

  • High‑protein, grain‑free cat food – wet food is good. Some owners feed raw (consult us first).
  • For an adult Savannah, 1/2 to 1 cup of dry food per day (divided into two meals) plus wet food. Adjust based on size and activity.
  • Keep them lean – obesity affects joints and heart.
  • Always fresh, clean water.

Real story from our clinic

A family bought an F2 Savannah kitten. They loved his wild look but did not prepare their home. He escaped through a second‑floor window and was lost for 3 days. He was found injured by a car. After treatment, the family built a large catio and reinforced all windows. “We didn’t know they could jump so high,” they said. “Now he is safe.”

Should you get a Savannah cat?

Yes, only if you:

  • Are an experienced cat owner – not your first cat.
  • Have a very secure, tall enclosure (catio) or a cat‑proofed yard.
  • Have time for 1+ hour of daily active play and enrichment.
  • Can afford a high‑protein diet and potential veterinary costs.

Maybe not, if you:

  • Live in a small apartment with no outdoor space – they need room to run and climb.
  • Are away from home long hours – they get bored and destructive.
  • Have small pets like birds or hamsters – their prey drive is high.
  • Live in an area where hybrid cats are restricted or banned.

Action for Week 36

  1. If you have a Savannah, check your fencing – can they jump over? Reinforce to at least 2 metres height.
  2. Provide a catio or a secure outdoor enclosure – never let them roam free.
  3. Increase daily play to at least 1 hour – a tired Savannah is a happy Savannah.
  4. If you are thinking of getting a Savannah, check local laws and consider adopting an Indie cat instead – they are easier and also wonderful.
