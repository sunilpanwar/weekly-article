🐕 Bulldog – Cute Wrinkly Face, But Struggles to Breathe and Overheats Easily

The Bulldog (English or French) is a small, muscular dog with a wrinkled face, flat nose, and a very sweet personality. They are calm, loyal, and great with families. At Dr. Deepa pet vet clinic, we see many Bulldogs because they are popular in India. But they have serious health problems because of their flat face (brachycephalic). They snore, wheeze, and can collapse in hot weather. Their skin folds get infected if not cleaned daily. They cannot give birth naturally – almost all need a C‑section. They also have joint problems. If you want a Bulldog, you must be ready for high vet bills and daily care.

🐾 Key things to remember: Bulldogs cannot tolerate heat – they need air conditioning in summer. Clean their face wrinkles daily with a damp cloth. They cannot swim – keep away from pools. Most need C‑sections to give birth – breeding is expensive and risky.

What does a Bulldog look like?

  • Size: Small to medium – English Bulldog 18–25 kg, French Bulldog 8–14 kg.
  • Coat: Short, smooth – easy to groom. Sheds moderately.
  • Colours: White, fawn, brindle, piebald, or combinations.
  • Build: Stocky, low to the ground, wide head, flat face (brachycephalic), wrinkled skin, and a short, straight or screwed tail.

Why Bulldogs are popular – but very high maintenance

  • Sweet temperament: They are gentle, patient, and great with children.
  • Low exercise needs: Short walks are enough – good for apartments.
  • Quiet: They don’t bark much.
🌡️ Dr. Deepa’s warning: “Bulldogs are very sensitive to heat. Never walk them in the sun – even 10 minutes can cause heatstroke. They must live in air conditioning in Indian summers. Also, their flat face means they snore and may struggle to breathe during exercise or sleep. Some need surgery to open their airways.”

Common health problems in Bulldogs

  • Brachycephalic airway syndrome: Flat face causes narrow nostrils, long soft palate, and small windpipe. Signs: noisy breathing, snoring, gagging, collapsing in heat. Surgery can help severe cases.
  • Heatstroke: Very high risk. Never leave in a hot car, never walk in sun. Signs: heavy panting, dark red tongue, collapse – emergency!
  • Skin fold infections (pyoderma): Moisture and dirt trap in face wrinkles, tail folds. Clean daily with a damp cloth and dry thoroughly.
  • Eye problems: Cherry eye (prolapsed third eyelid), dry eye, corneal ulcers. Keep eyes lubricated if dry.
  • Hip and elbow dysplasia: Common – keep them lean.
  • Bloat (gastric torsion): Feed two small meals daily, no exercise after eating.
  • Birthing problems (dystocia): Puppies have large heads and cannot pass through the birth canal. Most Bulldog births require C‑sections.

Grooming – daily face cleaning is essential

  • Face wrinkle cleaning – every day: Use a soft, damp cloth to wipe inside each wrinkle. Then dry with a dry cloth. Never leave moisture in wrinkles.
  • Tail fold cleaning – weekly: If your Bulldog has a screw tail, clean under the tail fold.
  • Brush once a week with a rubber curry brush – removes loose hair.
  • Bathe once a month – dry thoroughly, especially in wrinkles.
  • Clean ears weekly – their small ears can get dirty.
  • Trim nails every 3–4 weeks. Brush teeth weekly.

Exercise and environment

  • Walks: Only early morning (before 7 AM) or late evening (after 7 PM). Keep walks to 10–15 minutes in summer. Watch for panting – stop if they struggle.
  • Avoid heat: Never walk when the sun is up. Use the paw test (Week 13) – if road is hot, stay inside.
  • Air conditioning: Essential in Indian summer – they cannot cool themselves well.
  • No swimming: Bulldogs are top‑heavy and cannot swim – keep away from pools.

Training and behaviour

  • Bulldogs are smart but stubborn. Use positive reinforcement (treats, praise).
  • They are sensitive to heat and will tire quickly – do not push them.
  • They are good with children and other pets.

Feeding guidelines

  • High‑quality dog food – they need good nutrition but are prone to obesity.
  • For an adult Bulldog, 1–2 cups of dry food per day (divided into two meals). Adjust based on size and activity.
  • Do not overfeed – obesity worsens breathing and joint problems.
  • Use a slow feeder bowl – they eat very fast and can choke or bloat.
  • Always fresh, cool water.

Real story from our clinic

A French Bulldog named “Oscar” was brought in after collapsing during a 10‑minute morning walk at 8 AM. His tongue was dark purple, and he had a fever of 107°F. We cooled him with wet towels and gave oxygen. He survived but had permanent lung damage. His owner now walks him only at 6 AM and keeps him in air conditioning all day. “I almost lost him because I didn’t understand how sensitive they are,” he said.

Should you get a Bulldog?

Yes, only if you:

  • Have air conditioning in summer – non‑negotiable.
  • Can commit to daily face wrinkle cleaning.
  • Are prepared for potential high vet bills (breathing surgery, C‑sections, skin infections).
  • Live in a cool climate or are willing to walk only before sunrise.

Maybe not, if you:

  • Live in a very hot city without AC – they will suffer.
  • Cannot clean wrinkles daily – infections will happen.
  • Are not prepared for expensive medical care.
  • Want an active, outdoorsy dog – Bulldogs are low‑energy but heat‑sensitive.

Action for Week 35

  1. If you have a Bulldog, check their face wrinkles today – are they red, smelly, or oozing? Clean them.
  2. Feel their nose – are their nostrils tiny? If they struggle to breathe even at rest, discuss surgery with us.
  3. Check your walking schedule – are you walking them after sunrise? Switch to 6 AM.
  4. If you are thinking of getting a Bulldog, adopt from a rescue – many need homes due to health issues. And ask the breeder about airway and hip screening.
