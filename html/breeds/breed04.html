Week 4 • Cat Breed

🐈 Persian Cat – Beautiful but Needs Daily Grooming and Eye Care

The Persian cat is one of the most beautiful and popular cat breeds in India. With their long, silky coat and flat, sweet face, they look like living teddy bears. But they are also one of the highest‑maintenance breeds. At Dr. Deepa pet vet clinic, we see many Persians with eye problems, breathing issues, and matted fur. If you want a Persian, you must be ready to groom them every single day and clean their eyes daily.

🐾 Key things to remember: Persians need daily brushing to prevent painful mats. Their flat face causes tearing and breathing problems. They are indoor cats – never leave them outside.

What does a Persian cat look like?

  • Size: Medium – 3–5 kg. Stocky, not lean.
  • Coat: Very long, thick, and silky. Needs daily brushing.
  • Face: Flat (brachycephalic) – small nose, large round eyes, small ears.
  • Colours: White, black, blue, cream, silver, golden, and many more.

Why Persians are popular in India

  • Beautiful appearance: Their luxurious coat and sweet face attract many people.
  • Calm temperament: They are quiet, gentle, and not very active – good for apartments.
  • Affectionate: They love to sit near you and be petted.
🧼 Dr. Deepa’s warning: “Persians are not low‑maintenance cats. If you cannot brush them daily and clean their eyes every morning, their coat will mat painfully and their eyes will get infected. Think carefully before getting one.”

Common health problems in Persians

  • Eye problems (excessive tearing): Their flat face means tear ducts don’t drain well. You must wipe their eyes daily with a soft, damp cloth. Staining and infections are common.
  • Breathing issues: Brachycephalic airway syndrome – they snore, wheeze, and overheat easily. Never leave them in a hot room or car.
  • Polycystic kidney disease (PKD): A genetic disease that causes kidney failure. Reputable breeders test for this. Adopted Persians should be screened.
  • Dental disease: Flat face = crowded teeth. Brush their teeth or get yearly dental cleanings.
  • Skin infections: Mats pull on the skin, causing pain and infection. Daily brushing prevents this.

Grooming – very high maintenance

  • Daily brushing: Use a steel comb and a slicker brush. Work through the coat in sections. If you skip even a day, mats will form.
  • Bathing: Once a month with a cat‑safe shampoo. Dry thoroughly – damp fur mats faster.
  • Eye cleaning: Every morning, wipe the area under the eyes with a soft, damp cloth to remove discharge.
  • Ear cleaning: Weekly with a cotton ball – never Q‑tips inside.
  • Nail trimming: Every 2–3 weeks.

If you cannot commit to daily grooming, consider a shorthair breed like the Indie cat or British Shorthair.

Exercise and play

Persians are low‑energy cats. They prefer lounging to running. But they still need some play:

  • 10–15 minutes of play with a feather wand or toy mouse, twice a day.
  • Cat trees or shelves – they like to be up high, even if they don’t jump much.
  • Puzzle feeders – prevents boredom and overeating.

Feeding guidelines

  • High‑quality cat food – wet food is good because it adds water and helps prevent kidney disease.
  • Measure portions – Persians easily become obese. For an adult Persian, 1/3 to 1/2 cup of dry food per day (divided into two meals) plus wet food.
  • Always fresh water. Their flat face makes drinking from deep bowls hard – use a shallow, wide bowl.

Real story from our clinic

A family bought a Persian kitten from a pet shop. They loved him but did not know about daily grooming. After a month, his coat was full of painful mats. They brought him to us with red, weeping eyes and a skin infection. We had to shave his entire coat (which he hated) and treat the infection. We taught them daily brushing and eye cleaning. Now, the Persian is healthy and beautiful, but the family says, “We had no idea they needed so much work. We love him, but we will never get another Persian.”

Should you get a Persian cat?

Yes, if you:

  • Have time to brush them daily (10–15 minutes).
  • Can clean their eyes every morning.
  • Have air conditioning in summer (they overheat easily).
  • Can afford regular vet checkups for kidney screening and dental care.

Maybe not, if you:

  • Work long hours or travel often – they need daily grooming.
  • Live in a hot, humid city without AC.
  • Want a low‑maintenance, independent cat (get an Indie cat instead).

Action for Week 4

  1. If you have a Persian, check their coat today – any mats? If yes, carefully cut them out or bring them to us.
  2. Start a daily eye‑cleaning routine – a soft, damp cloth every morning.
  3. If you are thinking of getting a Persian, visit a rescue or a responsible breeder who tests for PKD.
  4. Schedule a kidney screening (ultrasound or DNA test) for your Persian – early detection saves lives.
