🐕 Saint Bernard – Gentle Giant, but Severe Heat Sensitivity and Bloat Risk

The Saint Bernard is a giant, powerful dog famous for rescue work in the Swiss Alps. They are gentle, patient, and wonderful with families – especially children. At Dr. Deepa pet vet clinic, we see Saint Bernards that are loving companions. However, they have serious health problems and are very difficult to keep in India. Their thick coat and large size make them extremely prone to heatstroke. They also have a high risk of bloat (stomach twist), joint problems, and heart disease. They drool constantly and shed heavily. They need a lot of space, air conditioning, and an experienced owner. They are not for first‑time owners or hot climates.

🐾 Key things to remember: Saint Bernards overheat very easily – they need 24/7 air conditioning in India. They are very prone to bloat – feed two small meals daily, no exercise after eating. They drool a lot – be prepared for slime. They need space – not for apartments.

What does a Saint Bernard look like?

  • Size: Giant – 50–90 kg. Height 65–80 cm.
  • Coat: Smooth or rough (long) – thick double coat. Colours: white with red, mahogany, or brindle patches.
  • Build: Massive, muscular, with a broad head, deep chest, droopy eyes, and a long tail.

Why Saint Bernards are special – and very challenging in India

  • Gentle temperament: They are calm, patient, and great with children – called “nanny dogs”.
  • Loyal and protective: They bond deeply with family.
  • Extreme heat sensitivity: Their thick coat is built for freezing mountains – they cannot tolerate Indian heat.
  • High drool and shedding: They drool constantly (especially after drinking) and shed heavily year‑round.
🌡️ Dr. Deepa’s warning: “Saint Bernards suffer greatly in Indian summers. Even with air conditioning, they can struggle. They must never be walked in the sun – even early morning can be too hot. Without AC, they will develop heatstroke very quickly.”

Common health problems in Saint Bernards

  • Heatstroke: Very high risk. Signs: heavy panting, dark red tongue, collapse – emergency!
  • Bloat (gastric torsion): Very common in deep‑chested giant breeds. Feed two small meals daily, no exercise after eating. Consider gastropexy surgery.
  • Hip and elbow dysplasia: Keep them lean.
  • Heart disease (dilated cardiomyopathy): Yearly heart checks recommended.
  • Eye problems: Entropion (eyelids roll inward), ectropion (eyelids sag), cherry eye.
  • Osteosarcoma (bone cancer): Any limp that doesn’t go away needs X‑rays.

Grooming – high maintenance

  • Brush 2–3 times a week: Use a slicker brush and undercoat rake. During shedding season (spring, autumn), brush daily.
  • Bathe once a month or when dirty – use a mild dog shampoo. Dry thoroughly – damp fur mats.
  • Drool management: Keep a towel handy – wipe their mouth and furniture regularly.
  • Clean ears weekly – their floppy ears trap moisture.
  • Trim nails every 3–4 weeks. Brush teeth weekly.

Exercise and environment

  • Exercise: Moderate – 20–30 minutes twice a day. Avoid peak heat – walk only before 7 AM and after 7 PM.
  • Space: They need a house with a large yard – not for apartments.
  • Air conditioning: Essential in Indian summer – they cannot cool themselves.
  • Cooling mats: Provide a cooling mat or tile floor for them to lie on.

Training and behaviour

  • Saint Bernards are intelligent but can be stubborn. Use positive reinforcement (treats, praise).
  • Early socialisation is important – they are naturally friendly but can be shy without exposure.
  • They are sensitive dogs – harsh training methods will break their spirit.
  • They can develop separation anxiety – do not leave them alone for long hours.

Feeding guidelines

  • High‑quality large breed dog food – good for joint and heart health.
  • For an adult Saint Bernard, 4–6 cups of dry food per day (divided into TWO or THREE small meals).
  • Do not feed one large meal – this increases bloat risk.
  • Restrict water for 1 hour after meals.
  • Keep them lean – obesity worsens joint and heart problems.

Real story from our clinic

A Saint Bernard named “Bernie” was brought in with a swollen belly and dry heaving – classic bloat. We rushed him to surgery. His stomach had twisted. We untwisted it and performed a gastropexy (tacking the stomach) to prevent recurrence. Bernie survived. His owner now feeds him two small meals a day and never exercises him after eating. “I had no idea giant breeds are so prone to bloat,” he said. “Now I am very careful.”

Should you get a Saint Bernard in India?

Only consider if you:

  • Live in a cool climate (hill station) or have 24/7 air conditioning.
  • Have a large, securely fenced yard.
  • Are prepared for high grooming, heavy shedding, and constant drool.
  • Can afford potential bloat surgery, heart checks, and joint care.

Otherwise, choose a breed better suited for India – like an Indie dog.

Action for Week 50

  1. If you have a Saint Bernard, ensure air conditioning is available – never leave them in a hot room.
  2. Feed two or three small meals daily – never one large meal.
  3. Discuss gastropexy (stomach tacking) with us – this surgery can prevent bloat.
  4. If you are thinking of getting a Saint Bernard, consider adopting a rescue or choose a breed better suited for India.
