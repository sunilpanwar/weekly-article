Week 26

💊 The Deworming Rule – Worms Love the Rainy Season

Monsoon creates the perfect conditions for intestinal worms – roundworms, hookworms, whipworms, and tapeworms thrive in wet soil and puddles. At Dr. Deepa pet vet clinic, we see a sharp rise in worm infestations during and after the rains. The good news: a simple, safe deworming tablet every 3 months (or monthly during monsoon) keeps your pet’s tummy clean and healthy.

💊 Common sense rule: Deworming isn’t optional – it’s essential. Puppies and kittens: every 2 weeks until 12 weeks old. Adults: every 3 months. During monsoon, many vets recommend monthly deworming.

Why worms are more common in monsoon

  • Wet soil: Worm eggs thrive in moist environments and can survive for months.
  • Puddles and standing water: Pets drink from or walk through contaminated water, ingesting eggs.
  • Increased stray animal activity: Strays carry worms and contaminate public areas.
  • Fleas: Tapeworms are transmitted by fleas, which also flourish in humid weather.

Types of worms and their signs

  • Roundworms: Pot‑belly appearance, vomiting, diarrhoea, rice‑like segments in stool. Puppies are most affected.
  • Hookworms: Dark, tarry stool (blood), anaemia (pale gums), weakness, weight loss.
  • Tapeworms: Rice‑like segments around the anus or in stool, scooting (dragging bottom on floor).
  • Whipworms: Chronic diarrhoea, straining, weight loss – harder to detect.
🩸 Dr. Deepa’s warning: “Hookworms suck blood from the intestinal wall. In puppies, a severe infestation can cause life‑threatening anaemia. Deworming is not a suggestion – it’s a necessity.”

Signs your pet may have worms

  • Visible worms or rice‑like segments in stool or around the anus.
  • Bloated, pot‑belly appearance (especially in puppies).
  • Weight loss despite normal appetite.
  • Dull, dry coat.
  • Vomiting or diarrhoea.
  • Scooting (dragging bottom on the ground).
  • Lethargy or weakness (from anaemia).

Recommended deworming schedule

  • Puppies and kittens (under 6 months): Every 2 weeks until 12 weeks old, then monthly until 6 months.
  • Adult pets (over 6 months): Every 3 months (four times a year).
  • During monsoon (June–September): Many vets recommend monthly deworming for all pets, regardless of age.
  • Pregnant or nursing mothers: Special schedule – consult us.

What deworming tablets are safe?

Only use broad‑spectrum dewormers prescribed by a veterinarian. Over‑the‑counter products may not cover all worm types, and incorrect dosing can be ineffective or dangerous. At our clinic, we recommend products that cover roundworms, hookworms, whipworms, and tapeworms – all in one tablet.

How to administer a deworming tablet

  • Hide in food: Wrap in a small piece of cheese, meat, or peanut butter (xylitol‑free).
  • Use a pill popper: A device that places the tablet far back on the tongue.
  • Manual method: Hold the upper jaw, tilt the head back, place the tablet at the back of the tongue, close the mouth, and blow on the nose (encourages swallowing).
  • Liquid or chewable options: Ask us – some pets prefer these.

What to expect after deworming

  • Dead worms in stool: You may see white, noodle‑like worms or rice‑like segments. This is normal and means the medication worked.
  • Mild side effects: Some pets experience soft stool, vomiting, or temporary lethargy. These usually resolve within 24 hours. If severe, call us.
  • Repeat dose: Some dewormers need a second dose after 2 weeks to catch newly hatched worms. Follow our instructions.

Real case from our clinic

A 4‑month‑old puppy, Chotu, was brought in with a bloated belly, diarrhoea, and weakness. He hadn’t been dewormed since adoption. A faecal test revealed a heavy roundworm infestation. We administered a deworming tablet and prescribed a second dose in 14 days. Within 48 hours, Chotu passed dozens of worms. His energy returned, and his belly shrank. His owner now marks the calendar for deworming every month. “I didn’t know worms could make a puppy so sick,” she said. “Now I never miss a dose.”

Prevention – beyond deworming tablets

  • Clean up faeces immediately – worm eggs are shed in stool and contaminate the environment.
  • Prevent hunting and scavenging – rodents and birds carry worms.
  • Control fleas – tapeworms come from fleas. Use year‑round flea prevention.
  • Provide clean drinking water – avoid puddles and stagnant water.
  • Wash bedding and toys regularly – hot water kills worm eggs.

Zoonotic risk – worms can infect humans

Some pet worms (roundworms, hookworms) can infect humans, especially children who play in contaminated soil or put hands in their mouths. Regular deworming protects your family too. Always wash hands after handling pets or cleaning up stool.

Action for Week 26

  1. Check your pet’s deworming record – when was the last dose? If it’s been more than 3 months, schedule a visit.
  2. During monsoon, ask us about monthly deworming – especially if your pet goes outdoors.
  3. Collect a fresh stool sample (within 4 hours) and bring it to the clinic for testing if you suspect worms.
  4. Mark your calendar for the next three deworming dates – consistency is key.
