🌧️ The Rain Prep – Checking Ears for a Yeasty Smell Before Monsoon

Monsoon humidity creates the perfect environment for ear infections. Yeast and bacteria thrive in warm, moist ear canals. At Dr. Deepa pet vet clinic, we see a flood of ear infection cases as soon as the rains begin – most of which could have been prevented with simple pre‑monsoon checks and weekly cleaning.

👂 Common sense rule: Smell your pet’s ears once a week. Healthy ears have no odour or a faint, clean smell. A bread‑like, musty, or sweet smell means yeast. A foul, fishy smell means bacteria. Both need attention.

Why monsoon is peak ear infection season

High humidity, rain, and pets shaking water into their ears create a damp environment. Dogs with floppy ears (Labradors, Cocker Spaniels, Beagles) are especially vulnerable because air doesn’t circulate. Cats can also get ear infections, though less frequently. The moisture, warmth, and darkness of the ear canal allow yeast (Malassezia) and bacteria to multiply rapidly.

Signs of an ear infection

  • Smell: Yeasty (bread, corn chips, musty) or bacterial (fishy, rotten).
  • Discharge: Brown, black, yellow, or green wax.
  • Redness and swelling inside the ear flap.
  • Head shaking or tilting – trying to dislodge fluid.
  • Scratching at ears or rubbing them on the floor.
  • Pain – yelping when you touch the ear.
  • Loss of balance (in severe cases affecting inner ear).
🍞 Dr. Deepa’s tip: “If you smell fresh bread or beer around your dog’s head, that’s yeast. Don’t wait for head shaking – start cleaning early. Once the infection takes hold, it’s much harder to treat.”

How to do a pre‑monsoon ear check (weekly)

  1. Smell the ear: Gently lift the ear flap and sniff. Any odour other than clean skin is suspicious.
  2. Look inside: Healthy ears are pale pink, dry, and have very little wax. Brown, black, or yellow discharge is abnormal.
  3. Feel the base: Gently massage the ear base (below the ear canal). If your pet flinches or pulls away, there may be pain.
  4. Watch for head shaking: Even occasional shaking after rain is a sign of irritation.

How to clean your pet’s ears safely (preventive care)

  • Use a vet‑approved ear cleaner (we can recommend one). Avoid alcohol, hydrogen peroxide, or vinegar – they irritate inflamed tissue.
  • Apply cleaner generously into the ear canal (follow product instructions).
  • Massage the ear base for 30 seconds – you’ll hear a squishing sound.
  • Let your pet shake their head – this brings debris to the outer ear.
  • Wipe the outer ear with a cotton ball or soft gauze. Never push anything into the ear canal (no Q‑tips, no rolled cotton).
  • Repeat weekly during monsoon, or more often if your pet swims or gets frequent baths.

Important: Do not clean if the ear is already red, painful, or has discharge – you may push infection deeper. Bring your pet to the clinic instead.

What NOT to do

  • Never use Q‑tips or cotton buds inside the ear canal – you’ll pack wax deeper and risk rupturing the eardrum.
  • Avoid home remedies like curd, oil, or vinegar – they alter pH and can worsen infections.
  • Do not use human ear drops – many contain ingredients toxic to pets.
  • Do not ignore a mild smell – it progresses quickly in monsoon.

Real case from our clinic

A 5‑year‑old Cocker Spaniel, Charlie, was brought in with a strong yeast smell and constant head shaking. The owner said, “It started two days ago after a walk in the rain.” When we examined Charlie’s ears, they were filled with dark brown, coffee‑ground‑like wax. He had a severe yeast infection. After a thorough cleaning under sedation (because it was too painful to do awake), we prescribed antifungal ear drops and a weekly cleaning routine. Charlie’s infection cleared in 10 days. His owner now cleans his ears every Sunday – “I never miss a week, especially before monsoon.”

Prevention – keeping ears dry in monsoon

  • After every rain walk, dry the outer ear with a soft towel. Gently wipe the ear flap and the opening of the canal.
  • Use a drying solution (vet‑approved) after swimming or bathing – it evaporates moisture.
  • Consider a snood or rain hood for very floppy‑eared dogs during heavy rain.
  • Keep ears well‑ventilated – tie back long ear hair if needed (ask us how).
  • Treat underlying allergies – allergic pets produce more wax, which traps moisture.

Breeds most prone to ear infections

  • Cocker Spaniels, Springer Spaniels
  • Labrador Retrievers
  • Golden Retrievers
  • Beagles
  • Basset Hounds
  • Indies with floppy ears or narrow ear canals
  • Cats with flat faces (Persians, Himalayans) – though less common

When to visit the clinic

  • Any discharge, redness, or swelling.
  • Your pet yelps or pulls away when you touch the ear.
  • Head shaking is constant or violent.
  • You see blood or pus.
  • Your pet is tilting their head or walking in circles (inner ear involvement).

Action for Week 23

  1. Smell your pet’s ears right now. Do they smell like bread or corn chips? If yes, schedule a check.
  2. Buy a vet‑approved ear cleaner before monsoon starts. We can recommend one during your visit.
  3. Practice the cleaning technique (massage, wipe) on a healthy ear so you’re ready.
  4. After every rain walk this week, dry your pet’s ears with a towel.
