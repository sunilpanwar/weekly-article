Week 11 • March

💧 Water is Life – Scrub the Water Bowl Daily

The water bowl looks clean, but feel the inside – is it slimy? That biofilm harbours bacteria like Pseudomonas and E. coli. It causes diarrhoea, vomiting, and chronic gut issues. At Dr. Deepa pet vet clinic, we see pets with repeated digestive problems – and often the culprit is a dirty water bowl.

💧 Common sense rule: Scrub the water bowl daily with soap and hot water. Stainless steel or ceramic bowls are best – plastic develops scratches that trap germs.

The hidden danger of a slimy bowl

That slippery film you feel is a bacterial colony called biofilm. It forms within 24 hours, especially in warm weather. When your pet drinks, they ingest millions of bacteria. Over time, this can lead to:

  • Recurring diarrhoea or loose stools.
  • Vomiting after drinking.
  • Loss of appetite (nausea from bacterial toxins).
  • Increased risk of urinary tract infections (if bacteria enter the urethra).

Which bowls are safest?

  • Stainless steel – non‑porous, dishwasher safe, long lasting. Best choice.
  • Ceramic – good if glazed and chip‑free. Cracks can harbour bacteria.
  • Glass – easy to clean but breakable.
  • Plastic – scratches easily, traps bacteria, and can cause chin acne in cats. Avoid if possible.
🧼 Dr. Deepa’s tip: “Keep two bowls – use one while the other dries after washing. Change water at least twice a day, more often in summer.”

How to properly clean a water bowl

  • Daily: Empty, scrub with dish soap and a dedicated sponge (not the same one you use for human dishes), rinse thoroughly, and dry.
  • Weekly: Soak in a diluted vinegar solution (1 part vinegar, 3 parts water) for 10 minutes to remove mineral deposits, then wash as usual.
  • Monthly: Run ceramic or stainless bowls through the dishwasher (if dishwasher safe) for deep sanitisation.

Signs your pet’s water bowl is making them sick

  • They drink less than usual (bad taste from biofilm).
  • They prefer drinking from puddles, toilets, or other strange sources.
  • They vomit shortly after drinking.
  • They have chronic soft stools despite a good diet.

Real case from our clinic

A 3‑year‑old cat, Gingi, had recurrent vomiting for three months. Multiple tests were normal. The owner said, “I wash her bowl every few days.” We asked to see the bowl – it was plastic, scratched, and had a visible slimy ring. We switched to a stainless steel bowl, cleaned daily. The vomiting stopped within a week. Gingi’s owner was shocked: “I never thought the bowl could be the problem.”

Summer water tips

  • Add an ice cube or two to the bowl – pets drink more when water is cool.
  • Place multiple bowls around the house, especially in warm rooms.
  • For outdoor pets, keep bowls in the shade and refill at least three times a day.
  • Consider a pet water fountain – moving water stays fresher longer and encourages drinking.

What about filtered or bottled water?

Tap water is generally safe for pets in most Indian cities. If your tap water has a strong chlorine smell or you’re concerned, use a simple carbon filter. Bottled water is fine but expensive. Never give your pet distilled water long‑term – it lacks minerals and can cause electrolyte imbalances.

Action for Week 11

  1. Right now, go feel the inside of your pet’s water bowl. Is it slimy? If yes, scrub it immediately.
  2. If you’re using a plastic bowl, replace it with stainless steel or ceramic.
  3. Set a daily reminder to wash the bowl with soap and hot water.
  4. Observe your pet’s drinking habits – do they drink more from a clean bowl?
