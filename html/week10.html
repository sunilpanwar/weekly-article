Week 10 • March

🕷️ Tick Warning – Check Under Ears and Between Toes

As temperatures rise, ticks emerge. These tiny parasites carry deadly diseases like Ehrlichiosis and Babesiosis (tick fever). At Dr. Deepa pet vet clinic, we see tick fever cases spike every spring – many of which could have been prevented with daily checks and prompt removal.

🕷️ Common sense rule: After every walk, run your fingers through your pet’s fur. A tick feels like a small pea. Check under ears, between toes, armpits, and groin.

The tick life cycle – why spring is peak season

Ticks thrive in warm, humid weather. They hide in grass, bushes, and leaf litter. When a pet brushes past, ticks climb on and search for a thin‑skinned area to attach. They feed for days, transmitting diseases after 24–48 hours of attachment.

In India, the most common ticks are the Brown Dog Tick (Rhipicephalus) and the Lone Star Tick. Both are active from March to October, with two peaks: April‑May and September‑October.

Which pets are most at risk?

  • Dogs that go for walks in parks, gardens, or wooded areas.
  • Cats that go outdoors – even a balcony with plants can harbour ticks.
  • Pets living in homes with gardens or near vacant land.
  • Stray animals – they have no protection.

How to do a full tick check (2‑minute routine)

  • Start at the nose: check inside ears, ear flaps, and around eyes.
  • Run your hands over the neck, chest, and armpits (front legs).
  • Check between each toe and the paw pads.
  • Look at the groin, tail base, and under the tail (anal area).
  • Use a fine‑tooth comb for long‑haired dogs.
🔍 Dr. Deepa’s trick: “After a walk, put your pet on a white towel or sheet. Groom them vigorously. Any fallen ticks will be visible on the white background.”

Safe tick removal – step by step

  • Use fine‑point tweezers or a tick removal tool.
  • Grasp the tick as close to the skin as possible – don’t squeeze the body.
  • Pull straight up with steady, even pressure. Do not twist or jerk – that leaves mouthparts inside.
  • After removal, clean the bite with Betadine (povidone‑iodine).
  • Save the tick in a ziploc bag or a small container with a damp cotton ball – we can identify it if your pet becomes sick.

What NOT to do when removing a tick

  • Never burn a tick – this can make it regurgitate disease into your pet.
  • Don’t use petroleum jelly, alcohol, or nail polish – these irritate the tick and can cause it to release more saliva.
  • Avoid twisting or jerking – mouthparts left behind can cause infection.

Signs of tick fever (Ehrlichiosis / Babesiosis)

  • Sudden laziness or reluctance to move.
  • Not eating or drinking.
  • Fever (temperature above 102.5°F / 39.2°C).
  • Pale gums, nosebleeds, or small bruises on skin.
  • Weight loss and weakness.

If you see these signs, especially within 1–3 weeks of finding a tick, bring your pet to the clinic immediately. Tick fever is treatable if caught early, but can be fatal if ignored.

Real case from our clinic

A 5‑year‑old Indie, Rocky, was brought in with fever, loss of appetite, and lethargy. His owner hadn’t noticed any ticks. A blood test confirmed Ehrlichiosis. Rocky had a tick attached behind his ear – too small to see easily. He was hospitalised for three days on IV fluids and antibiotics. After two weeks of medication, Rocky recovered fully. His owner now does a daily tick check and uses a vet‑approved preventive. “I never realised a tiny tick could cause so much damage,” he said.

Prevention – what works

  • Daily tick checks – especially after walks in grassy areas.
  • Keep your garden tidy – mow the lawn, clear leaf litter, trim bushes.
  • Use a vet‑approved tick preventive – spot‑on treatments, oral tablets, or tick collars. Ask us which is right for your pet.
  • Consider the tick fever vaccine – not 100% effective but reduces severity. We can discuss during your next visit.

Action for Week 10

  1. Buy a tick removal tool or fine tweezers. Keep them in your first aid kit.
  2. After every walk, do a 2‑minute tick check. Focus on ears, toes, armpits, and groin.
  3. Consult us about a tick preventive – we’ll recommend based on your pet’s lifestyle.
  4. Mow your lawn and clear leaf litter to reduce tick habitat around your home.
