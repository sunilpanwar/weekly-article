📅 The Annual Checkup – Once a Year, See a Vet Even If They Are “Fine”

“My pet looks healthy. Why should I take them to the vet?” This is the most common – and most dangerous – misconception we hear at Dr. Deepa pet vet clinic. Pets are masters at hiding pain and illness. By the time you notice symptoms, a disease may have already progressed. An annual checkup (even for apparently healthy pets) catches problems early, when they are easier to treat. Prevention is always better than emergency care.

📅 Common sense rule: Once a year, bring your pet for a full physical exam – even if they seem perfectly fine. We check weight, teeth, heart, lungs, skin, eyes, ears, and joints. For seniors (7+ years), twice a year is better.

What happens during an annual checkup?

  • Weight and body condition score: We identify obesity or unexplained weight loss early.
  • Dental exam: Check for tartar, gum disease, loose or broken teeth.
  • Heart and lung auscultation: Listen for murmurs, arrhythmias, or abnormal lung sounds.
  • Abdominal palpation: Feel for organ enlargement, masses, or pain.
  • Skin and coat check: Look for lumps, parasites, allergies, or dryness.
  • Eye and ear exam: Catch cataracts, glaucoma, or ear infections early.
  • Joint and mobility assessment: Detect early arthritis before limping starts.
  • Vaccination updates: Rabies, DHPP, leptospirosis, etc., based on lifestyle.
  • Parasite control review: Deworming, flea/tick prevention tailored to season.
🩺 Dr. Deepa’s tip: “For senior pets (cats and dogs over 7 years), we recommend a senior wellness panel – blood work, urine analysis, and thyroid testing. Many age‑related diseases (kidney, liver, diabetes) have no early signs but show up in blood work.”

What annual checkups catch – real examples

  • Early kidney disease: No symptoms until 70% of kidney function is lost. Blood work catches it early – diet changes can add years of quality life.
  • Dental disease: Bad breath is not normal – under the gums, infection is eating away bone. Annual dental checks prevent tooth loss and heart/kidney damage.
  • Heart murmurs: Heard with a stethoscope long before a dog shows coughing or fainting. Early medication can slow progression.
  • Lumps and bumps: Most are benign, but some are mast cell tumours or sarcomas. Early removal is curative.
  • Arthritis: Subtle stiffness (slower on stairs, difficulty rising) is often dismissed as “just old age”. Early pain management preserves mobility.

Real case from our clinic

A 6‑year‑old Indie, Kalu, came for his annual checkup – his owner said he was “perfectly fine, just a little lazy”. On physical exam, we found a small lump on his chest. The owner hadn’t noticed it. We aspirated the lump – it was a mast cell tumour (a form of skin cancer). Because it was caught early, we removed it surgically with clean margins. Kalu recovered fully and has had no recurrence. His owner told us, “If I hadn’t brought him for that ‘pointless’ checkup, that lump would have grown and spread. The checkup saved his life.”

What about blood work – is it necessary?

For young, healthy pets (under 5 years), a physical exam may be enough. For pets over 5, we strongly recommend annual blood work (complete blood count and chemistry panel). It screens for:

  • Kidney and liver disease
  • Diabetes
  • Thyroid disorders (common in older cats and dogs)
  • Anaemia or infection
  • Electrolyte imbalances

Blood work also establishes a baseline – if your pet becomes sick later, we can compare results to see what has changed.

How often should seniors go?

For pets over 7 years (dogs) or 9 years (cats), twice‑yearly checkups are recommended. Health can change rapidly in old age. A six‑month gap can catch problems that would otherwise become emergencies.

What about indoor cats?

Indoor cats are often neglected for checkups because “they never go outside”. But indoor cats still develop dental disease, kidney disease, hyperthyroidism, and cancer. Annual exams are just as important – and because cats hide illness well, the vet may find things you’ve missed.

Action for Week 48

  1. Check your pet’s medical records – when was their last vet visit? If it’s been more than a year, schedule an appointment today.
  2. For senior pets (over 7 years), book a wellness exam plus blood work – we’ll create a senior care plan.
  3. Write down any changes you’ve noticed: appetite, thirst, energy, lumps, bad breath, stiffness. Bring this list to the appointment.
  4. Make the annual checkup a positive experience – bring treats, use a calm carrier/leash, and practice happy visits (Week 16) if your pet is fearful.
