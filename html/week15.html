🥣 Summer Cooling – Curd (Dahi) is Your Pet’s Best Friend

April heat is brutal – not just for us, but for our pets. While dogs pant and cats seek cool tiles, their internal body temperature can rise quickly. One of the safest, most natural ways to cool your pet from the inside out is plain, unsweetened curd (dahi). At Dr. Deepa pet vet clinic, we recommend this simple, affordable remedy every summer.

🥣 Common sense rule: A few spoons of plain curd with lunch helps lower body temperature, soothes the stomach, and provides probiotics. Avoid sweetened, flavoured, or artificially coloured curd.

Why curd works for summer cooling

Curd has a natural cooling effect on the body. It contains probiotics (good bacteria) that aid digestion – important because heat often causes gastrointestinal upset. The cool temperature and soft texture make it easy to eat, even for pets with reduced appetite in summer.

  • Lowers body temperature – the cool curd and its metabolic effect help reduce internal heat.
  • Soothes the stomach – probiotics calm indigestion, diarrhoea, and vomiting caused by heat stress.
  • Hydration boost – curd has high water content, helping prevent dehydration.
  • Palatable – most dogs and cats love the taste, making it easy to administer.

How to safely give curd to your pet

  • Use plain, unsweetened, unflavoured curd – no sugar, no fruit, no artificial sweeteners (xylitol is deadly).
  • Start small – 1 teaspoon for small pets (under 5 kg), 1 tablespoon for medium (5–15 kg), 2 tablespoons for large (15 kg+).
  • Mix with regular food – stir into kibble or rice for easy consumption.
  • Serve at room temperature – straight from the fridge is fine, but not ice‑cold (can cause stomach cramps).
  • Offer once daily – preferably with the main meal, not as a standalone treat (to avoid tummy upset on an empty stomach).
🧊 Dr. Deepa’s tip: “Freeze plain curd in an ice tray – curd popsicles! Offer one after a walk. It’s a refreshing, healthy treat that cools them down gradually.”

Which pets benefit most from curd?

  • Dogs of all breeds – especially active dogs who walk in warm weather.
  • Cats – many enjoy curd; start with a tiny lick.
  • Pets with heat‑related digestive issues – loose stools, vomiting, or reduced appetite.
  • Puppies and kittens – small amounts only, after weaning (over 8 weeks).

What to avoid – common mistakes

  • Never give sweetened or flavoured curd – sugar causes obesity and dental issues; artificial sweeteners (xylitol) kill.
  • Do not give curd that is too sour or old – it can cause diarrhoea. Fresh curd only.
  • Avoid if your pet is lactose intolerant – curd has less lactose than milk, but some pets still react. Watch for gas or loose stools.
  • Do not replace water with curd – fresh, clean water must always be available.

Other summer cooling foods (safe alternatives)

  • Watermelon (seedless, rind removed) – hydrating and cooling, but high in sugar – small amounts only.
  • Cucumber slices – low calorie, hydrating.
  • Buttermilk (chaas, no salt/spices) – another probiotic option, but introduce slowly.
  • Ice cubes in water bowl – encourages drinking and cools the water.

Real case from our clinic

A 6‑year‑old Labrador, Bruno, was brought in last April with lethargy, vomiting, and loose motion. He had been playing in the garden at noon. His temperature was slightly elevated. We treated him for mild heat stress, but also asked about his diet. The owner said Bruno had refused food for two days. We recommended plain curd mixed with a little rice. Bruno ate it eagerly. Within 24 hours, his vomiting stopped, and his energy returned. His owner now gives him a spoon of curd every afternoon during summer – “It’s our daily cooling ritual,” she says.

When curd is not enough – signs of heatstroke

Curd helps with mild heat stress, but heatstroke is a medical emergency. If you see any of these signs, skip the curd and go to the clinic immediately:

  • Excessive panting with a dark red or purple tongue.
  • Thick, sticky saliva.
  • Weakness, collapse, or inability to stand.
  • Vomiting or diarrhoea with blood.
  • Body temperature above 104°F (40°C).

For heatstroke, move your pet to a cool area, apply cool (not cold) water to the belly and paws, and bring them to us immediately. Do not give curd or any food until they are stable.

Action for Week 15

  1. Buy fresh, plain, unsweetened curd from your local store or make it at home.
  2. Start with a small spoonful mixed into your pet’s meal. Observe for any digestive upset.
  3. Freeze curd in an ice tray for hot afternoon treats.
  4. Remember: curd is a supplement, not a replacement for shade, water, and avoiding peak heat.
