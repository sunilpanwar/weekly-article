Week 47 • November

🎆 New Year Noise – Keep Pets Indoors and Give a Big Bone to Chew

New Year’s Eve brings another round of crackers, fireworks, and loud parties. For pets, it’s a night of fear and confusion. At Dr. Deepa pet vet clinic, we see the same preventable tragedies every year: pets who bolt out of doors, injure themselves trying to hide, or suffer from noise‑induced seizures. The solution is simple: keep your pet indoors in a safe room, and give them a long‑lasting chew to occupy their mind and reduce anxiety.

🎆 Common sense rule: On New Year’s Eve, confine your pet to an inner room with a fan or AC (white noise). Provide a large, safe chew – a stuffed Kong, a bully stick, or a raw marrow bone (supervised). Chewing releases endorphins, calming your pet naturally.

Why New Year’s Eve is as bad as Diwali for pets

  • Sudden, unpredictable explosions: Fireworks are loud, bright, and random – pets cannot predict or control them.
  • Late night parties: Noise continues past midnight, often with guests coming and going – doors left open are an escape risk.
  • Alcohol and food hazards: Guests may feed pets toxic foods (chocolate, xylitol sweets, grapes) or leave drinks within reach.
  • Cold weather: Escaped pets face hypothermia and traffic risks.

Safe room setup – use the same as Diwali

If you created a safe haven for Diwali (Week 40), use it again for New Year’s Eve:

  • Innermost room, no windows or curtains drawn tight.
  • Fan or AC running for white noise – plus calming music or TV.
  • Your pet’s bed, favourite toys, and a piece of your worn clothing.
  • Water bowl, but limit food (anxious pets may vomit).

If you don’t have an inner room, use a large closet, a bathroom with exhaust fan, or a covered crate in the quietest corner.

🦴 Dr. Deepa’s tip: “A long‑lasting chew is not just a distraction – it’s a calming tool. The act of chewing releases serotonin and dopamine, natural anti‑anxiety chemicals. A frozen Kong filled with peanut butter (xylitol‑free) can keep a dog busy for an hour.”

Safe chew options for New Year’s Eve

  • Frozen Kong: Fill with xylitol‑free peanut butter, mashed banana, plain yoghurt, or wet food. Freeze overnight – lasts 30–60 minutes.
  • Bully stick or pizzle: Natural, digestible, long‑lasting. Supervise to prevent choking when it becomes small.
  • Raw marrow bone (beef): Never cooked (cooked bones splinter). Give raw, from a butcher, and remove after 1 hour to avoid tooth fractures.
  • Lickimat: Spread with soft food or peanut butter – licking is also calming.
  • Cardboard box with hidden treats: Let them shred and search – mental stimulation tires them out.

What to avoid: Cooked bones (splinter), rawhide (choking hazard), dental chews with high calories (too many may cause diarrhoea).

On New Year’s Eve – step by step

  1. Walk your dog well before sunset. Ensure they pee/poop so they don’t need to go out during fireworks.
  2. Move your pet to the safe room by early evening. Before any crackers start.
  3. Give them the long‑lasting chew. This will occupy them during the loudest hours (11 PM – 1 AM).
  4. Play white noise or calming music. Classical music or pet‑specific tracks (available on streaming platforms).
  5. Stay with them if possible. Your presence is calming. If you must attend a party, check on them every 30 minutes.
  6. Do not let guests into the safe room. Strangers increase anxiety.
  7. Keep doors and windows locked. Many pets escape when guests arrive or leave.

Real case from our clinic

A 4‑year‑old Indie, Kalu, used to panic during New Year’s crackers – trembling, drooling, and trying to climb walls. His owner tried everything. We suggested a frozen Kong (peanut butter and banana) plus a covered crate in the inner bathroom with a fan. On New Year’s Eve, Kalu licked the Kong for 45 minutes, then slept through the rest. His owner was amazed: “He didn’t even seem to notice the crackers. The Kong saved the night.”

What about cats?

Cats also fear fireworks. Provide a covered crate, a high perch (top of wardrobe), or a cardboard box hideout. Use Feliway spray or diffuser. Give a puzzle feeder or a treat ball to keep them occupied. Never let cats outside on New Year’s Eve – many never return.

If your pet is severely noise‑phobic

For pets who panic despite safe rooms and chews, we can prescribe anti‑anxiety medication (trazodone, gabapentin, or alprazolam) for the night. Schedule a consultation at least a week before New Year’s Eve – we’ll calculate the correct dose based on your pet’s weight and medical history. Never use human sedatives or over‑the‑counter products without veterinary guidance.

What NOT to do

  • Do not leave your pet outside or on a balcony – they may jump in fear.
  • Do not tie them up – they can strangle themselves trying to escape.
  • Do not punish trembling or hiding – it’s fear, not misbehaviour.
  • Do not give alcohol or human sedatives – both can be fatal.

Action for Week 47

  1. Prepare your safe room today – even if you don’t plan to use it, have it ready.
  2. Buy or make a frozen Kong or other long‑lasting chew this week – test it before New Year’s Eve to ensure your pet likes it.
  3. If your pet has a history of severe noise phobia, schedule a medication consultation with us now – don’t wait until the last minute.
  4. Share a calm New Year’s Eve plan with family and guests – no opening of safe room doors, no feeding table scraps.
