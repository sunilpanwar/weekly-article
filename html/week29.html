🐱 Cat Comfort – Give Your Cat a High Spot to Feel Safe During Humidity

Monsoon humidity doesn’t just make cats uncomfortable – it can make them irritable, anxious, and even aggressive. Unlike dogs, cats are solitary hunters who rely on vertical space to feel secure. When humidity rises, their need for a safe, high, dry perch becomes even more critical. At Dr. Deepa pet vet clinic, we see a spike in stress‑related behaviours (hiding, hissing, inappropriate urination) during the rainy season – often resolved by simply providing a high spot.

🐱 Common sense rule: High humidity makes cats grumpy. Give them a high spot – top of a fridge, a cat tree, or an empty shelf – where they can observe without being disturbed. Vertical space = emotional security.

Why cats need height – especially in monsoon

  • Instinctual safety: In the wild, cats climb to escape predators and survey their territory. A high spot mimics this.
  • Humidity avoidance: Warm, moist air rises, but the highest spots often have better airflow and are less damp.
  • Stress reduction: Cats feel trapped at floor level, especially in busy households or during storms. Height gives them control.
  • Temperature regulation: Cooler air can sometimes be found near ceilings or on high perches away from floor‑level dampness.

Signs your cat needs a high spot

  • Hiding under furniture or in closets.
  • Hissing, swatting, or aggression when approached.
  • Inappropriate urination (outside the litter box).
  • Excessive grooming or pacing.
  • Refusing to come down from high places you don’t want them on (cupboards, shelves).

If you see these signs, your cat is stressed – likely from humidity, noise, or lack of safe retreat. A designated high spot can transform their behaviour.

📦 Dr. Deepa’s tip: “You don’t need an expensive cat tree. A sturdy cardboard box on top of a wardrobe, a shelf cleared of clutter, or even a window perch can work. The key is stability and a clear view of the room.”

How to create a cat‑friendly high spot – step by step

  1. Choose a stable surface: Top of a bookcase, refrigerator, wardrobe, or a dedicated cat tree. Ensure it can’t tip over.
  2. Add a non‑slip mat or a folded towel – gives grip and comfort.
  3. Place a familiar blanket or piece of your clothing – your scent calms them.
  4. Ensure access: Use a sturdy stool, a nearby chair, or wall shelves to create a “cat highway” if the spot is high.
  5. Location matters: Choose a quiet corner away from loud appliances, windows with heavy rain noise, or high‑traffic areas.
  6. Add a toy or a small bowl of water – some cats like to stay up for hours.

What if you have multiple cats?

Provide multiple high spots – one per cat, plus an extra. Cats are territorial and may fight over a single perch. Arrange them so each cat has a clear escape route and doesn’t feel trapped.

Affordable DIY high spot ideas

  • Window perch: A wooden plank covered with carpet, secured to a windowsill with brackets. Cats love watching rain and birds.
  • Wall shelves: Simple L‑shaped brackets with a piece of wood and carpet. Arrange in a staircase pattern.
  • Top of a wardrobe: Clear the area, add a non‑slip mat, and place a small box or bed.
  • Cardboard box tower: Stack sturdy boxes, cut holes, and secure with tape. Cats love enclosed spaces with a view.

Real case from our clinic

A 3‑year‑old Persian cat, Gulu, was brought in for inappropriate urination – she was peeing on her owner’s bed. All medical tests were normal. The owner lived in a small apartment and had no cat tree. During monsoon, Gulu hid under the bed and seemed agitated. We suggested installing a simple shelf near the ceiling, covered with a towel. Within days, Gulu started spending hours on the shelf, watching the rain. The inappropriate urination stopped completely. Her owner said, “I didn’t realise she just needed a place of her own.”

Additional monsoon comfort tips for cats

  • Use a dehumidifier in the room where your cat spends most time.
  • Place a fan (low setting) near their high spot – moving air reduces humidity stress.
  • Keep litter boxes clean and dry – humidity makes litter clump and smell faster.
  • Play calming music or white noise to drown out thunder and heavy rain.
  • Consider a pheromone diffuser (Feliway) – reduces stress‑related behaviours.

When to seek veterinary help

If your cat continues to hide, act aggressively, or urinate outside the litter box even after providing high spots, there may be an underlying medical issue (urinary tract infection, arthritis, or hyperthyroidism). Bring them for a checkup.

Action for Week 29

  1. Look around your home – identify one potential high spot you can clear for your cat (top of a shelf, wardrobe, or fridge).
  2. Clear the area and add a non‑slip mat or towel today.
  3. Observe your cat’s behaviour over the next week – do they use the spot? If not, try moving it or adding a familiar scent.
  4. If your cat shows signs of stress despite a high spot, schedule a visit to rule out medical issues.
