🧹 The Spring Shed – How to Manage the Hair Apocalypse

Hair everywhere – on your sofa, clothes, even in your food. Spring shedding is normal, but you can manage it without losing your mind. At Dr. Deepa pet vet clinic, we hear the same complaint every March: “My pet is leaving fur tumbleweeds all over the house!”

🧹 Common sense rule: Brush your pet outdoors for 5 minutes every morning. The key is frequency, not duration.

Why shedding happens in spring

As days get longer and temperatures rise, your pet’s body sheds the thick winter undercoat to prepare for summer. Double‑coated breeds (Labs, GSDs, Indies, Huskies) shed heavily twice a year. Indoor pets may shed lightly all year due to artificial lighting and heating.

Which breeds shed the most?

  • Heavy shedders: Labrador Retrievers, German Shepherds, Huskies, Akitas, Indies.
  • Moderate shedders: Beagles, Boxers, Cats (Domestic Shorthairs).
  • Light shedders: Poodles, Bichon Frises, Sphynx cats.

Tools that actually work

  • Slicker brush: For removing loose topcoat and mats. Use daily.
  • Undercoat rake: Essential for double coats – reaches the fluffy underlayer. Use 2–3 times per week.
  • Rubber curry brush: Great for short‑haired breeds and for massage. Use daily.
  • Deshedding tool: Use once a week only – overuse can damage guard hairs.
🛁 Dr. Deepa’s tip: “A warm bath with a deshedding shampoo loosens dead hair. Blow‑dry on cool setting while brushing outdoors – you’ll see clouds of hair fly off.”

Diet and shedding – what helps?

Healthy skin = less shedding. Add omega‑3 fatty acids (fish oil) to your pet’s food – 1 teaspoon per 10 kg body weight daily. Also ensure adequate protein; low‑quality kibble leads to dry, brittle fur that breaks off. Ask us for a diet recommendation during your next visit.

Real case from our clinic

A 4‑year‑old Labrador, Simba, came in with patchy fur and excessive shedding. His owner was brushing once a month. We recommended a daily 5‑minute outdoor brush session using an undercoat rake, plus fish oil supplements. Within two weeks, the hair tumbleweeds reduced by 70%. Simba’s coat became glossy, and his owner said, “I can finally wear black clothes again!”

What NOT to do

  • Never shave a double‑coated dog – it ruins their natural insulation and can cause permanent coat damage.
  • Avoid human shampoos – they dry out skin, increasing shedding.
  • Don’t brush too hard – gentle strokes, not aggressive scraping.

Action for Week 8

  1. Buy a proper deshedding tool (slicker brush or undercoat rake – we can advise which is right for your breed).
  2. Brush outdoors every morning for 5 minutes – set a timer.
  3. Add fish oil to meals (start with a small dose to avoid diarrhoea, then increase gradually).
  4. Vacuum upholstery weekly and use a lint roller on clothes.
