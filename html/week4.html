🐾 The Zoomies – Why Pets Run Crazy at Night

Does your cat suddenly sprint around the house at 10 PM, bouncing off furniture and meowing wildly? Or your dog tearing laps around the garden for no reason? That’s the “zoomies” – technically FRAPs (Frenetic Random Activity Periods). It’s pent‑up energy, not madness or a ghost.

🏃 Common sense rule: A tired pet sleeps through the night. 5–10 minutes of active play before bedtime can stop the midnight chaos.

Why zoomies happen

Indoor pets, especially apartment cats and dogs without enough exercise, have no outlet for their natural hunting or herding instincts. The zoomies are a release of accumulated energy – like a child running wild after being in class all day. They often happen after pooping (a vagus nerve stimulation), after a bath, or when you’re about to sleep.

Which pets get zoomies?

  • Cats of all ages – especially indoor‑only cats.
  • Young dogs – puppies and adolescents (under 2 years).
  • High‑energy breeds – Huskies, Border Collies, Indies, Bengals.
  • Any pet that has been confined or inactive for hours.

How to prevent destructive zoomies

  • Schedule two active play sessions daily – morning and evening. Each session 10 minutes minimum.
  • Use interactive toys: Feather wands for cats, tug ropes or fetch for dogs. Laser pointers work but end with a physical toy so they “catch” something.
  • Create vertical space for cats: Shelves, cat trees, window perches – climbing burns energy.
  • For dogs: A quick game of fetch or tug‑of‑war indoors, or a 15‑minute training session (sit, down, spin) mentally tires them.
🐱 Dr. Deepa’s observation: “Cats that get zoomies at 3 AM are often bored, not evil. A 10‑minute play session before your bedtime shifts their active period to match yours.”

Real case from our clinic

Mrs. Sharma’s cat, Gulu, used to run across her face at 2 AM every night. She tried locking him out – he scratched the door for hours. We suggested a structured play routine: 5 minutes with a feather wand at 10 PM, then a small treat. Within a week, Gulu slept through the night. Mrs. Sharma now calls it “the magic wand”. No medication, no expensive gadgets – just consistent play.

When to worry about zoomies

Occasional zoomies are completely normal. But if your pet:

  • Seems frantic, disoriented, or runs into walls,
  • Zooms after every single meal (could be pain or digestive issue),
  • Is a senior dog with sudden nighttime pacing (possible cognitive dysfunction),
  • Also shows signs of fleas or skin irritation,

then bring them to Dr. Deepa pet vet clinic for a checkup.

Action for Week 4

  1. Set a daily alarm for “play time” – 10 minutes before your own bedtime.
  2. Use an interactive toy – make them jump, run, and pounce. No lazy waving.
  3. After play, offer a small treat. This mimics the “hunt‑catch‑eat” sequence and relaxes them.
  4. If zoomies continue, increase playtime to 15 minutes or add a second session in the morning.
