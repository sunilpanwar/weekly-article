🍂 Changing Seasons – Adjust Your Pet’s Food as It Gets Cooler

As October arrives and temperatures drop, your pet’s metabolism changes. Many dogs and cats naturally feel hungrier – they are instinctively preparing for winter by building a little fat layer and growing a thicker coat. At Dr. Deepa pet vet clinic, we often see owners either overfeed (leading to obesity) or underfeed (causing weight loss). The key is to adjust portions slightly – but only for active pets, and only until spring.

🍂 Common sense rule: If your pet is active outdoors, you can increase food by 10–15% in cool weather. If they are mostly indoors (heated home), keep portions the same. Monitor body condition weekly.

Why pets get hungrier in cooler months

  • Increased energy expenditure: Staying warm burns calories, even indoors. Outdoor pets need even more.
  • Winter coat growth: Growing a thicker fur coat requires extra protein and energy.
  • Natural instinct: Ancestral dogs ate more in autumn to prepare for winter scarcity.
  • Less daylight: Melatonin and other hormones can influence appetite.

How much to increase – and for which pets?

  • Active outdoor dogs: Those who walk, run, or play outside for more than an hour daily – increase by 10–15%.
  • Working dogs or farm dogs: May need 20–30% more – consult us.
  • Indoor couch potatoes: No increase needed – they burn very few extra calories.
  • Senior pets: Often less active – increase protein quality, not quantity (ask us).
  • Cats: Most indoor cats do not need a winter increase. Outdoor cats may need a small bump.
⚖️ Dr. Deepa’s tip: “Don’t just pour more kibble. Use a measuring cup and increase by 10% – e.g., from 100g to 110g per meal. Weigh your pet weekly. If they gain weight, cut back.”

How to monitor body condition – the touch test

You don’t need a scale to know if your pet is at a healthy weight:

  • Ideal: You can feel ribs easily without pressing, see a waist behind the ribs when viewed from above, and see a tuck-up (belly higher than chest) from the side.
  • Underweight: Ribs, spine, and hip bones are visible from a distance. Increase food.
  • Overweight: Can’t feel ribs without pressing, no waist, belly sagging. Decrease food.

Adjust portions gradually – a 10% change can take 2–4 weeks to show results.

Real case from our clinic

A 4‑year‑old Indie, Kalu, came in for his annual checkup in November. He had gained 3 kg since summer – a 15% increase in body weight. His owner said, “He seemed hungrier, so I filled the bowl more.” Kalu was now overweight, with early signs of joint stress. We advised reducing his food back to summer portions and adding a low‑calorie vegetable (like beans or carrot) to help him feel full. Within three months, Kalu lost the extra weight. His owner now adjusts portions seasonally – but measures every meal. “I thought I was being kind,” she said. “Now I know that extra weight hurts more than hunger.”

What about treats and chews?

Treats count toward daily calories. If you increase meals, do not increase treats – or reduce treats to compensate. A single large chew (like a rawhide) can add 100+ calories. Consider switching to low‑calorie treats (green beans, cucumber slices, or air‑dried liver in tiny pieces).

Special considerations for senior pets

Older dogs often gain weight in winter because they move less but eat the same. Instead of increasing food, focus on:

  • High‑quality protein to maintain muscle mass.
  • Omega‑3 fatty acids for joint health (fish oil).
  • Warm, soft bedding to reduce stiffness (not extra food).

What about cats?

Indoor cats are less affected by temperature changes. Do not increase food unless your cat is losing weight (weigh them monthly). Outdoor cats may need a small increase – but monitor for obesity. Obesity is more dangerous for cats than mild underweight (leads to diabetes, arthritis, urinary issues).

When to consult us

  • Your pet is losing weight despite eating more – could be medical (worms, diabetes, thyroid).
  • Your pet is gaining weight rapidly – could be fluid retention or metabolic disease.
  • You’re unsure how much to feed – bring your pet and their food bag for a personalised plan.

Action for Week 36

  1. Weigh your pet today (weigh yourself, then with pet, subtract). Write it down.
  2. Feel their ribs – can you feel them easily? If not, reduce food.
  3. If they are active outdoors, increase food by 10% – measure it, don’t guess.
  4. Re‑weigh in 4 weeks – adjust again if needed.
